05 / Licensing and cost

An architecture has both a service model and a cost model

“We already have BTP” is not enough to confirm access to a product. Ask which contract, which global account, which service and plan, which region and how usage is metered. A technical subscription, an entitlement and a purchased subscription are three different facts.

Free tier is not “automatic free capacity, then automatic paid overflow”

The supplied lesson describes free capacity in broad commercial terms. SAP’s service-plan documentation makes an important distinction: free plans have technical limits and a move to a paid plan may be required. Not every service supports an in-place transition. Verify the selected plan instead of promising seamless conversion or automatic paid overrun.

Always Free in Discovery Center means a plan is included without additional charges in the relevant overall contract; it is not a statement that the whole application, its runtime or connected services are free. Free plans are not generally subject to productive SLAs in the cited guidance.

Turn the diagram into a service bill of materials

For each component, record the exact service/plan, environment, region, production and non-production quantity, meter, unit, duty cycle, minimum charge and dependency. This is a service cost inventory. A formal software bill of materials (SBOM) records software components and dependencies; it is a different artifact.

Worked sizing example — synthetic, not an SAP quote: two application instances, each with 0.5 GB of memory, running for 730 hours produce 730 GB-hours. One 0.5 GB test instance used for 220 hours adds 110 GB-hours. Total: 840 GB-hours, before the selected plan’s billing rules. Multiply by the applicable rate only after checking its unit and conditions.

For a token-metered AI path, a first model is: requests × calls per request × (input tokens × input rate + output tokens × output rate). Add other chargeable services and convert units correctly. This is a planning formula, not a universal SAP pricing formula.

Use the Discovery Center estimator for the specified region and commercial model, then reconcile it with the agreement. Estimate low, expected and high demand. Include recurring operations and change effort in total cost of ownership. A lower runtime bill can still produce a more expensive solution if it needs difficult support.

Control cost where work can actually multiply

Set ownership, budgets and usage alerts. Bound an agent’s tool calls, elapsed time and output size; stop loops and repeated failures. A resource quota may limit capacity without preventing every form of spending. Usage analytics, available credits and the billed amount are related but not identical.

Client explanation: “We can estimate the design once we know the services and demand. I will show the assumptions, non-production costs and the expensive failure paths. The estimator is a planning aid; your contract defines the bill.”

Reference: commercial, entitlements, free, trial, ai-pricing.

Why can a small agent become expensive even when the model’s price per token is low?

It can retrieve too much context, call several models, repeat failed tools or run continuously. Cost is driven by the complete execution path and supporting services, not only by the advertised price of one model.
