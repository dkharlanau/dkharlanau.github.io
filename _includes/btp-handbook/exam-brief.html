What must be understood, and what can wait?

This priority map is an editorial study aid based on the supplied course and the handbook review. It is not an official topic weighting, leaked question list or passing-score predictor. You do not need to configure every product to explain why it belongs—or does not belong—in a solution.

PriorityCapability to demonstrateRead and test yourself
Essential: explainBusiness outcome, system of record, fit-to-standard, clean core and the three platform pillars.Platform and architecture. Explain why external review is separate from internal supplier approval.
Essential: decideOn-stack versus side-by-side; CAP versus RAP; runtime versus IDE; account and service boundaries.Accounts and extensions. Give one reason to reject your preferred option.
Essential: traceOne real request through identity, application, integration and ERP; separate routing from authorization and completion.Integration, security and request paths. Explain the owner of every arrow.
Essential: costExact service/plan/region, technical entitlement, commercial metric, non-production usage and expensive failure paths.Commercial model and license gates. Explain what still costs money when no user opens the app.

A two-hour revision route—not a replacement for the full guide

20 minutes: platform, architecture and the account model. 20: extensions and mandatory/optional components. 25: integration, identity and one failed request. 25: data, AI and governance. 20: licensing, operating requirements and methodology outputs. 10: explain the case without notes and answer one changed constraint. These are suggested revision times, not SAP exam timings or a guarantee of sufficient preparation.

Use the existing case workbook for depth. Before starting the exam clock, be able to answer three questions without a product list: Why this design? What can fail? What evidence would make you accept it?
