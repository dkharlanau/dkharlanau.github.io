Use an evidence completeness check when the workflow matures

You do not need to collect every element in every first workflow. Use this as a completeness check when the workflow becomes sensitive, repeated, or ready to expand.

The goal is to make the workflow reconstructable. A reviewer should be able to understand what ran, what was in scope, what evidence supported the conclusion, what remained unproven, what decision was made, and where that decision was recorded.

Evidence element What it should capture
Workflow or run identifier The run, scan, review, or workflow instance being discussed.
Scope boundary What was reviewed and what was out of scope.
Approved artifact Repository, branch, commit, path, ticket, scanner finding, or code slice.
Environment Local checkout, sandbox, lower-level environment, CI-style worker, or another approved runtime.
Source fact What the code, finding, log, diff, test output, or artifact directly shows.
Validation method How the claim was checked, such as source review, approved test, scanner comparison, or human review.
Supported inference A reasonable conclusion drawn from the available evidence.
Proof gap What has not been tested, reviewed, reproduced, or approved.
Proposed action Investigate, suppress, defer, remediate, re-test, escalate, or record.
Reviewer disposition The human reviewer’s decision or required next review.
System-of-record entry Where the decision, owner, status, and next action are recorded.
Retention and cleanup status What evidence is retained and what temporary artifacts must be removed.

This checklist improves traceability, but it does not raise the evidence ceiling. Recording a proof gap more carefully does not turn it into runtime proof. The permitted activity still determines the strongest claim the team can responsibly make.

identify the run
-> confirm scope and approved artifact
-> capture source evidence and validation method
-> separate inference from proof gap
-> record proposed action and human disposition
-> write the decision to the system of record
-> retain or clean up evidence according to policy
