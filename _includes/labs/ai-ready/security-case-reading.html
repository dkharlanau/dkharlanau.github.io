Separate facts, assumptions, and open questions

Before recommending a Daybreak or Codex Security next step, separate what is known from what is assumed and what still needs clarification.

Case element What it means How to use it
Known facts Information the customer has explicitly said, provided, approved, or shown in source material. Use these as the starting evidence for scope, workflow, constraints, and decisions.
Reasonable assumptions Things that may be true based on the available context but have not yet been confirmed. Keep them visible as assumptions. Do not quietly promote them into facts or use them to justify a higher evidence claim.
Open questions Missing information that could change the scope, evidence ceiling, access model, risk, or recommended next step. Clarify these before making a recommendation that depends on the answer. 
known facts
-> reasonable assumptions
-> open questions
-> confirm what changes the decision
-> recommend the next bounded step

This distinction is especially useful when reading an assessment case. A case may strongly suggest that a repository is customer-owned, a staging environment exists, or a reviewer is available. Until the case confirms those points, treat them as assumptions or open questions rather than facts.

Reasonable assumptions are not the same as supported inferences. An assumption helps structure discovery before enough evidence is available. A supported inference is a conclusion drawn from source facts. Once evidence confirms or disproves an assumption, update the case model instead of carrying the old assumption forward.

A practical rule is simple: if an unanswered question could change Proceed, Pause, Escalate, or Expand, it belongs in the readout or must be resolved before the recommendation.
