      <h1>Technical interest is not yet a qualified opportunity.</h1>
      <p>MEDDPICC is a practical way to test whether a complex B2B opportunity has enough business evidence, decision access, internal support, and execution clarity to move forward. It is useful for SAP transformation, enterprise AI, integration, automation, and other deals where many people influence the outcome.</p>
      <a class="research-canvas__button" href="#meddpicc-map">Open the framework <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">arrow_downward</span></a>
    </div>
    <div class="research-canvas__signal" aria-label="MEDDPICC summary">
      <p>Qualification lens</p>
      <div class="research-canvas__signal-line"><span>M</span><strong>Value</strong><small>Can we measure it?</small></div>
      <div class="research-canvas__signal-line"><span>E</span><strong>Authority</strong><small>Who can fund it?</small></div>
      <div class="research-canvas__signal-line"><span>D</span><strong>Decision</strong><small>How will they choose?</small></div>
      <em>The framework is a gap map, not a reason to force a deal forward.</em>
    </div>
  </header>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">filter_alt</span>
    <p><strong>Working rule:</strong> MEDDPICC separates technical interest from a qualified business opportunity.</p>
    <p>A customer may like the architecture, ask for a demo, or request a proof of concept. That is useful interest. A stronger opportunity also has a meaningful problem, measurable value, a decision path, a budget owner, internal support, and a realistic commercial process.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="meddpicc-map" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">The eight lenses</p>
      <h2>Use MEDDPICC to find what is known, what is assumed, and what is still missing.</h2>
      <p>The method works best as structured discovery. Do not turn it into a checklist interview. Follow the customer’s workflow and decision process, then use MEDDPICC to see where evidence is strong and where the opportunity still depends on assumptions.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Element</th><th scope="col">What it means</th><th scope="col">Lead question</th><th scope="col">Useful evidence</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Metrics</th><td>The measurable business result the customer wants to improve.</td><td>What should change in time, cost, quality, risk, capacity, revenue, or service?</td><td>Baseline, target, volume, cycle time, error rate, backlog, cost, SLA, or another observable indicator.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Economic Buyer</th><td>The person with final economic authority for the investment.</td><td>Who can approve the spend or stop it even if everyone else supports the idea?</td><td>Budget ownership, investment authority, direct access, or a confirmed path to that person.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Criteria</th><td>The criteria the customer will use to compare acceptable solutions.</td><td>What must be true for this solution to be selected?</td><td>Business fit, SAP integration, security, data residency, TCO, implementation effort, accuracy, support model, or architecture constraints.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Process</th><td>The real sequence of people, reviews, and approvals that leads to a decision.</td><td>Who decides what, in which order, and by when?</td><td>Workshop, PoC, architecture review, steering committee, budget approval, executive sign-off, or another named step.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Paper Process</th><td>The commercial and control steps required after the business decision.</td><td>What must happen before a contract, PO, or production start is possible?</td><td>Procurement, security review, legal review, DPA, vendor onboarding, contract, purchase order, or compliance approval.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Identify Pain</th><td>The business problem important enough to justify change.</td><td>What is happening today, who feels it, and what happens if nothing changes?</td><td>Manual effort, delay, missed revenue, control weakness, operational risk, poor service, rework, or capacity pressure.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Champion</th><td>An internal person who benefits from the change and actively helps the opportunity move.</td><td>Who will help us understand the organisation, reach the right people, and defend the change when we are not in the room?</td><td>Access to stakeholders, internal context, feedback, preparation for decision meetings, and active support.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Competition</th><td>Every realistic alternative to the proposed path.</td><td>What could the customer choose instead?</td><td>Another vendor, SAP standard capability, custom development, existing automation, internal team, delayed investment, or doing nothing.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>

    <div class="ecg-decision-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>Economic Buyer is not the same as process owner</h3>
        <p>The process owner can explain the operational problem and may own the target outcome. The Economic Buyer controls the investment decision. Sometimes they are the same person; often they are not.</p>
        <p>A Lead should know both: who owns the result and who can release the money.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Champion is more than a friendly contact</h3>
        <p>A contact can like the idea and still be unable to move it. A Champion has something to gain from success, has credibility inside the customer organisation, and uses that position to help the work progress.</p>
        <p>The strongest signal is action: access, context, preparation, feedback, and internal advocacy.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">SAP + Business AI example</p>
      <h2>From “we want AI for sales orders” to a qualification picture.</h2>
      <p>The numbers below are illustrative. The point is not the exact target. The point is to show how a broad AI request becomes a business case with owners, criteria, and a decision path.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">MEDDPICC element</th><th scope="col">Example evidence</th><th scope="col">What is still worth testing</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Identify Pain</th><td>Customer orders arrive by email and PDF. Sales operations manually reads them, checks master data, and enters order data into SAP.</td><td>Which step creates the most delay, errors, or rework?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Metrics</th><td>The team wants lower manual touch time, faster order entry, and fewer corrections.</td><td>Current monthly volume, baseline effort, error rate, target improvement, and the cost of exceptions.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Economic Buyer</th><td>For example, a VP Sales Operations or Supply Chain leader with budget authority.</td><td>Is this person the real investment owner, and what result would justify funding?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Criteria</th><td>SAP S/4HANA integration, controlled write access, auditability, acceptable extraction quality, EU data requirements, supportability, and total cost.</td><td>Which criteria are mandatory and which are trade-offs?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Process</th><td>Discovery workshop → bounded prototype → architecture and security review → business validation → steering decision.</td><td>Who owns each gate, and what evidence is required to pass it?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Paper Process</th><td>Procurement, security, data protection, legal, vendor onboarding, contract, and PO.</td><td>Which step has the longest lead time or can block the target start date?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Champion</th><td>Head of Order Management actively brings process experts, shares approved examples, and helps define the success criteria.</td><td>Can this person influence the decision process and reach the Economic Buyer?</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Competition</th><td>SAP-native capability, another AI platform, custom development, RPA/OCR, process redesign, or continuing the manual process.</td><td>Why should the customer change now, and why is the proposed path better than the strongest alternative?</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>

    <div class="research-canvas__boundary">
      <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">psychology_alt</span>
      <p><strong>Lead lens:</strong> a good architecture answer is not enough. You should also be able to explain the value, owner, decision criteria, approval path, control boundary, and alternative choices.</p>
    </div>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="qualification-template" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Reusable template</p>
      <h2>One page is enough to expose most qualification gaps.</h2>
      <p>Use this after discovery, before a PoC, before an architecture commitment, or before claiming that an opportunity is ready to progress. Write evidence, not optimistic summaries.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Area</th><th scope="col">Evidence to capture</th><th scope="col">Status</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Problem / Pain</th><td>What is wrong today? Where in the workflow? Who is affected? What is the consequence?</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Metrics</th><td>Baseline, target, volume, time, cost, quality, risk, capacity, or service measure.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Economic Buyer</th><td>Name or role, budget authority, business priority, access path, and what would justify investment.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Criteria</th><td>Business, technical, security, data, architecture, delivery, operating, and commercial criteria.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Decision Process</th><td>Decision stages, participants, evidence required at each stage, target dates, and final approval.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Paper Process</th><td>Procurement, security, legal, data protection, contract, vendor onboarding, PO, and expected lead times.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Champion</th><td>Who actively supports the change? What have they done? Can they reach the decision path?</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Competition</th><td>Other vendors, SAP standard, custom build, existing process, delay, or no change.</td><td>Known / assumption / gap</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Next proof</th><td>The next action that reduces the biggest uncertainty, with owner and expected evidence.</td><td>Committed / proposed / missing</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>

    <div class="ecg-decision-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>Simple evidence score</h3>
        <p>This is a working aid for this site, not part of the MEDDPICC standard.</p>
        <ul>
          <li><strong>0 — Unknown:</strong> we do not have an answer.</li>
          <li><strong>1 — Assumption:</strong> we have a plausible view but no strong customer evidence.</li>
          <li><strong>2 — Evidence:</strong> the customer has confirmed it through facts, access, documents, named owners, or committed actions.</li>
        </ul>
        <p>The total score matters less than the pattern. One major gap in the Economic Buyer, decision process, pain, or Champion may matter more than several easy points elsewhere.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Do not fake completeness</h3>
        <p>MEDDPICC is useful because it makes uncertainty visible. If the Economic Buyer is unknown, write <em>unknown</em>. If a metric is only a hypothesis, label it as a hypothesis. If the Champion has not acted, do not promote a friendly contact into that role.</p>
        <p>The next discovery step should target the highest-value gap.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="assessment-lens" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">SAP Lead assessment lens</p>
      <h2>Show that you can move from solution design to business decision.</h2>
      <p>In a Lead assessment, MEDDPICC is useful even when nobody asks for the acronym. It gives you a disciplined way to challenge a weak requirement and explain what you need before committing to a solution.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="ecg-decision-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>If the interviewer says</h3>
        <p><em>“The customer wants an AI agent to automate order processing. What would you propose?”</em></p>
        <p>Do not start with a model, vendor, or integration diagram. First establish the problem, process boundary, measurable outcome, ownership, decision criteria, and control requirements.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>A Lead-level answer</h3>
        <p>“I would first qualify the business problem and decision path. I want to know where manual work happens, how we measure the pain, who owns the outcome and budget, what criteria the solution must meet, and what approvals are needed. Then I can define a bounded prototype and architecture that tests the biggest uncertainty.”</p>
      </div>
