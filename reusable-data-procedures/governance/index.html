Governance

Govern the reusable asset, not every spreadsheet click.

The framework needs enough control to make approved procedures trustworthy without turning a monthly reconciliation into a six-person committee. Governance should scale with risk and reuse.

OverviewCatalogBlocksDeployment & RunTestingGovernance

Lifecycle

Separate maturity from trust.

A procedure can be mature but stale, or new but well tested. Lifecycle and trust should not be collapsed into one label.

01Idea

Problem and candidate approach are recorded. No reusable execution claim yet.

02Draft

Procedure contract exists and can run against fixtures or controlled examples.

03Pilot

Used on real runs with an explicit owner and close review of exceptions and drift.

04Approved

Accepted for its declared scope. Required tests, owner and policy checks are present.

05Deprecated

Still traceable for history, but new use should move to a named replacement.

Trust evidence

Prefer evidence badges over one magic score.

Regression tested

Known fixtures cover important normal, boundary and failure cases.

Owner reviewed

The accountable business owner has reviewed purpose, rules and output meaning.

Production proven

Successful real runs exist for the declared source family and scope.

Fresh review

Review date is still valid for the procedure's criticality.

Policy cleared

Data classification, external calls, AI mode and custom code fit the approved policy.

Drift observed

The procedure has seen source changes and its repair behavior has been exercised.

Roles

Use clear responsibility without inventing a department.

RoleResponsibility
AuthorBuilds or changes procedure logic and fixtures.
Business ownerOwns purpose, business meaning, acceptance criteria and continued use.
Technical stewardOwns runtime compatibility, blocks, dependencies and operational behavior.
Data stewardReviews important mappings, reference data, identifiers and data-quality rules when needed.
ReviewerChecks a proposed version according to risk. One person may hold several roles in small teams.

Policy profiles

Reuse policy bundles instead of repeating controls in every procedure.

A policy profile belongs to deployment and governance. It describes how a procedure may run, not what its business transformation means.

ProfileTypical useExample controls
local-safeConsultant utility on local filesNo network, no raw AI samples, local evidence, manual trigger.
team-standardShared operational workNamed owner, regression cases, controlled references, team storage, review on breaking drift.
production-criticalBusiness-critical scheduled outputApproved versions, pinned blocks, atomic publication, idempotency, stronger retention, alerts and rollback evidence.
restricted-dataSensitive corporate dataApproved runtime only, restricted storage, no external endpoint by default, tighter retention and access control.
Rule: a procedure should not need a new version just because it moves from a local manual deployment to a scheduled production deployment. The policy and deployment contracts absorb that change.

Promotion

Separate lifecycle from execution environment.

Sandbox

Exploration, new locators, temporary samples and AI-assisted discovery are allowed according to data policy.

Team

Shared procedure versions, fixtures and reviews. Changes are visible to other users.

Production

Only approved versions and pinned dependencies execute. Runtime policy is stricter and evidence retention is enforced.

Important: promoting a procedure does not copy its raw input data. It promotes the definition, approved dependencies and policy.

Change control

Review based on impact, not file size.

ChangeTypical treatment
Description or tagsPatch change. No execution review.
New optional alias or non-blocking validationMinor change with regression tests.
Required field, key, mapping cardinality or business transformMajor change. Impact analysis and owner review.
Shared block major versionQuery dependency graph before promotion.
Deployment schedule or destinationDeployment review. Procedure version normally stays unchanged.
New custom code, external endpoint or AI data modePolicy and security review according to classification.

Expiry & deprecation

Reusable does not mean immortal.

Review by date

Operational and business-critical procedures have an explicit next review date.

Stale warning

A procedure with no recent review or successful runs remains discoverable but loses freshness evidence.

Replacement chain

Deprecated procedures point to a successor and explain the migration reason.

Rollback

Approved previous versions stay addressable so a bad revision can be rolled back without reconstructing yesterday from memory.
