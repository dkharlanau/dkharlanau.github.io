# Research and Attribution Policy

## Context

This site discusses SAP products using public sources, professional experience, and independent analysis. Sources support verification; the public explanation remains independently written.

This website is an independent personal knowledge project. It is not official SAP documentation, SAP training material, or an SAP publication. It is not sponsored, approved, certified, or endorsed by SAP SE or its affiliates.

## How I build the content

I use public primary sources to check facts such as product names, capabilities, release boundaries, architecture options, and licensing signals. I then explain the topic in my own words and combine it with professional experience, architecture reasoning, and clearly marked synthetic examples.

The goal is understanding, not reproduction.

## What I publish

- Original explanations, summaries, taxonomies, diagrams, comparisons, and decision models written for this site.
- Independent professional interpretation and lessons learned from SAP delivery and support work, without exposing client or employer confidential information.
- Synthetic examples created to explain processes, integrations, failures, and architecture decisions safely.
- Links to primary sources so readers can verify time-sensitive facts at the source.

## What I avoid

- Copying paragraphs, pages, tables, diagrams, or training material from SAP documentation.
- Mirroring or republishing SAP Help Portal or other SAP website content.
- Using SAP logos or SAP-authored graphics as decoration.
- Publishing SAP screenshots by default. A screenshot would only be used deliberately where the specific use is permitted and necessary.
- Publishing restricted, paywalled, licensed, partner-only, customer-only, or confidential material.
- Publishing client, employer, project, or system information that is confidential or identifiable.
- Bulk scraping, automated extraction, or building a local copy of SAP web content.

Direct quotations are avoided by default. When a source matters, I prefer a short independent summary and a link to the original source.

## Facts, experience, and interpretation

Where practical, the knowledge model separates different kinds of statements:

- **Documented fact**: checked against a public primary source.
- **Professional experience**: a practical observation from delivery, support, or architecture work.
- **Reasoned interpretation**: my conclusion based on facts, constraints, and experience.
- **Synthetic example**: a fictional scenario created for learning or evaluation.

This separation is intentional. A vendor statement and an architect's judgment are not the same thing.

## Voice and authenticity

I write in the first person when I am describing a professional method, a diagnostic habit, an architecture preference, or a lesson that can be explained without exposing confidential work. The purpose is to teach the reasoning, not to make the page sound more personal for its own sake.

I do not invent client stories, project outcomes, savings figures, incident details, or experience that is not mine. When an example is fictional, it is marked as synthetic or illustrative. When I say that I would check something first, that is a working heuristic and not a claim that every SAP landscape behaves the same way.

A useful page should normally reveal more than the conclusion. It should show the business problem, the evidence I would ask for, the boundary or trade-off I care about, the failure mode I am trying to avoid, and the next decision. That is the part of professional knowledge worth preserving.

## Source handling

A source is used as evidence, not as copy. Source registries record where a claim was checked and when it was reviewed. Public-facing explanations should remain independently written even when several facts come from the same vendor page.

For time-sensitive product or licensing information, the official vendor source remains authoritative. This site records an interpretation at a point in time and may become outdated.

## Corrections and rights concerns

If a rights holder believes that material on this site has been used incorrectly, I will review the concern and correct or remove the material where appropriate. Contact details are available on the [About page](/about/).

This policy describes the publication approach for this site. It is not legal advice.
