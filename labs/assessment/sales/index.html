Assessment rule: do not answer a broad Sales question as a configuration inventory. Start with the business outcome, name the important decisions and owners, show the process boundary, then add technical detail where it changes the result.

Diagnostic rule: start from the symptom, find the first wrong decision or state, collect evidence, identify the owner, and prove the fix end to end.

Open Sales Diagnostic Casebook troubleshoot

90-second anchor

One answer should hold the full Order-to-Cash chain.

Do not memorize every word. Memorize the structure and replace detail when the assessor changes the scenario.

Spoken answer

Order-to-Cash starts with customer demand and ends when both the physical and financial results are complete. In Sales I first define the process variant and the sales-order controls. The order determines partners, product behavior, dates, pricing, availability and credit. Shipping then turns the promise into delivery execution, with EWM or TM at the boundary when they are in scope. Goods issue changes stock and creates accounting impact. Billing creates the customer receivable and revenue postings, while tax and account determination must also be correct.

As a Lead, I do not treat these as isolated SD steps. I ask which rule owns each result, where the first wrong decision appears, which system owns the current state, and how we prove completion. For an incident, I start from the business symptom, trace the document and status chain, compare a few hypotheses, and fix the first wrong decision rather than the last broken document.

What the answer signals

  • Business process before transaction codes.
  • Decision ownership before configuration detail.
  • Physical and financial completion.
  • Cross-domain boundaries instead of an artificial “SD-only” view.
  • Evidence-based diagnosis instead of trial-and-error repair.

Answer depth

Use three speeds, not one giant answer.

The first answer should create a map. Detail belongs in the follow-up.

30 seconds

State the business purpose, the main document chain, two or three controlling decisions, the key boundary, and the completion proof.

Use when: the question is broad or the assessor is checking structure.

2 minutes

Add determination logic, master data, integration points, financial impact, one important process variant, and one design trade-off.

Use when: the assessor asks how the process is designed.

Scenario

Start from the symptom. Build a small hypothesis tree. Find the first wrong decision or state. Name the evidence, owner, fix, regression risk, and proof of business completion.

Use when: the assessor gives an incident or ambiguous requirement.

Priority deep dive / Professional Services Billing

Project billing changes what the billable event means.

For services and customer projects, the invoice may be driven by actual resources, an agreed fixed amount, a billing plan, or project milestones. Start by naming the commercial model. Only then explain the SAP mechanics.

45-second Lead answer

Professional Services Billing starts with the contract model. For Time & Material, the billable amount is based on eligible time, expense, material, service, or other project postings. For fixed price, the customer charge follows the agreed contract amount and billing plan rather than actual cost volume. Milestone billing releases agreed percentages or amounts when planned billing dates or project milestones become billable. The project or WBS structure links execution and financial postings to the commercial scope.

I separate two solution families. In modern S/4HANA Professional Services Project Billing, billing elements and project billing requests control preparation before a billing document request is created. In classic resource-related billing, the Dynamic Item Processor selects source expenditure, summarizes it into dynamic items, determines billing materials, and hands the result to SD pricing. The Lead decision is to keep source-cost selection, customer pricing, billing approval, and revenue recognition as separate controls.

Do not collapse these concepts

  • Actual cost is not automatically customer price. Source postings explain consumed resources; Sales pricing can calculate a different customer charge.
  • Billing is not revenue recognition. Customer invoicing and accounting recognition can follow different timing and rules.
  • WBS is not the billing method. It structures project responsibility and account assignment; the contract model still decides how the customer is billed.
  • Classic RRB/DIP and modern Project Billing are not one identical engine. Deployment and process architecture matter.

Billing models

T&M

Time & Material / Time & Expenses

Bill eligible actual postings such as labor, expenses, materials, and services. The design must define which postings are billable, how they are grouped, which rate applies, and what can be postponed or written off.

Lead question: how do we prove that every customer charge can be traced back to an approved source posting?

FIX

Fixed price

Bill the agreed commercial amount according to a billing plan. Actual project cost is still important for margin and project control, but it does not directly define the customer invoice value.

Lead question: what event or date makes each planned amount due, and how are scope changes controlled?

MILE

Milestone billing

Distribute the contract value across billing dates, percentages, or amounts. In classic Project System scenarios, project milestones can control whether milestone-related billing dates are released.

Lead question: who confirms milestone completion and what prevents early billing?

WBS

WBS-based billing

Use the project structure to connect execution, cost and revenue postings, work packages, and customer billing scope. A billing element can be a project or WBS element depending on the solution.

Lead question: is the WBS structure designed for project delivery only, or also for commercial and reporting responsibility?

Modern Project Billing: end-to-end process

01contract

Define the commercial model

What has the customer agreed to pay for?

Decisions

  • Fixed Price or Time & Expenses
  • billing item and billing plan
  • due dates, amounts, on-account or down-payment logic where applicable

Proof

The project billing item has the intended contract type and billing schedule.

02structure

Connect work to billing elements

Which project work and postings belong to this customer billing scope?

Decisions

  • billing element / WBS structure
  • work-package assignment
  • project stage and billing readiness

Proof

The billing element represents the intended commercial scope and is eligible for project billing.

03execute

Post project execution

Which time, expense, material, or service postings should influence billing?

Time & Expenses

Actual postings up to the relevant billing date are candidates for billing.

Fixed price

The billing plan drives the customer amount; actual postings remain project cost and margin evidence.

04prepare

Prepare project billing

What should be billed now, postponed, or written off?

Control

  • billing due date
  • eligible postings or fixed-price plan items
  • amount to bill
  • postpone / write-off decisions for supported methods

Output

A project billing request captures the prepared commercial decision.

05bill

Create the billing request and invoice

How does approved project billing become a customer invoice?

Flow

Project billing request → billing document request → billing document → FI/customer-facing output.

Completion proof

Reconcile project billing state, billing document, accounting result, and the remaining unbilled amount.

Classic Resource-Related Billing / DIP

account_tree

Memory path: SOURCE → SELECT → CHARACTERIZE → DETERMINE MATERIAL → AGGREGATE → PRICE → REVIEW → BILL.

The Dynamic Item Processor does not simply copy cost lines into an invoice. The DIP profile controls which source data is relevant and how detailed source information becomes customer-facing billing items.

1. Source expenditure

Actual project, order, service, material, activity, personnel, travel, or other supported expenditure provides the source facts. The first question is whether the posting exists in the expected account-assignment object.

2. Source selection

The DIP profile defines which expenditure is relevant. A posting can exist correctly in CO or the project and still never become billable because it does not match the source selection.

3. Characteristics

Characteristics describe source information used for structuring and summarizing dynamic items. Choose them to preserve the invoice transparency the business needs without creating unnecessary line volume.

4. Material determination

Relevant source information is mapped to a sales material or service representation used by the billing document. Missing or wrong determination can stop billing or produce the wrong customer-facing item.

5. Aggregation

The processor summarizes selected data into dynamic items. Too much aggregation hides traceability; too little aggregation creates noisy invoices and high processing volume.

6. SD pricing

After dynamic items exist, Sales pricing determines the commercial price according to the configured pricing context. Cost selection and customer pricing are related, but they are not the same decision.

7. Billing review

Depending on the scenario, billable quantity or amount can be reviewed and items can be rejected or postponed. The design needs a clear audit trail for what was excluded and why.

8. Billing request and invoice

The reviewed result becomes the relevant billing request and then follows Sales Billing into the final customer document and FI handoff.

DIP configuration model

SRC

Source

Which actual or planned source records are eligible? Define the expenditure types and account-assignment scope intentionally.

CHAR

Characteristics

Which attributes must remain visible for grouping, determination, invoice explanation, and later troubleshooting?

MAT

Material determination

How are source characteristics converted into sales materials or service items that SD can price and bill?

AGG

Structuring and aggregation

What invoice granularity is required by the customer, contract, tax, and internal reconciliation process?

USE

Usage and process context

Use the DIP profile for the intended process. Do not assume a profile built for quotation or one service scenario is automatically correct for another billing flow.

PRICE

Pricing handoff

Define which pricing procedure and conditions produce customer value after dynamic-item creation. Do not hard-code the assumption that cost equals price.

Troubleshooting: start from the first missing state

!no items

Costs exist, but RRB finds nothing

Check first

  • correct project/order/service reference
  • actual posting and account assignment
  • DIP profile and intended usage
  • source selection and billing relevance
  • whether the source has already been processed

Lead trap

Changing pricing cannot fix a posting that never became a dynamic item.

!wrong item

Dynamic items exist, but the billing material is wrong

Check first

  • source characteristics
  • material determination
  • aggregation level
  • customer-facing description and unit

Lead trap

Do not repair the invoice manually before proving why the source mapped to the wrong sales item.

!wrong value

Correct resource is selected, but customer value is wrong

Check first

  • pricing context and procedure
  • rate or condition record
  • quantity / unit conversion
  • manual or contractual adjustments
  • billing-date pricing effect

Lead trap

Do not change DIP source selection when the dynamic item is correct and only the commercial price is wrong.

!invoice noise

The invoice has too many or too few lines

Check first

  • characteristics used for grouping
  • aggregation rules
  • customer traceability requirement
  • volume and performance impact

Lead trade-off

Balance invoice readability and auditability against runtime volume. “More detail” is not automatically better.

Integration and ownership boundaries

Project / WBS

Owns project structure, execution scope, account assignment, and the relationship between work packages and billing responsibility.

Time, expense, MM and service postings

Provide source evidence. Their approval and posting quality determine whether T&M billing has trustworthy input.

Sales / Billing

Owns customer-facing commercial value, billing document creation, pricing handoff, tax context, and the customer charge.

FI/CO

Owns accounting state, receivable, project cost/revenue evidence, settlement or recognition processes where applicable, and reconciliation.

Assessment questions

  1. Explain Time & Material billing from a consultant time posting to the customer invoice. Start with the source posting, billability, grouping/material representation, pricing, billing preparation, invoice, and accounting proof.
  2. What does the DIP profile actually control? Explain source selection, characteristics, material determination, structuring/aggregation, process usage, and the handoff to pricing.
  3. A cost is visible on the project but does not appear in resource-related billing. Why? Separate posting existence from DIP source eligibility, billing relevance, processed state, and material determination.
  4. Why can customer price differ from project cost? DIP selects and structures resource data; SD pricing determines the commercial charge. Markup, rate cards, discounts, and other conditions can change value.
  5. Fixed price versus T&M: what changes technically and financially? Explain billing basis, due logic, dependence on actual postings, margin visibility, and correction/change-order handling.
  6. How does milestone billing work? Explain billing plan dates/percentages or amounts, milestone confirmation where used, billing blocks, and release into billing.
  7. What is the role of the WBS or billing element? Explain account assignment and project structure first, then how the commercial billing method is assigned to that scope.
  8. How do you avoid duplicate billing? Use document/process status and processed-source state, controlled billing preparation, correction history, and reconciliation. Do not rely on user memory.
  9. How do you design DIP aggregation? Start from customer invoice transparency, audit trail, tax/commercial needs, and expected transaction volume.
  10. When would you discuss modern Project Billing instead of classic RRB/DIP? First identify the deployment and Professional Services process. Do not present Public Cloud Project Billing and classic PS/CS resource-related billing as one configuration model.

Oral checkpoint

Ten questions a Sales Lead should answer without opening a page.

  1. 01Explain Order-to-Cash end to end.

    Include the main decisions, physical completion, financial completion, and system boundaries.

  2. 02The price is wrong. Where do you start?

    Separate procedure, condition determination, runtime key, record, calculation, manual change, and billing transfer.

  3. 03The requested quantity or date is not confirmed.

    Separate supply reality from ATP design, policy, master data, and downstream scheduling.

  4. 04A credit block appears unexpectedly.

    Explain exposure, check rule, account or segment, trigger, decision owner, release and recheck.

  5. 05The order exists but delivery cannot be created.

    Check due state, blocks, incompletion, item relevance, dates, quantity, shipping determination, and execution boundaries.

  6. 06Delivery is complete but billing is impossible.

    Trace billing relevance, due state, copy control, status, split logic, blocks, and process-specific prerequisites.

  7. 07Billing looks correct but the accounting result is wrong.

    Move across the SD/FI boundary: account determination, tax, value transfer, posting status, and reconciliation evidence.

  8. 08How does a special Sales process differ from standard sell-from-stock?

    Start from the baseline and name the changed rule: supply, ownership, legal entity, production, stock, billing, return, or settlement.

  9. 09Where does Sales ownership stop?

    Name the handoff to FI/CO, MM, PP, EWM, TM, MDG, tax, or integration operations and explain who owns the next state.

  10. 10How do you diagnose an unfamiliar Sales incident?

    Start from business impact, trace the chain, compare hypotheses, find the first wrong state, fix the cause, and prove end-to-end recovery.

Ownership map

Keep the handoffs explicit.

Many weak answers are technically correct inside one component and still wrong end to end because ownership silently changed.

Commercial decision

  • Sales process and order behavior
  • Pricing and commercial conditions
  • Credit and tax dependencies
  • Customer-facing document controls

Promise and execution

  • ATP or aATP owns confirmation logic
  • Shipping owns delivery preparation and scheduling
  • EWM can own warehouse execution
  • TM can own transport planning and execution

Financial truth

  • Goods issue changes inventory and accounting impact
  • Billing creates the commercial accounting handoff
  • FI/CO owns journal, clearing, settlement and reconciliation
  • Completion needs business evidence, not only a green document status

Integration state

  • Define message or business-object identity
  • Know which system owns durable state
  • Separate delivery from processing and commit
  • Design retry, duplicate control and reconciliation

Assessment red flags

Correct detail can still produce a weak Lead answer.

These patterns usually hide the process instead of explaining it.

Configuration dump

Listing customizing objects without first stating the business decision, runtime result, and owner.

Transaction-code answer

Starting from a screen or transaction instead of the process state and evidence. Useful later, not as the map.

Repairing the last document

Changing delivery, billing, or accounting output before proving where the first wrong decision entered the chain.

“It is an SD issue”

Treating FI, MM, EWM, TM, tax, master data, or integration state as somebody else’s mystery instead of an explicit boundary.

No completion proof

Stopping at document creation without checking the physical, financial, integration, and customer-facing outcome.

No trade-off

Presenting one design as universally correct without naming volume, deployment, legal, operational, or governance constraints.
