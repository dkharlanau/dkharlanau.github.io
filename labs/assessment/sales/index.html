90-second anchor

One answer should hold the full Order-to-Cash chain.

Do not memorize every word. Memorize the structure and replace detail when the assessor changes the scenario.

Spoken answer

Order-to-Cash starts with customer demand and ends when both the physical and financial results are complete. In Sales I first define the process variant and the sales-order controls. The order determines partners, product behavior, dates, pricing, availability and credit. Shipping then turns the promise into delivery execution, with EWM or TM at the boundary when they are in scope. Goods issue changes stock and creates accounting impact. Billing creates the customer receivable and revenue postings, while tax and account determination must also be correct.

As a Lead, I do not treat these as isolated SD steps. I ask which rule owns each result, where the first wrong decision appears, which system owns the current state, and how we prove completion. For an incident, I start from the business symptom, trace the document and status chain, compare a few hypotheses, and fix the first wrong decision rather than the last broken document.

What the answer signals

  • Business process before transaction codes.
  • Decision ownership before configuration detail.
  • Physical and financial completion.
  • Cross-domain boundaries instead of an artificial “SD-only” view.
  • Evidence-based diagnosis instead of trial-and-error repair.

Topic 01 / Enterprise structure

Do not memorize boxes. Explain the boundary each box creates.

For an assessment, organizational structure is stronger when we can explain both the SAP definition and the architectural reason for the unit.

Financial boundary

Company Code owns external accounting. A Sales Organization belongs to one Company Code; a Plant also belongs to one Company Code.

Commercial boundary

Sales Organization answers who sells. Distribution Channel answers how we go to market. Division answers which product or service family is in scope.

Commercial context key

Sales Area = Sales Organization + Distribution Channel + Division. It is a valid selling context and a scope for sales-area-dependent customer data.

Commercial-to-logistics bridge

Distribution Chain = Sales Organization + Distribution Channel. Delivering plants are assigned to this pair, which connects the selling model to fulfillment.

Fulfillment node

Plant is where supply can be planned, produced, stored, distributed, or serviced. It connects Sales to ATP, MM, PP, inventory, valuation, and delivery.

Shipping execution node

Shipping Point owns outbound delivery processing. Each outbound delivery is handled by one shipping point.

schema

60-second answer: I see the Sales enterprise structure as a set of responsibility boundaries. Company Code gives the accounting entity. Sales Organization is the commercial seller. Channel and Division add route-to-market and product context; together with Sales Organization they form the Sales Area. The Distribution Chain connects that commercial context to possible delivering plants. The Plant owns the broader fulfillment location, while the Shipping Point owns outbound execution. The design is useful because the same customer, product, and process can behave differently in a controlled context without mixing legal, commercial, and logistics responsibility.

Order-to-Cash / control handoffs

SOURCE → SHIP → BILL → COLLECT.

The Sales order is not the whole process. It creates or carries demand, but fulfillment crosses several logistics and finance owners before cash is collected.

01source

How will the material be supplied?

Stock, external supply, or own production?

Main paths

  • Use available stock.
  • Replenish through Procurement, for example purchase requisition / purchase order depending on the process.
  • Replenish through in-house production, for example planned or production orders depending on planning and execution.
  • Use a special branch such as third-party processing where the supplier can deliver directly to the customer.

Lead distinction

Sourcing is not ATP. Sourcing describes how supply is obtained. ATP describes whether and when the requested quantity can be confirmed under the relevant availability rules. They interact, but they answer different questions.

02ship

Turn the confirmed promise into physical execution

Which document owns outbound execution?

Execution object

The outbound delivery starts shipping execution and controls or supports picking, packing, warehouse processing, transport planning, and goods issue.

Boundary

EWM can own warehouse execution and TM can own transport planning/execution when they are in scope. Posting goods issue changes stock quantity and value and creates the accounting impact of the physical issue.

03bill

Convert fulfillment into a customer charge

What makes the transaction billable?

Billing basis

Physical-product scenarios are commonly delivery-related; service and other scenarios can be order-related. Special processes can use different billing triggers, so the preceding document is part of the process design.

Finance handoff

The billing document transfers commercial data to Finance. Typical accounting includes customer receivables, revenue, and tax-related postings according to configuration.

04collect

Billing is not payment

Who owns the open receivable after Sales has billed?

Owner

Incoming payment belongs to Finance / Accounts Receivable rather than core Sales processing. Payment clears or reduces the customer open item.

Why Sales still cares

Unpaid receivables can affect credit exposure and therefore future Sales orders. The process boundary changes, but the business outcome is still connected.

Process vocabulary

Sales Order Management captures commercial demand and its controls. Sourcing decides how supply is obtained. Shipping / Delivery Processing owns outbound execution. Inventory Management records the stock movement at goods issue. Billing creates the customer charge and accounting handoff. Accounts Receivable owns collection and clearing.

Assessment trap

Do not say “the Sales process ends with delivery” or “billing means the customer paid”. Physical completion, commercial invoicing, accounting posting, and cash collection are separate states with different owners.

Oral drills

  1. Why do we need both Sales Area and Distribution Chain? Sales Area adds the product/division dimension for the commercial context. Distribution Chain is the Sales Organization + Channel pair used in important Sales-to-logistics assignments such as delivering plants.
  2. Why is Plant not part of the Sales Area? Plant is a logistics and fulfillment node, not the definition of who sells what through which commercial route. One selling context can use several plants.
  3. Stock exists, but the order is not confirmed. What does that tell you? Physical stock, ATP scope/policy, plant, dates, requirements, and sourcing are different layers. Start with the confirmation decision, not with the stock number alone.
  4. Where does Sales stop after billing? Billing creates the commercial and accounting handoff. Customer payment and clearing are owned by Finance/AR, although credit management can bring the financial state back into future Sales decisions.

Sales order / three core engines

PRICE → PROMISE → RISK.

Do not mix these decisions. Pricing answers what we charge. ATP answers what we can promise. Credit answers whether we accept the financial risk.

PRICE — Pricing

60-second shape: SAP first determines the pricing procedure. The procedure contains condition types in a controlled sequence. For a condition, the access sequence searches condition records using the runtime key. The result can still change through base formulas, scales, group logic, exclusion, manual conditions, or other calculation controls. In billing, copying control and the pricing type decide whether pricing is copied or determined again.

When wrong: procedure → condition row → requirement → access/key → record/validity → base/rate/scale → interaction.

Open Pricing Engine →

PROMISE — ATP / aATP

60-second shape: scheduling gives ATP the material-availability date. ATP then evaluates the requirement against the relevant supply and demand under the configured check and confirmation policy. Advanced ATP can add capabilities such as alternative-based confirmation or product allocation depending on scope. A stock number alone does not prove what ATP can confirm.

When wrong: plant and requirement → scheduling date → scope/check → supply-demand picture → confirmation strategy → resulting quantity/date.

Open ATP / aATP →

RISK — Credit

60-second shape: the Sales document triggers a credit check for the relevant credit account and segment. The Business Partner credit profile supplies risk policy such as risk class and checking rule; the segment carries values such as credit limit and exposure. The rule can contain several checks, so free credit limit alone does not guarantee a pass.

When wrong: trigger → checked account → segment → risk/check rule → exposure → failed check → recheck or authorized release.

Three traps to avoid

  1. Sourcing is not ATP. Sourcing asks how supply is obtained. ATP asks whether and when the requirement can be confirmed.
  2. Condition record found is not the same as final price. Calculation and interaction can still change whether the condition is active and what value it contributes.
  3. Available credit limit is not the whole credit policy. Another configured check can still block the document.

Sales assessment / Shipping recall

DUE → DELIVERY → EXECUTE → PGI → BILLING DUE.

Use this when the assessor asks what Shipping does, why the delivery document matters, or how the customer date becomes an executable plan.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Shipping turns a due order or other logistics requirement into physical execution. The delivery document is the central object: it carries the shipping context, supports picking and packing, provides status, drives output, and is the reference for goods issue. Before delivery creation, scheduling works backward from the requested delivery date using transit, loading, pick/pack, and transportation-planning time. If the plan is no longer feasible, forward scheduling finds a new feasible date. Operations can create deliveries individually or through the delivery due list. At goods issue, stock and accounting state change, and a normal delivery-related process can move toward billing.

Open the Shipping & Scheduling deep dive arrow_forward
ROLE

Why do we need a delivery document?

It is the logistics execution object and status carrier for picking, packing, output, goods issue, and the handoff to warehouse or transportation execution.

POINT

What determines the shipping point?

In the classic model: delivering plant + shipping condition + loading group. Verify the plant first because it is upstream of the shipping point.

DATES

Which four durations matter?

Transit time, loading time, pick/pack time, and transportation planning lead time. Do not collapse them into one generic “lead time”.

DUE

Due list vs delivery monitor?

The due list finds operations that require delivery creation. The delivery monitor observes deliveries that already exist and supports follow-on processing.

SPLIT

Why can one order create several deliveries?

Different shipping or grouping criteria can require separate delivery documents. A later split can also come from TM or EWM execution, so first identify where the split happened.

EWM

Where does SD Shipping stop?

The delivery owns the ERP shipping document and PGI context; detailed bins, quants, warehouse tasks, warehouse orders, and stock-removal execution belong to EWM when EWM is in scope.

ITEM

How is the delivery item category found?

With a sales-order reference it is normally copied from the order item. Without that reference, determination uses the delivery type, material item-category group, item usage, and higher-level item category where relevant.

Likely follow-up traps

  1. “The delivery always comes from a sales order.” No. Delivery documents also support stock transport, subcontracting, supplier returns, inbound purchase-order processes, and project-driven scenarios.
  2. “A late delivery date means ATP is wrong.” Not necessarily. First read the scheduling chain and the material availability date that ATP received.
  3. “Precise scheduling means ATP checks the exact time.” No. Precise scheduling can calculate to the minute, while ATP checks the material availability date.
  4. “One IDoc basic type is universal for every delivery interface.” Avoid this assumption. Confirm the scenario, message type, basic type, release, and receiving system.

Sales assessment / Billing recall

REFERENCE → DUE → COPY → GROUP → POST → CORRECT.

Billing turns an order or delivery result into a customer charge. For assessment answers, focus on the billing basis, copying control, split logic, pricing behavior, FI handoff, and the correction path.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: A billing document is normally created with reference to an earlier business document. Physical goods are often billed from the outbound delivery, while services and billing-plan scenarios can be order-related. Copying control defines whether the reference is allowed, which quantity is billed, which data is transferred, and how pricing behaves in billing. During collective billing, compatible references can be combined; different header-relevant data can force a split. When an accounting-relevant billing document is posted, the result is transferred to FI for receivables, revenue, tax, and other configured postings. Corrections use controlled follow-on documents such as cancellation, credit memo, debit memo, or invoice correction rather than rewriting history.

Open the Billing deep dive arrow_forward

1. What is the billing reference?

Start with the business event. Ask whether billing is order-related, delivery-related, plan-based, or part of a special process.

2. Why is it billable now?

Check billing relevance, due state, blocks, completion status, and the copying requirement. A source document can exist and still not be ready for billing.

3. What does copy control decide?

It connects source and target documents and controls quantity, data transfer, requirements, and pricing behavior at header and item level.

4. Why one invoice or several?

Compatible source documents can be grouped. If a header-relevant value differs, SAP can create separate billing documents. Use split analysis to prove the differing field.

5. Why can the invoice price differ?

Billing pricing can copy or redetermine pricing according to the configured pricing type. Do not assume the sales-order price is always copied unchanged.

6. What proves completion?

Check the billing document, document flow, billing status, and the FI accounting result. A saved invoice with a failed accounting transfer is not financially complete.

Special branches worth recognizing

PLAN

Billing plans

Periodic or milestone billing changes the due event from physical delivery to planned dates, percentages, amounts, or project milestones.

DOWN

Down payments

Sales can request and later clear down payments, while the actual money movement belongs to Finance. Keep request, receipt, and final clearing separate.

PRO

Pro forma

A pro forma document is informational and does not create the normal FI posting. Do not treat it as proof that the customer receivable exists.

CORR

Corrections

Cancellation reverses the original billing event. Credit, debit, and invoice-correction processes adjust the commercial result with document history intact.

Fast diagnostic questions

  1. Nothing appears in the billing due list. Is the source billing-relevant, due, complete, and free from the relevant block?
  2. Two deliveries created two invoices. Which billing-header field differs? Prove it with split analysis before changing configuration.
  3. Order and invoice values differ. Which pricing type was used in copying control, and which conditions were copied or redetermined?
  4. Billing exists but FI is missing. Check posting block, account determination, tax/accounting data, and accounting transfer status.
  5. The original invoice is wrong. Choose the correction process that preserves document history instead of editing the financial past.

Order-to-Cash / business event side effects

PGI, Billing, and Payment commit different business states.

Do not describe Order-to-Cash only as a document sequence. Explain what becomes true in logistics and finance when each event is posted.

conversion_path

Memory model: PGI = physical + valuation → Billing = commercial + receivable → Payment = cash + clearing.

PGI — goods have left

Stock quantity and value change, the goods movement creates its accounting effect, document status changes, and a normal delivery-related process can move toward billing. The customer is not necessarily invoiced yet.

Billing — customer is charged

The billing document creates the commercial result and transfers accounting-relevant values to FI. Receivable, revenue, tax, and related postings are created according to configuration. Cash has not necessarily been received.

Payment — receivable is settled

Finance posts incoming payment and clears or reduces the customer open item. Differences, discounts, deductions, and residual items belong to the AR process.

PROOF

Physical completion

Use delivery, goods movement, stock and accounting evidence. Delivery creation alone is not the physical commit.

PROOF

Commercial completion

Use billing status, billing document, FI transfer and accounting document. A green billing screen is not enough if accounting failed.

PROOF

Cash completion

Use customer open-item and clearing evidence. Billing does not mean payment.

OWNER

Ownership changes

Shipping owns the physical execution, Sales Billing owns the customer charge, and Finance/AR owns payment and clearing.

Assessment traps

  1. “PGI means the invoice exists.” No. PGI commits the goods issue; billing is a later commercial event.
  2. “Billing means Order-to-Cash is financially complete.” The receivable can still be open until payment and clearing.
  3. “FI is a downstream black box.” A Lead should know which Sales event created the accounting handoff and how to prove whether that handoff completed.

Sales monitoring apps

OBSERVE → TRACE → RESOLVE.

Do not memorize three app names as unrelated tools. They form a useful operational sequence around the same fulfillment process.

F2577 — Observe

Track Sales Orders. Portfolio-level view for customer inquiries and order-status monitoring. It answers where the order is in fulfillment and whether the process has issues.

F2981 — Trace

Track Sales Order Details. Drill into one order, its process flow, subsequent documents, statuses, items, and issues.

F0029A — Resolve

Sales Order Fulfillment - Analyze and Resolve Issues. Exception-oriented workbench for orders that are blocked or at risk and need analysis or action.

troubleshoot

Assessment answer: F2577 is mainly for visibility, F2981 for a detailed process trace, and F0029A for exception management. I would use the monitoring app to locate the bad process phase, then move to the owning document or domain to prove and resolve the first wrong state.

Sales master data / Multiple Address Handling

IDENTITY → ROLE → ADDRESS → BEHAVIOR.

The feature is easier to explain when we separate the legal customer from the physical location used in one transaction.

Identity

One Business Partner can represent the legal customer instead of creating another customer record for every branch or warehouse.

Role

Sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, and payer are partner functions. The role answers who participates in the process.

Address

The same BP can have several valid addresses, including several valid addresses for the same role such as ship-to.

Behavior

An address can carry address-dependent data such as delivering plant, shipping condition, Incoterms, tax classification, or unloading point. Selecting another address can therefore change process behavior.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Multiple Address Handling separates customer identity from physical location. Instead of creating several customer masters for one legal entity, we can maintain several BP addresses with usages and validity periods. Partner determination chooses the business role, address determination narrows the valid physical locations, and supported address-dependent data is copied into the sales document. I would pay special attention to migration, because activation does not consolidate old records automatically and integrations may need the BP address UUID rather than only the customer number or free-text address.

TRAP

Partner ≠ address

A wrong ship-to partner and a correct ship-to partner with the wrong physical address are different defects and have different owners.

VALID

Validity matters

Address usage and date validity are part of determination. A branch move can be modeled without changing the customer identity.

DATA

The address can drive data

Do not debug plant or shipping condition only in ordinary customer master data if the selected address has address-dependent values.

API

Use address identity

Where supported, APIs identify the selected BP address using a UUID. This is stronger than passing only formatted address text.

Priority deep dive / Professional Services Billing

First decide what makes the work billable.

Project billing is easier when we separate the commercial model from the source evidence. Time & Material, fixed price, milestones, and classic Resource-Related Billing do not use the same billable event.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: I start with the contract: are we billing actual effort, a fixed amount, a milestone, or another planned event? For Time & Material, approved time, expenses, materials, or other project postings become source evidence and are prepared for customer billing. For fixed-price or milestone models, the due amount is driven mainly by the agreed billing plan or project billing rules rather than by reimbursing every cost line. In classic Resource-Related Billing, the DIP profile selects eligible source expenditure, keeps the characteristics needed for grouping and material determination, creates dynamic items, and then hands them to SD pricing and billing. I separate source selection from customer pricing, because a cost can be valid but not billable, or billable but priced differently for the customer.

Time & Material

Billability depends on trusted source postings such as approved time, expenses, services, materials, or other eligible project costs. The main risk is missing, duplicate, or wrongly classified source evidence.

Fixed price

The customer charge is agreed independently from the exact actual cost. Margin therefore depends on controlling the delivery cost while billing follows the commercial plan.

Milestone billing

The billable event is a planned milestone or billing date. The design must connect project progress, billing block/release, amount or percentage, and the final invoice.

Resource-Related Billing / DIP

Classic RRB converts selected project or service expenditure into dynamic billing items. The DIP profile controls source selection, characteristics, material determination, and aggregation before SD pricing.

Classic DIP memory path

SOURCE→ SELECT→ CHARACTERIZE→ MATERIAL→ AGGREGATE→ PRICE→ BILL
SRC

Source selection

A posting can exist correctly in the project and still not be billable. Prove the project/order reference, account assignment, eligibility, and processed state first.

MAT

Material determination

Source characteristics are converted into a customer-facing sales material or service representation. Wrong mapping creates the wrong billing item even when the cost is correct.

AGG

Aggregation

Too much detail makes invoices noisy; too much aggregation destroys traceability. Choose the granularity required by the customer, contract, tax, and audit process.

PRICE

Commercial pricing

Cost selection and customer price are different decisions. Rate cards, markups, discounts, units, and other conditions can make the billed value different from project cost.

Fast troubleshooting

Cost exists, no billable item

Check source eligibility, account assignment, DIP/profile usage, billing relevance, and whether the source was already processed. Pricing cannot repair a source that never became a billing item.

Billable item exists, wrong material

Check source characteristics, material determination, units, and aggregation before editing the invoice manually.

Correct item, wrong customer value

Move to SD pricing: procedure, rate, unit conversion, date, and commercial conditions. Do not change source selection when the dynamic item is correct.

Too many or too few invoice lines

Review the characteristics used for grouping and the required invoice transparency. This is an aggregation design problem, not automatically a pricing problem.

Ownership boundary

Project / WBS

Owns project structure, account assignment, work scope, and the project-side billing context.

Time, expense, MM and service postings

Provide the source evidence. Their approval and posting quality determine whether the billing input is trustworthy.

Sales / Billing

Owns the customer-facing item, pricing, billing document, tax context, and customer charge.

FI/CO

Owns cost and revenue evidence, receivables, accounting state, and reconciliation.

Assessment questions

  1. Explain T&M from time posting to invoice. Source posting → eligibility → billing preparation → customer-facing item → pricing → billing → FI.
  2. What does the DIP profile control? Source selection, characteristics, material determination, aggregation, and process usage before pricing.
  3. Why can cost differ from customer price? The project provides cost evidence; SD pricing determines the commercial charge.
  4. Fixed price versus T&M? Fixed price is driven by the commercial plan; T&M depends on eligible actual source evidence.
  5. How do you avoid duplicate billing? Use processed-source state, document status, controlled billing preparation, correction history, and reconciliation.
  6. Modern Project Billing or classic RRB/DIP? Identify the deployment and process first. Do not mix the two configuration models.

Cross-application / Output Control

OBJECT → TYPE → RECEIVER → CHANNEL → FORM → RELEVANCE.

Output Control is not only a Sales feature. It is a cross-application S/4HANA framework. In a Sales question, first identify whether the document uses SAP S/4HANA Output Management or classic SD output determination.

1. Framework

For Sales_Document, S/4HANA Output Management can be activated in Customizing. A framework switch applies to new documents; old documents keep the framework used when they were created.

2. Decision tables

Output Parameter Determination uses BRFplus rules. Conditions come from document attributes; results define output type, recipient, channel, printer/email settings, form template, and output relevance.

3. Rule order

Rows are checked from top to bottom, so specific rules should come before general rules. Receiver and channel rules can be exclusive when only the first matching result should be used.

4. Processing

If the right parameters are determined but the output still fails, move to rendering and channel processing. Do not debug the mail server before proving the determination.

8 steps

Sales memory

Output Type, Recipient, Channel, Printer Settings, E-Mail Settings, E-Mail Recipient, Form Template, Output Relevance.

Example

Same sales org, different channel

Germany + End Customer can return EMAIL, while Germany + Wholesale can return PRINT. Business attributes are conditions; channel is the result.

Trap

Do not answer “NACE” automatically

Classic output determination and S/4HANA Output Control are different models. First prove which framework owns the document.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: SAP S/4HANA Output Control is a cross-application framework. For Sales I first confirm whether Sales_Document uses the S/4HANA framework or classic SD output determination. In the S/4HANA framework, Output Parameter Determination uses BRFplus decision tables. Document attributes are conditions and the rules return the output type, receiver, channel, channel-specific settings, form template, and output relevance. Rows are ordered from specific to general, and exclusive receiver or channel rules can stop additional matches. I troubleshoot from determination to relevance, form, and only then technical sending or printing.

Sales document control / Copy control

SOURCE → CHECK → TRANSFER → TARGET → STATUS.

Copy control explains how one sales document becomes the next. For an assessment, the important point is not the transaction code. It is the control chain between the predecessor and the follow-on document.

1. Source → target

First prove that the document-type relationship exists. Copy control is structured at header, item, and schedule-line level, so the valid chain can depend on more than the two header document types.

2. Copying requirement

This is the gate. SAP checks whether the reference is allowed before it transfers data. A requirement can reject the copy because of business conditions such as status, validity, customer context, or an already completed reference.

3. Data transfer and switches

Transfer routines decide which fields move or are derived. Switches control specific behavior, for example whether source item numbers are copied into the target.

4. Target categories

At item and schedule-line level, the target category must be valid for the relevant category assignment. If no valid target is entered, SAP can propose one from that assignment.

5. Document flow update

At item level, this setting updates referred quantities or values and therefore supports the reference status of the preceding document.

6. Completion rule

The completion rule belongs to the source item category. It decides when the predecessor item becomes Completed. This is separate from the copy-control switch that updates document flow.

Rule A

First reference can complete

An inquiry item can be completed as soon as a quotation refers to it, even when only part of the quantity is referenced.

Rule B

Full quantity must be referenced

A quotation can stay Partially referred after a partial order. Further orders can still reference the remaining quantity until the full quantity is used.

Reject

Rejection also closes the item

If a reason for rejection is entered, the item receives Completed status.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: I see copy control as a source-to-target contract. I first check the valid source and target relationship, then the copying requirement, transfer routine, switches, and item or schedule-line mapping. If the question is about partial reference or predecessor status, I separate two controls: Document flow update records the reference progress, while the completion rule in the source item category decides when that item is complete.

Oral checkpoint

Ten questions a Sales Lead should answer without opening a page.

  1. 01Explain Order-to-Cash end to end.

    Include the main decisions, physical completion, financial completion, and system boundaries.

  2. 02The price is wrong. Where do you start?

    Separate procedure, condition determination, runtime key, record, calculation, manual change, and billing transfer.

  3. 03The requested quantity or date is not confirmed.

    Separate supply reality from ATP design, policy, master data, and downstream scheduling.

  4. 04A credit block appears unexpectedly.

    Explain exposure, check rule, account or segment, trigger, decision owner, release and recheck.

  5. 05The order exists but delivery cannot be created.

    Check due state, blocks, incompletion, item relevance, dates, quantity, shipping determination, and execution boundaries.

  6. 06Delivery is complete but billing is impossible.

    Trace billing relevance, due state, copy control, status, split logic, blocks, and process-specific prerequisites.

  7. 07Billing looks correct but the accounting result is wrong.

    Move across the SD/FI boundary: account determination, tax, value transfer, posting status, and reconciliation evidence.

  8. 08How does a special Sales process differ from standard sell-from-stock?

    Start from the baseline and name the changed rule: supply, ownership, legal entity, production, stock, billing, return, or settlement.

  9. 09A Sales process crosses a system or domain boundary. How do you design the handoff?

    Name the business object or document, direction, sync/async need, next owner, correlation and idempotency approach, monitoring, recovery, and proof that the target business state completed.

  10. 10How do you diagnose an unfamiliar Sales incident?

    Start from business impact, trace the chain, compare hypotheses, find the first wrong state, fix the cause, and prove end-to-end recovery.

Ownership map

Keep the handoffs explicit.

Many weak answers are technically correct inside one component and still wrong end to end because ownership silently changed.

Commercial decision

  • Sales process and order behavior
  • Pricing and commercial conditions
  • Credit and tax dependencies
  • Customer-facing document controls

Promise and execution

  • ATP or aATP owns confirmation logic
  • Shipping owns delivery preparation and scheduling
  • EWM can own warehouse execution
  • TM can own transport planning and execution

Financial truth

  • Goods issue changes inventory and accounting impact
  • Billing creates the commercial accounting handoff
  • FI/CO owns journal, clearing, settlement and reconciliation
  • Completion needs business evidence, not only a green document status

Integration state

  • Define message or business-object identity
  • Know which system owns durable state
  • Separate delivery from processing and commit
  • Design retry, duplicate control and reconciliation

Assessment red flags

Correct detail can still produce a weak Lead answer.

These patterns usually hide the process instead of explaining it.

Configuration dump

Listing customizing objects without first stating the business decision, runtime result, and owner.

Transaction-code answer

Starting from a screen or transaction instead of the process state and evidence. Useful later, not as the map.

Repairing the last document

Changing delivery, billing, or accounting output before proving where the first wrong decision entered the chain.

“It is an SD issue”

Treating FI, MM, EWM, TM, tax, master data, or integration state as somebody else’s mystery instead of an explicit boundary.

No completion proof

Stopping at document creation without checking the physical, financial, integration, and customer-facing outcome.

No trade-off

Presenting one design as universally correct without naming volume, deployment, legal, operational, or governance constraints.

Read less. Retrieve more.

Use the deep pages to correct gaps, then return to oral practice. Re-reading everything feels productive because humans enjoy progress bars; the assessment rewards retrieval under pressure.
