Assessment rule: do not answer a broad Sales question as a configuration inventory. Start with the business outcome, name the important decisions and owners, show the process boundary, then add technical detail where it changes the result.

Diagnostic rule: start from the symptom, find the first wrong decision or state, collect evidence, identify the owner, and prove the fix end to end.

Open Sales Diagnostic Casebook troubleshoot

90-second anchor

One answer should hold the full Order-to-Cash chain.

Do not memorize every word. Memorize the structure and replace detail when the assessor changes the scenario.

Spoken answer

Order-to-Cash starts with customer demand and ends when both the physical and financial results are complete. In Sales I first define the process variant and the sales-order controls. The order determines partners, product behavior, dates, pricing, availability and credit. Shipping then turns the promise into delivery execution, with EWM or TM at the boundary when they are in scope. Goods issue changes stock and creates accounting impact. Billing creates the customer receivable and revenue postings, while tax and account determination must also be correct.

As a Lead, I do not treat these as isolated SD steps. I ask which rule owns each result, where the first wrong decision appears, which system owns the current state, and how we prove completion. For an incident, I start from the business symptom, trace the document and status chain, compare a few hypotheses, and fix the first wrong decision rather than the last broken document.

What the answer signals

  • Business process before transaction codes.
  • Decision ownership before configuration detail.
  • Physical and financial completion.
  • Cross-domain boundaries instead of an artificial “SD-only” view.
  • Evidence-based diagnosis instead of trial-and-error repair.

Topic 01 / Enterprise structure

Do not memorize boxes. Explain the boundary each box creates.

For an assessment, organizational structure is stronger when we can explain both the SAP definition and the architectural reason for the unit.

Financial boundary

Company Code owns external accounting. A Sales Organization belongs to one Company Code; a Plant also belongs to one Company Code.

Commercial boundary

Sales Organization answers who sells. Distribution Channel answers how we go to market. Division answers which product or service family is in scope.

Commercial context key

Sales Area = Sales Organization + Distribution Channel + Division. It is a valid selling context and a scope for sales-area-dependent customer data.

Commercial-to-logistics bridge

Distribution Chain = Sales Organization + Distribution Channel. Delivering plants are assigned to this pair, which connects the selling model to fulfillment.

Fulfillment node

Plant is where supply can be planned, produced, stored, distributed, or serviced. It connects Sales to ATP, MM, PP, inventory, valuation, and delivery.

Shipping execution node

Shipping Point owns outbound delivery processing. Each outbound delivery is handled by one shipping point.

schema

60-second answer: I see the Sales enterprise structure as a set of responsibility boundaries. Company Code gives the accounting entity. Sales Organization is the commercial seller. Channel and Division add route-to-market and product context; together with Sales Organization they form the Sales Area. The Distribution Chain connects that commercial context to possible delivering plants. The Plant owns the broader fulfillment location, while the Shipping Point owns outbound execution. The design is useful because the same customer, product, and process can behave differently in a controlled context without mixing legal, commercial, and logistics responsibility.

Order-to-Cash / control handoffs

SOURCE → SHIP → BILL → COLLECT.

The Sales order is not the whole process. It creates or carries demand, but fulfillment crosses several logistics and finance owners before cash is collected.

01source

How will the material be supplied?

Stock, external supply, or own production?

Main paths

  • Use available stock.
  • Replenish through Procurement, for example purchase requisition / purchase order depending on the process.
  • Replenish through in-house production, for example planned or production orders depending on planning and execution.
  • Use a special branch such as third-party processing where the supplier can deliver directly to the customer.

Lead distinction

Sourcing is not ATP. Sourcing describes how supply is obtained. ATP describes whether and when the requested quantity can be confirmed under the relevant availability rules. They interact, but they answer different questions.

02ship

Turn the confirmed promise into physical execution

Which document owns outbound execution?

Execution object

The outbound delivery starts shipping execution and controls or supports picking, packing, warehouse processing, transport planning, and goods issue.

Boundary

EWM can own warehouse execution and TM can own transport planning/execution when they are in scope. Posting goods issue changes stock quantity and value and creates the accounting impact of the physical issue.

03bill

Convert fulfillment into a customer charge

What makes the transaction billable?

Billing basis

Physical-product scenarios are commonly delivery-related; service and other scenarios can be order-related. Special processes can use different billing triggers, so the preceding document is part of the process design.

Finance handoff

The billing document transfers commercial data to Finance. Typical accounting includes customer receivables, revenue, and tax-related postings according to configuration.

04collect

Billing is not payment

Who owns the open receivable after Sales has billed?

Owner

Incoming payment belongs to Finance / Accounts Receivable rather than core Sales processing. Payment clears or reduces the customer open item.

Why Sales still cares

Unpaid receivables can affect credit exposure and therefore future Sales orders. The process boundary changes, but the business outcome is still connected.

Process vocabulary

Sales Order Management captures commercial demand and its controls. Sourcing decides how supply is obtained. Shipping / Delivery Processing owns outbound execution. Inventory Management records the stock movement at goods issue. Billing creates the customer charge and accounting handoff. Accounts Receivable owns collection and clearing.

Assessment trap

Do not say “the Sales process ends with delivery” or “billing means the customer paid”. Physical completion, commercial invoicing, accounting posting, and cash collection are separate states with different owners.

Oral drills

  1. Why do we need both Sales Area and Distribution Chain? Sales Area adds the product/division dimension for the commercial context. Distribution Chain is the Sales Organization + Channel pair used in important Sales-to-logistics assignments such as delivering plants.
  2. Why is Plant not part of the Sales Area? Plant is a logistics and fulfillment node, not the definition of who sells what through which commercial route. One selling context can use several plants.
  3. Stock exists, but the order is not confirmed. What does that tell you? Physical stock, ATP scope/policy, plant, dates, requirements, and sourcing are different layers. Start with the confirmation decision, not with the stock number alone.
  4. Where does Sales stop after billing? Billing creates the commercial and accounting handoff. Customer payment and clearing are owned by Finance/AR, although credit management can bring the financial state back into future Sales decisions.

Sales assessment / Shipping recall

DUE → DELIVERY → EXECUTE → PGI → BILLING DUE.

Use this when the assessor asks what Shipping does, why the delivery document matters, or how the customer date becomes an executable plan.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Shipping turns a due order or other logistics requirement into physical execution. The delivery document is the central object: it carries the shipping context, supports picking and packing, provides status, drives output, and is the reference for goods issue. Before delivery creation, scheduling works backward from the requested delivery date using transit, loading, pick/pack, and transportation-planning time. If the plan is no longer feasible, forward scheduling finds a new feasible date. Operations can create deliveries individually or through the delivery due list. At goods issue, stock and accounting state change, and a normal delivery-related process can move toward billing.

Open the Shipping & Scheduling deep dive arrow_forward
ROLE

Why do we need a delivery document?

It is the logistics execution object and status carrier for picking, packing, output, goods issue, and the handoff to warehouse or transportation execution.

POINT

What determines the shipping point?

In the classic model: delivering plant + shipping condition + loading group. Verify the plant first because it is upstream of the shipping point.

DATES

Which four durations matter?

Transit time, loading time, pick/pack time, and transportation planning lead time. Do not collapse them into one generic “lead time”.

DUE

Due list vs delivery monitor?

The due list finds operations that require delivery creation. The delivery monitor observes deliveries that already exist and supports follow-on processing.

SPLIT

Why can one order create several deliveries?

Different shipping or grouping criteria can require separate delivery documents. A later split can also come from TM or EWM execution, so first identify where the split happened.

EWM

Where does SD Shipping stop?

The delivery owns the ERP shipping document and PGI context; detailed bins, quants, warehouse tasks, warehouse orders, and stock-removal execution belong to EWM when EWM is in scope.

ITEM

How is the delivery item category found?

With a sales-order reference it is normally copied from the order item. Without that reference, determination uses the delivery type, material item-category group, item usage, and higher-level item category where relevant.

Likely follow-up traps

  1. “The delivery always comes from a sales order.” No. Delivery documents also support stock transport, subcontracting, supplier returns, inbound purchase-order processes, and project-driven scenarios.
  2. “A late delivery date means ATP is wrong.” Not necessarily. First read the scheduling chain and the material availability date that ATP received.
  3. “Precise scheduling means ATP checks the exact time.” No. Precise scheduling can calculate to the minute, while ATP checks the material availability date.
  4. “One IDoc basic type is universal for every delivery interface.” Avoid this assumption. Confirm the scenario, message type, basic type, release, and receiving system.

Sales assessment / Billing recall

REFERENCE → DUE → COPY → GROUP → VALUE → POST → CORRECT.

Billing is the commercial and financial handoff of Order-to-Cash. We first identify what is being billed and from which reference. Then we prove how SAP copies quantity and value, whether several references may share one invoice, and whether the billing document reached Financial Accounting.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: In SAP S/4HANA Sales, a billing document is normally created with reference to an existing business document. For physical goods this is often the outbound delivery; for services and other scenarios it can be the sales order. Copying control defines which source and target documents are allowed, which quantity is billed, how data is transferred, and whether pricing is copied or determined again. During collective billing, SAP combines compatible references and splits them when header-relevant data is different. When an accounting-relevant billing document is posted, it updates document flow and billing status and transfers the financial result to FI, where receivables, revenue, tax, and related accounts are posted. Corrections use controlled documents such as cancellation, credit memo, debit memo, or invoice correction rather than changing history.

What a billing document changes

BILL

Create the customer charge

Billing can create invoices, credit memos, debit memos, returns-related credits, cancellations, pro forma documents, intercompany billing, and invoice lists depending on the process.

FLOW

Update document flow

The billing document becomes part of the document chain. The preceding order, delivery, request, or billing document keeps its relationship to the new billing document.

STAT

Update billing status

The source document or billing-plan date is updated according to the billing result. Pro forma billing is an important exception because it does not update the billing status of the reference document.

FI

Create Financial Accounting documents

Accounting-relevant billing transfers values to FI. The accounting result is a separate state: an SD billing document can exist while FI transfer is blocked or has failed.

DATA

Update analytics and credit context

Billing contributes to reporting and can change the customer credit exposure depending on the process and configuration.

CO

Feed profitability analysis

Billing values can be passed to profitability and controlling processes. The exact financial design belongs to the FI/CO boundary.

Billing basis: start with the reference document

Delivery-related billing

Use the outbound delivery as the billing reference when the commercial rule is to invoice what was delivered. This is common for physical goods. A copying requirement can require goods issue before the invoice is created.

Order-related billing

Use the sales document as the billing reference when no delivery is required for the billing event. Services are a common example. Billing plans also create order-related billing dates.

Mixed billing

One invoice can contain order-related and delivery-related items when the normal combination criteria are compatible. The billing document is still one commercial result built from different references.

INV

Invoice

Reference: sales document or delivery.

CR

Credit memo

Reference: credit memo request or an existing billing document, depending on the complaint process.

DR

Debit memo

Reference: debit memo request or an existing billing document.

RET

Returns credit

Reference: returns document.

PRO

Pro forma

Reference: sales document or delivery. It does not create an FI posting.

CANC

Cancellation

Reference: the billing document that must be reversed.

LIST

Invoice list

Reference: billing documents that have already been created and posted.

ICO

Intercompany billing

Reference: delivery in the standard flow described in the training material.

Billing types worth recognizing

Customer billing

F2 invoice · BV cash sale · RE returns billing.

Adjustments and reversal

G2 credit memo · L2 debit memo · S1 cancellation invoice · S2 cancellation credit memo.

Special billing

F8 pro forma invoice · IV intercompany invoice · IG intercompany credit memo · LR invoice list · LG credit memo list.

The billing type controls the whole billing document. For an assessment, explain the business purpose first and use the type code as supporting detail, not as the answer.

Copying control: where the source becomes the billing document

Header control

The source document type and target billing type define whether the reference is allowed and how header data is transferred. Header rules can also influence reference numbers, allocation numbers, foreign-trade data, and item numbering.

Item control

At item level, the source item category is part of the key. The important decisions are billing quantity, pricing behavior, exchange-rate handling, price source, and data-transfer routines.

Copying requirements

Requirements decide whether billing is allowed for the specific case. A requirement can, for example, check that goods issue has been posted. Customer-specific requirements can be implemented with VOFM in classic Customizing.

functions

Quantity memory: order-related billing normally starts from order quantity minus quantity already billed. Delivery-related billing starts from delivered quantity minus quantity already billed. In the standard pro forma examples, F5 uses order quantity and F8 uses delivery quantity.

Pricing in billing: do not assume the invoice always copies the order price unchanged

A

Copy and update scales

Pricing elements are copied from the reference and scale values are updated.

B

New pricing

Pricing is carried out again in the billing document.

C

Keep manual, reprice the rest

Manual pricing elements are copied; the other elements are determined again.

D

Copy unchanged

Pricing elements are copied from the reference document without redetermination.

G

Copy, redetermine tax

Pricing elements are copied unchanged and tax conditions are determined again.

H

Copy, redetermine freight

Pricing elements are copied unchanged and freight is determined again.

If the order value and invoice value differ, first identify the pricing type used in copying control. Do not start by changing condition records.

Billing due list: selection and creation are different steps

Individual billing

Create one billing document for each selected source. There is no attempt to combine several selected references.

Collective billing

Process all selected due items and combine compatible references as far as possible. The system returns a processing result for successful and failed items.

Collective billing online

Move to billing creation while still using the normal combination rules. This gives the user more direct control during creation.

Use simulation before saving when you want to see the expected billing documents and errors. For regular high-volume processing, billing can run in background jobs at a planned time or schedule. The Fiori app Create Billing Documents — Billing Due List Items also supports billing settings such as a chosen billing date/type, separate billing per due-list item, automatic posting, and temporary billing documents for review before final creation.

Invoice combination and split

call_split

Lead rule: SAP tries to combine compatible transactions. If one customer receives two invoices, prove which header-relevant field is different before adding custom logic. The cause is usually a difference that cannot live on one billing-document header.

What can force a split?

Examples include payer or bill-to context, billing date, sales organization, currency, payment terms, tax-related context, customer reference, or custom header-relevant fields.

How do we prove it?

Use split analysis and compare the resulting header fields. Do not infer the cause only from the two source documents.

Can we force individual billing?

Yes. Copying control can be configured so that each source creates its own billing document. In the standard training example, data transfer routine 3 uses the reference document number in VBRK-ZUKRI to create an intentional split.

Complaint processing: reversal, adjustment, and correction are different

CANCEL

Cancellation

Use when the original billing event itself must be reversed. SAP creates a cancellation billing document and preserves the document chain.

CREDIT

Credit memo request

A request can be blocked for billing until review. The responsible person can release it, reject items, or adjust the amount or quantity to be credited.

DEBIT

Debit memo request

Use the same controlled request-and-release idea when the customer must be charged more.

ICR

Invoice correction request

Use item pairs to correct quantity or price differences. The debit item carries the corrected quantity or pricing result.

WF

Workflow

Credit memo requests can use approval workflow with value thresholds, automatic release conditions, and approvers. The manager can approve, reject, or ask for rework.

PRO

Pro forma invoice

Use for information or export-related scenarios. It does not transfer data to FI, and the reference billing status is not updated, so several pro forma documents can be created.

Billing plans: when one order has several billing dates

Periodic billing

Used for recurring charges such as rental or service agreements. The billing plan contains a sequence of billing dates, often based on a contract start/end date and a periodic rule.

Milestone billing

Distribute the total contract value across several dates, percentages, or fixed amounts. A billing-plan date can stay blocked until the related project milestone is confirmed.

Billing rule

The rule for a billing-plan date decides how its billing value is calculated, for example as a fixed amount or a percentage of the total value and whether it acts as a final invoice.

Down payments: Sales request, FI money movement, final clearing

01agree

Put the down-payment agreement into the billing plan

How much must the customer pay and when?

Control

The agreement can be a fixed amount or a percentage. Standard billing rules 4 and 5 cover percentage- and value-related milestone down-payment scenarios.

Pricing detail

Condition type AZWR is used for down-payment items in the described standard setup.

02request

Create the down-payment request

What does Sales send to Finance?

FI result

The down-payment request is posted as a noted item with special G/L indicator F; it is statistical rather than a normal receivable posting.

Business use

The request can also control whether later delivery processing remains blocked until payment conditions are met.

03receive

Post the customer down payment

Where is the real payment recorded?

FI result

The received down payment is assigned to the request and sales-order account. The training material uses special G/L indicator A for the payment.

Status

The sales process can distinguish not paid, partially paid, and fully paid states.

04clear

Clear the down payment in partial or final billing

How does the customer avoid paying twice?

Billing

Down payments already received are transferred into the billing document as amounts to be cleared.

Final invoice

Remaining down payments are taken into account so that the final customer receivable reflects the agreed settlement.

Special billing patterns

EBDR

External Billing Document Request

An EBDR stores billing data received from an external source inside S/4HANA. It appears in the billing due list and can be billed alone or combined with internal billable SD items.

Use case: one invoice can combine hardware delivery, a software order, consulting charges, and external usage-based data.

CONV

Omnichannel convergent billing

Convergent billing combines billable data from different internal and external channels into one customer invoice when the combination rules allow it.

PRE

Preliminary billing document

A preliminary billing document can be reviewed before final billing. Its statuses include In Progress, Rejected, Finalized, and Completed.

Important: creating the preliminary document changes the preceding document billing status and removes it from the normal due list until the preliminary document is rejected or converted.

LIST

Invoice list

Several already-created billing documents can later be grouped in an invoice list. The billing documents must be posted to FI before they are combined.

Billing to Financial Accounting

Organizational bridge

The Sales Organization is assigned to a Company Code. The Company Code owns the accounting context, including its chart of accounts. This is the organizational bridge from Sales Billing into FI.

Account determination

When billing is transferred to FI, the system determines G/L accounts for revenue, sales deductions, VAT, and other configured postings. Cash sales are a special case because posting goes to a cash settlement account instead of the customer account.

Posting block

A billing type can prevent automatic FI transfer. In that case, the billing document is created first and accounting documents are created only after release. If account determination has an error, the accounting documents are also not created until the error is corrected.

REF

Reference and assignment numbers

Copying control can define which business number is passed to FI, for example customer reference, sales order, delivery, or billing document. This is important for later payment matching and reconciliation.

DOC

FI document type

Billing type configuration can transfer a specific FI document type. If no special type is entered, the standard training material describes RV as the default for SD billing documents.

NEG

Negative postings

Credit memos and cancellations can be configured for negative posting so that the account-side presentation reflects the reversal logic. The Company Code must also permit negative postings.

BRANCH

Head office and branch

Billing type settings influence which customer partner is transferred to FI when head-office and branch relationships are used.

Operations and technical recall

Main billing transactions

VF01/VF02/VF03 create/change/display billing documents · VF04 billing due list · VF11 cancellation · VF06 background billing · VFX3 blocked billing documents.

Invoice-list transactions

VF25 create/change/display invoice list · VF24 invoice-list worklist.

Core tables

VBRK billing header · VBRP billing items · VBFA document flow · VBPA partners · PRCD_COND pricing conditions.

Use transaction codes and tables as evidence after the process model is clear. For Public Cloud or Fiori-first environments, start from the relevant apps and business roles rather than assuming classic SAP GUI access.

Likely follow-up traps

  1. “Billing means payment.” No. Billing creates the customer charge and usually the receivable; incoming payment and clearing belong to Accounts Receivable.
  2. “Every invoice must reference a delivery.” No. Order-related billing is standard for services and other processes; billing plans are also order-related.
  3. “If the invoice exists, FI must be complete.” No. Posting can be blocked or fail after the billing document has been created.
  4. “The billing due list is the invoice.” No. The due list is a worklist of references that are ready or expected to be billed.
  5. “One customer means one invoice.” No. Incompatible header values create an invoice split.
  6. “The invoice always copies the order price.” No. Pricing behavior is controlled by the pricing type in copying control.
  7. “A pro forma invoice is an accounting invoice.” No. It does not post to FI and does not update reference billing status.
  8. “A credit memo request and a credit memo are the same document.” No. The request is the controlled sales document; the credit memo is the billing result.
  9. “Cancellation and credit memo are interchangeable.” No. Cancellation reverses the original billing event; a credit memo is a commercial adjustment.
  10. “EBDR means the external system creates the final SAP invoice.” No. The EBDR persists external billing data so S/4HANA Billing can process it and, where possible, combine it with internal billable items.

Assessment questions

  1. Explain order-related versus delivery-related billing. Name the billing reference, the quantity basis, the typical business use, and the process evidence you would check.
  2. A delivery is complete but it does not appear in the billing due list. What do you check? Billing relevance, date, status, block, completion, goods-issue requirement, and copy-control requirement before changing configuration.
  3. Why did two deliveries create two invoices? Compare split-relevant billing-header data and prove the differing field with split analysis.
  4. The invoice price is different from the order price. Where do you start? Check the pricing type in copying control, then determine whether the difference is expected redetermination or a real pricing error.
  5. The billing document exists but there is no accounting document. Why? Check posting block, billing status, account determination, incompleteness, pricing/account-assignment errors, and the FI transfer log/status.
  6. What is the difference between cancellation, credit memo, and invoice correction? Explain reversal of the original event, commercial adjustment, and controlled quantity/price correction.
  7. How do periodic and milestone billing differ? Periodic billing follows recurring dates; milestone billing distributes contract value across planned dates or confirmed project milestones.
  8. How does a down payment move from Sales to FI and back into the final invoice? Billing-plan agreement → down-payment request → FI payment → clearing in partial/final billing.
  9. What problem does EBDR solve? It brings external billable data into S/4HANA Billing so it can enter the due list and be invoiced alone or with internal billing data.
  10. What is a preliminary billing document? A reviewable billing stage before the final billing document, with its own status and a direct effect on the preceding document's billing status.

Order-to-Cash / business event side effects

PGI, Billing, and Payment are three different commit points.

A strong Sales answer should explain not only the document sequence but also what changes in the system when a business event is posted. This is where Sales becomes Inventory, Accounting, Controlling, Credit, Analytics, and Accounts Receivable.

conversion_path

Memory model: PGI = physical + valuation commit → Billing = commercial + receivable commit → Payment = cash + clearing event.

These are different states. Goods can be issued but not yet billed. An invoice can be posted but still be unpaid. A Lead should always name which state has actually been completed.

1. Post Goods Issue — the physical event becomes system truth

Stock and requirements

Inventory quantity is reduced and the delivery requirement is updated in planning. The physical fulfillment event now affects both Inventory Management and the supply-demand picture.

Material valuation

The stock value changes. The accounting impact is based on the material valuation relevant for the posting. In a standard sell-from-stock scenario this is the point where inventory value leaves the balance sheet and the cost-side accounting impact is created.

Accounting and Controlling

The goods movement creates the material and accounting consequences required by the process. Controlling or profitability-related information may also be updated depending on the scenario and configuration.

Billing readiness

For delivery-related billing, PGI normally moves the delivery into the state from which it can become billing due. Think of this as a status transition into the billing worklist rather than as an isolated “list creation” event.

Document flow

Statuses and linked documents are updated. Document flow is therefore not only navigation; it is evidence of how far the process has moved and which downstream object should exist next.

Lead meaning

PGI is a cross-module commit point. After it, we are no longer looking only at an SD delivery: stock, value, accounting, planning, and billing eligibility have all changed.

2. Save Billing Document — the customer charge becomes accounting truth

Receivable and revenue

The invoice becomes a source for Financial Accounting. In the standard case, the customer receivable is debited and revenue is credited, with tax and other configured account postings where applicable.

Controlling / profitability

Billing can update Controlling and profitability analysis. This is why a billing issue can be commercially correct on screen and still fail at the FI/CO handoff.

Analytics

Billing contributes to sales reporting and analytical views. The business event therefore changes both transactional accounting state and reporting state.

Credit exposure

If credit management is used, billing and open receivables can influence the customer's credit situation. A completed invoice can therefore affect future order decisions.

Document status

Related sales, delivery, billing, and accounting status is updated. A green billing document is not enough: the accounting transfer must also be complete.

Invoice combination

Several preceding documents can be combined only when relevant split characteristics are compatible. Payer, billing date, and destination country are useful examples, not the complete split key.

3. Incoming Payment — Finance settles the open item

Ownership changes

Incoming payment is an Accounts Receivable process, not a core Sales execution step. Finance posts the payment against the customer open item.

Typical posting

The cash or bank side is debited and the customer receivable is credited or cleared according to the payment process.

Differences matter

Real payments may not match the invoice exactly. Finance can handle partial payments, residual items, cash discounts, deductions, write-offs, and reason codes according to policy and tolerance settings.

Sales feedback loop

Although payment is owned by Finance, overdue or unpaid receivables can change credit exposure and therefore influence later Sales orders.

Do not collapse these states

PGI

Goods have left

Physical stock and value have changed. This does not prove that the customer has been invoiced.

BILL

Customer has been invoiced

A receivable exists. This does not prove that cash has been received.

PAY

Cash has been received and allocated

The open item can be cleared or reduced. This is a Finance state, not a shipping state.

FLOW

Document flow is process evidence

Use it to prove which events happened and where the chain stopped, but confirm the corresponding logistics and accounting objects when diagnosing.

Assessment drills

  1. What exactly happens at PGI? Do not stop at “stock decreases”. Explain inventory, requirements, valuation/accounting, billing readiness, and document flow.
  2. Why is PGI a stronger boundary than delivery creation? Delivery creation prepares execution; PGI commits the physical stock movement and its financial consequences.
  3. Billing document saved, but FI document is missing. Where is the failure? The Sales document exists, but the accounting handoff is incomplete. Check account determination, posting errors, tax/accounting data, and the release-to-accounting state instead of recreating the delivery.
  4. Can several deliveries become one invoice? Yes, when billing relevance and relevant split characteristics are compatible. Name payer, billing date, and destination country as examples, then say the full split logic is broader.
  5. Does invoice creation mean Order-to-Cash is complete? It can be the last core Sales step, but the receivable is still open until Finance receives and clears payment.

Sales monitoring apps

OBSERVE → TRACE → RESOLVE.

Do not memorize three app names as unrelated tools. They form a useful operational sequence around the same fulfillment process.

F2577 — Observe

Track Sales Orders. Portfolio-level view for customer inquiries and order-status monitoring. It answers where the order is in fulfillment and whether the process has issues.

Sales master data / Multiple Address Handling

IDENTITY → ROLE → ADDRESS → BEHAVIOR.

The feature is easier to explain when we separate the legal customer from the physical location used in one transaction.

Identity

One Business Partner can represent the legal customer instead of creating another customer record for every branch or warehouse.

Role

Sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, and payer are partner functions. The role answers who participates in the process.

Address

The same BP can have several valid addresses, including several valid addresses for the same role such as ship-to.

Behavior

An address can carry address-dependent data such as delivering plant, shipping condition, Incoterms, tax classification, or unloading point. Selecting another address can therefore change process behavior.

Instead of creating several customer masters for one legal entity, we can maintain several BP addresses with usages and validity periods. I would pay special attention to migration, because activation does not consolidate old records automatically and integrations may need the BP address UUID rather than only the customer number or free-text address.

Priority deep dive / Professional Services Billing

Project billing changes what the billable event means.

For services and customer projects, the invoice may be driven by actual resources, an agreed fixed amount, a billing plan, or project milestones. Start by naming the commercial model. Only then explain the SAP mechanics.

45-second Lead answer

Professional Services Billing starts with the contract model. For Time & Material, the billable amount is based on eligible time, expense, material, service, or other project postings. For fixed price, the customer charge follows the agreed contract amount and billing plan rather than actual cost volume. Milestone billing releases agreed percentages or amounts when planned billing dates or project milestones become billable. The project or WBS structure links execution and financial postings to the commercial scope.

I separate two solution families. In modern S/4HANA Professional Services Project Billing, billing elements and project billing requests control preparation before a billing document request is created. In classic resource-related billing, the Dynamic Item Processor selects source expenditure, summarizes it into dynamic items, determines billing materials, and hands the result to SD pricing. The Lead decision is to keep source-cost selection, customer pricing, billing approval, and revenue recognition as separate controls.

Do not collapse these concepts

  • Actual cost is not automatically customer price. Source postings explain consumed resources; Sales pricing can calculate a different customer charge.
  • Billing is not revenue recognition. Customer invoicing and accounting recognition can follow different timing and rules.
  • WBS is not the billing method. It structures project responsibility and account assignment; the contract model still decides how the customer is billed.
  • Classic RRB/DIP and modern Project Billing are not one identical engine. Deployment and process architecture matter.

Billing models

T&M

Time & Material / Time & Expenses

Bill eligible actual postings such as labor, expenses, materials, and services. The design must define which postings are billable, how they are grouped, which rate applies, and what can be postponed or written off.

Lead question: how do we prove that every customer charge can be traced back to an approved source posting?

FIX

Fixed price

Bill the agreed commercial amount according to a billing plan. Actual project cost is still important for margin and project control, but it does not directly define the customer invoice value.

Lead question: what event or date makes each planned amount due, and how are scope changes controlled?

MILE

Milestone billing

Distribute the contract value across billing dates, percentages, or amounts. In classic Project System scenarios, project milestones can control whether milestone-related billing dates are released.

Lead question: who confirms milestone completion and what prevents early billing?

WBS

WBS-based billing

Use the project structure to connect execution, cost and revenue postings, work packages, and customer billing scope. A billing element can be a project or WBS element depending on the solution.

Lead question: is the WBS structure designed for project delivery only, or also for commercial and reporting responsibility?

Classic Resource-Related Billing / DIP

account_tree

Memory path: SOURCE → SELECT → CHARACTERIZE → DETERMINE MATERIAL → AGGREGATE → PRICE → REVIEW → BILL.

The Dynamic Item Processor does not simply copy cost lines into an invoice. The DIP profile controls which source data is relevant and how detailed source information becomes customer-facing billing items.

1. Source expenditure

Actual project, order, service, material, activity, personnel, travel, or other supported expenditure provides the source facts. The first question is whether the posting exists in the expected account-assignment object.

2. Source selection

The DIP profile defines which expenditure is relevant. A posting can exist correctly in CO or the project and still never become billable because it does not match the source selection.

3. Characteristics

Characteristics describe source information used for structuring and summarizing dynamic items. Choose them to preserve the invoice transparency the business needs without creating unnecessary line volume.

4. Material determination

Relevant source information is mapped to a sales material or service representation used by the billing document. Missing or wrong determination can stop billing or produce the wrong customer-facing item.

5. Aggregation

The processor summarizes selected data into dynamic items. Too much aggregation hides traceability; too little aggregation creates noisy invoices and high processing volume.

6. SD pricing

After dynamic items exist, Sales pricing determines the commercial price according to the configured pricing context. Cost selection and customer pricing are related, but they are not the same decision.

7. Billing review

Depending on the scenario, billable quantity or amount can be reviewed and items can be rejected or postponed. The design needs a clear audit trail for what was excluded and why.

8. Billing request and invoice

The reviewed result becomes the relevant billing request and then follows Sales Billing into the final customer document and FI handoff.

DIP configuration model

SRC

Source

Which actual or planned source records are eligible? Define the expenditure types and account-assignment scope intentionally.

CHAR

Characteristics

Which attributes must remain visible for grouping, determination, invoice explanation, and later troubleshooting?

MAT

Material determination

How are source characteristics converted into sales materials or service items that SD can price and bill?

AGG

Structuring and aggregation

What invoice granularity is required by the customer, contract, tax, and internal reconciliation process?

USE

Usage and process context

Use the DIP profile for the intended process. Do not assume a profile built for quotation or one service scenario is automatically correct for another billing flow.

PRICE

Pricing handoff

Define which pricing procedure and conditions produce customer value after dynamic-item creation. Do not hard-code the assumption that cost equals price.

Cross-application / Output Control

OBJECT → TYPE → RECEIVER → CHANNEL → FORM → RELEVANCE.

Output Control is not only a Sales feature. It is a cross-application S/4HANA framework. In a Sales question, first identify whether the document uses SAP S/4HANA Output Management or classic SD output determination.

1. Framework

For Sales_Document, S/4HANA Output Management can be activated in Customizing. A framework switch applies to new documents; old documents keep the framework used when they were created.

2. Decision tables

Output Parameter Determination uses BRFplus rules. Conditions come from document attributes; results define output type, recipient, channel, printer/email settings, form template, and output relevance.

3. Rule order

Rows are checked from top to bottom, so specific rules should come before general rules. Receiver and channel rules can be exclusive when only the first matching result should be used.

4. Processing

If the right parameters are determined but the output still fails, move to rendering and channel processing. Do not debug the mail server before proving the determination.

8 steps

Sales memory

Output Type, Recipient, Channel, Printer Settings, E-Mail Settings, E-Mail Recipient, Form Template, Output Relevance.

Example

Same sales org, different channel

Germany + End Customer can return EMAIL, while Germany + Wholesale can return PRINT. Business attributes are conditions; channel is the result.

Trap

Do not answer “NACE” automatically

Classic output determination and S/4HANA Output Control are different models. First prove which framework owns the document.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: SAP S/4HANA Output Control is a cross-application framework. For Sales I first confirm whether Sales_Document uses the S/4HANA framework or classic SD output determination. In the S/4HANA framework, Output Parameter Determination uses BRFplus decision tables. Document attributes are conditions and the rules return the output type, receiver, channel, channel-specific settings, form template, and output relevance. Rows are ordered from specific to general, and exclusive receiver or channel rules can stop additional matches. I troubleshoot from determination to relevance, form, and only then technical sending or printing.

Sales document control / Copy control

SOURCE → CHECK → TRANSFER → TARGET → STATUS.

Copy control explains how one sales document becomes the next. For an assessment, the important point is not the transaction code. It is the control chain between the predecessor and the follow-on document.

1. Source → target

First prove that the document-type relationship exists. Copy control is structured at header, item, and schedule-line level, so the valid chain can depend on more than the two header document types.

2. Copying requirement

This is the gate. SAP checks whether the reference is allowed before it transfers data. A requirement can reject the copy because of business conditions such as status, validity, customer context, or an already completed reference.

3. Data transfer and switches

Transfer routines decide which fields move or are derived. Switches control specific behavior, for example whether source item numbers are copied into the target.

4. Target categories

At item and schedule-line level, the target category must be valid for the relevant category assignment. If no valid target is entered, SAP can propose one from that assignment.

5. Document flow update

At item level, this setting updates referred quantities or values and therefore supports the reference status of the preceding document.

6. Completion rule

The completion rule belongs to the source item category. It decides when the predecessor item becomes Completed. This is separate from the copy-control switch that updates document flow.

Rule A

First reference can complete

An inquiry item can be completed as soon as a quotation refers to it, even when only part of the quantity is referenced.

Rule B

Full quantity must be referenced

A quotation can stay Partially referred after a partial order. Further orders can still reference the remaining quantity until the full quantity is used.

Reject

Rejection also closes the item

If a reason for rejection is entered, the item receives Completed status.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: I see copy control as a source-to-target contract. I first check the valid source and target relationship, then the copying requirement, transfer routine, switches, and item or schedule-line mapping. If the question is about partial reference or predecessor status, I separate two controls: Document flow update records the reference progress, while the completion rule in the source item category decides when that item is complete.

Ownership map

Keep the handoffs explicit.

Many weak answers are technically correct inside one component and still wrong end to end because ownership silently changed.

Commercial decision

  • Sales process and order behavior
  • Pricing and commercial conditions
  • Credit and tax dependencies
  • Customer-facing document controls

Promise and execution

  • ATP or aATP owns confirmation logic
  • Shipping owns delivery preparation and scheduling
  • EWM can own warehouse execution
  • TM can own transport planning and execution

Financial truth

  • Goods issue changes inventory and accounting impact
  • Billing creates the commercial accounting handoff
  • FI/CO owns journal, clearing, settlement and reconciliation
  • Completion needs business evidence, not only a green document status
