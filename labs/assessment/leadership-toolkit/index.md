      <h1>Lead the work.<br />Do not manage the theatre.</h1>
      <p>A SAP Lead is tested by more than configuration knowledge. We need to make decisions under uncertainty, align people with different incentives, handle conflict, protect delivery, communicate bad news early, and keep ourselves useful when pressure rises.</p>
      <a class="research-canvas__button" href="#field-kit">Open the field kit <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">arrow_downward</span></a>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">construction</span>
    <p><strong>We do not collect management vocabulary.</strong> We use a method when it creates a clearer decision, a safer conversation, a better commitment, or stronger evidence.</p>
    <p><strong>We separate people from the problem.</strong> We can challenge a requirement, behaviour, plan, or decision without turning the conversation into a judgment about the person.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="decisions" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">01 / Decisions</p><h2>Make ownership visible before debating options.</h2><p>Many “technical disagreements” are really authority problems. We first define who drives the analysis, who decides, who contributes, and who only needs the outcome.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Decision methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Use it when</th><th>Working move</th><th>SAP Lead example</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>DACI</strong></td><td>A cross-team decision keeps circling.</td><td>Name one Driver and one Approver. Contributors advise; Informed receive the decision.</td><td>Choosing API, IDoc, or event for a new order integration.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Decision record</strong></td><td>The choice will be challenged later.</td><td>Write context, options, decision, reasons, assumptions, risks, owner, and revisit trigger.</td><td>Why we accepted a temporary side-by-side extension before a clean-core replacement.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Reversible vs. hard-to-reverse</strong></td><td>The team is over-analysing every choice.</td><td>Spend more evidence and senior attention on decisions that are expensive to undo.</td><td>A field label can move quickly; an enterprise BP numbering design cannot.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Pre-mortem</strong></td><td>A plan looks suspiciously clean.</td><td>Assume the initiative failed. Ask what caused it. Convert the strongest causes into controls and owners.</td><td>Before cutover, imagine billing failed for the first business day and trace likely causes.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Lead question:</strong> “Who makes this decision, by when, on which evidence, and what would make us revisit it?”</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="conflict" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">02 / Conflict</p><h2>Do not fight positions. Find the interests behind them.</h2><p>“We need custom code” and “we need clean core” are positions. The useful conversation is about speed, risk, upgrade cost, ownership, controls, and what must be true for each option to work.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Conflict methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Use it when</th><th>Working move</th><th>Failure to avoid</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Interests → options → criteria</strong></td><td>Two stakeholders defend incompatible solutions.</td><td>Write what each side is protecting, create several options, then compare them against agreed criteria.</td><td>Trying to compromise before understanding the real constraints.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>BATNA</strong></td><td>A negotiation has a hard boundary.</td><td>Know what we will do if no agreement is reached. It prevents accepting a worse deal only to end tension.</td><td>Threatening with a weak or imaginary alternative.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Steelman first</strong></td><td>The discussion has become tribal.</td><td>State the strongest version of the other side’s argument before answering it.</td><td>Repeating a caricature and calling it alignment.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Escalate the decision, not the emotion</strong></td><td>The team cannot close the issue at its authority level.</td><td>Escalate options, evidence, trade-offs, recommendation, and deadline.</td><td>Escalating people as “difficult” instead of escalating a blocked decision.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Useful sentence:</strong> “I think we agree on the outcome. We disagree on the risk we are willing to accept. Let us make that risk explicit.”</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="feedback" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">03 / Feedback</p><h2>Describe what happened. Do not diagnose the person.</h2><p>Vague feedback creates defence. Specific feedback creates something we can work with.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Feedback methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Structure</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>SBI / SBII</strong></td><td>Situation → observable Behaviour → Impact → ask about Intent.</td><td>“In today’s design review, when the test concern was cut off twice, we lost the evidence we needed for approval. What were you trying to achieve in that moment?”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Feed-forward</strong></td><td>After the fact is clear, define the next behaviour.</td><td>“For the next review, bring the risk and your proposed mitigation in the first five minutes.”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Positive specificity</strong></td><td>Reinforce behaviours worth repeating, not personality labels.</td><td>“You separated data, config, and interface hypotheses before testing. That shortened the diagnosis.”</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Rule:</strong> difficult feedback should become clearer, not harsher. We can be direct without being personal.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="delegation" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">04 / Delegation</p><h2>Delegate a result and a boundary.</h2><p>If every decision returns to the Lead, we have distributed tasks but kept all ownership. That does not scale.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Delegation methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Level</th><th>What we delegate</th><th>Lead behaviour</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Investigate</strong></td><td>Collect facts and options.</td><td>Lead decides.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Recommend</strong></td><td>Analyse and propose a decision.</td><td>Lead approves or challenges.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Decide within guardrails</strong></td><td>Own the decision inside defined cost, risk, architecture, or timeline limits.</td><td>Lead reviews exceptions, not every step.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Own outcome</strong></td><td>Deliver the result and keep stakeholders aligned.</td><td>Lead checks evidence and system health.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Delegation brief:</strong> outcome, why it matters, decision rights, constraints, evidence of done, check-in point, escalation trigger.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="motivation" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">05 / Motivation</p><h2>Motivation is usually a system problem before it is a speech problem.</h2><p>We look at whether people can make meaningful choices, become better at the work, see why it matters, experience fair treatment, and notice progress.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Motivation levers" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Lever</th><th>Lead action</th><th>Warning signal</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Autonomy</strong></td><td>Give a real decision boundary.</td><td>Every small choice needs permission.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Competence</strong></td><td>Match challenge with support, review, and learning.</td><td>The same person receives only easy work or impossible work.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Purpose</strong></td><td>Connect work to a business outcome.</td><td>The team only hears ticket numbers and deadlines.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Fairness</strong></td><td>Make workload, recognition, and expectations explainable.</td><td>Invisible work always lands on the same people.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Progress</strong></td><td>Make completed outcomes visible.</td><td>Long programmes feel like permanent incompletion.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Before calling someone “unmotivated”:</strong> check clarity, workload, authority, skill, feedback, dependency blocks, recognition, and whether the work still makes sense.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="planning" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">06 / Planning</p><h2>Plan what we know, expose what we assume.</h2><p>A date is not a plan. A useful plan shows deliverables, dependencies, capacity, uncertainty, risk, owners, and the next decisions.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Planning methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Use it for</th><th>Working rule</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Backward planning</strong></td><td>Cutover, assessment, release, milestone.</td><td>Start from the proof of done and work backward through prerequisites.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Critical dependency map</strong></td><td>Cross-team delivery.</td><td>Track the few dependencies that can block the outcome, with owners and needed-by dates.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Assumption log</strong></td><td>Early estimates and architecture work.</td><td>If an estimate depends on something unproven, write it as an assumption with a validation date.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Risk = probability × impact, then action</strong></td><td>Prioritising attention.</td><td>The score is not the work. Every material risk needs mitigation, contingency, owner, and trigger.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Buffer by uncertainty</strong></td><td>Plans with discovery or external dependencies.</td><td>Do not hide uncertainty inside every task. Make contingency visible at the appropriate level.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Assessment signal:</strong> when asked for a plan, we should speak about sequence, dependencies, risk and proof — not only dates.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="reporting" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">07 / Reporting</p><h2>Report for a decision, not for decoration.</h2><p>Senior stakeholders rarely need all activity. They need to know whether the outcome is moving, what changed, what can hurt it, and where they must decide.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Reporting structure" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Block</th><th>Question</th><th>Good evidence</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Outcome</strong></td><td>What moved since the last report?</td><td>Accepted process, passed test, closed defect class, completed migration object.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Forecast</strong></td><td>What do we expect now?</td><td>Date or range with confidence and assumptions.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Risk</strong></td><td>What could change the forecast?</td><td>Dependency, defect trend, decision delay, capacity, data quality.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Decision needed</strong></td><td>What must someone decide?</td><td>Options, recommendation, consequence of delay, decision date.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Evidence</strong></td><td>Why should the reader trust the status?</td><td>Test result, reconciliation, throughput, defect ageing, signed decision.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Avoid:</strong> “80% complete” without a measurable definition of complete. Prefer evidence that survives a challenge.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="meetings" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">08 / Meetings</p><h2>A meeting should earn the time it consumes.</h2><p>Use synchronous time for disagreement, decisions, problem solving, coordination, and difficult human conversations. Move simple status transfer into written updates.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Meeting methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Pattern</th><th>How we run it</th><th>Output</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Decision meeting</strong></td><td>Send context and options before the call. Start with the decision question.</td><td>Decision, owner, conditions, actions.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Working session</strong></td><td>Bring the artefact: process, design, test evidence, data, or incident timeline.</td><td>Changed artefact, not just discussion notes.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Daily coordination</strong></td><td>Focus on blockers, dependencies, and next handoffs.</td><td>Re-plan where needed.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>1:1</strong></td><td>Use the time for context, feedback, development, load, and concerns that do not belong in a group meeting.</td><td>Clarity and follow-up, not surveillance.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Meeting test:</strong> if nobody can name the decision, artefact, or coordination problem, we probably do not need the meeting.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="self" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">09 / Self-management</p><h2>Protect judgment before protecting busyness.</h2><p>A Lead becomes a bottleneck when every message feels urgent and every problem receives immediate personal attention.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Self-management methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Practical use</th><th>Lead question</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>Control / influence / observe</strong></td><td>Separate what we can change now, what we can influence, and what we only need to monitor.</td><td>“What is the next action inside our control?”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>WIP limit</strong></td><td>Limit simultaneous deep problems. Finish or delegate before opening another.</td><td>“Which current commitment should stop if this becomes priority?”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Timebox analysis</strong></td><td>Set a point when investigation must produce either evidence, escalation, or a new hypothesis.</td><td>“What will we know after 60 minutes that we do not know now?”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Decision journal</strong></td><td>Record important assumptions and expected outcomes.</td><td>“Was the decision bad, or did an uncertain outcome go against us?”</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Recovery margin</strong></td><td>Keep some unscheduled capacity for incidents, reviews, and hard conversations.</td><td>“If the week is already at 100%, where will real problems go?”</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="learning" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">10 / Learning from failure</p><h2>Fix the system without removing accountability.</h2><p>Blame hides information. No accountability hides repeated behaviour. We need both a safe investigation and clear ownership for improvements.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Learning methods" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>Questions</th><th>Output</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td><strong>After Action Review</strong></td><td>What did we expect? What happened? Why was there a gap? What will we change?</td><td>Small set of owned improvements.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>5 Whys with evidence</strong></td><td>Why did this condition exist? What evidence supports the next “why”?</td><td>Contributing causes, not a convenient single culprit.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Countermeasure test</strong></td><td>Would this action have prevented or detected the failure earlier?</td><td>Controls tied to the failure mechanism.</td></tr>
          <tr><td><strong>Reusable memory</strong></td><td>What should the next team know without rediscovering it?</td><td>Runbook, check, design rule, monitoring signal, or decision record.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__method" data-reveal>
    <div><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Pressure model</p><h2>When the room gets difficult, use five moves.</h2></div>
    <ol>
      <li><span>01</span><strong>State the outcome</strong><p>What are we trying to protect or achieve?</p></li>
      <li><span>02</span><strong>Separate facts from assumptions</strong><p>What do we know, and what are we currently guessing?</p></li>
      <li><span>03</span><strong>Name the decision</strong><p>What exactly must be decided now, later, or not at all?</p></li>
      <li><span>04</span><strong>Make the trade-off explicit</strong><p>Cost, risk, time, scope, architecture, operations, and reversibility.</p></li>
      <li><span>05</span><strong>Close with ownership</strong><p>Owner, next action, deadline, evidence, escalation trigger.</p></li>
    </ol>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Assessment drills</p><h2>Practice judgement, not definitions.</h2><p>Answer aloud in two minutes. Then challenge your own answer.</p></header>
    <div class="assessment-practice-table" role="region" aria-label="Leadership assessment questions" tabindex="0">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th>Situation</th><th>What a Lead answer should expose</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><td>Business demands a custom pricing enhancement; architecture rejects it.</td><td>Interests, decision owner, options, criteria, clean-core boundary, time pressure, reversible interim path.</td></tr>
          <tr><td>A strong consultant repeatedly interrupts junior team members.</td><td>Specific feedback, impact on team quality, intent inquiry, expected behaviour, follow-up.</td></tr>
          <tr><td>A workstream is “green” but three critical dependencies have no owners.</td><td>Status evidence, dependency map, ownership, forecast impact, escalation before the date is missed.</td></tr>
          <tr><td>A team member asks the Lead to approve every configuration choice.</td><td>Delegation level, guardrails, decision rights, review cadence, capability building.</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Go-live is in two weeks and defect closure is slowing.</td><td>Risk-based triage, trend, business severity, decision thresholds, contingency, honest forecast.</td></tr>
          <tr><td>Two teams blame each other for a failed interface.</td><td>Shared business outcome, timeline and evidence, ownership boundaries, first wrong state, recovery and prevention.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>
