  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="target">
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Choose the right target</p><h2>C_S4CS is the target; not every SD page is its syllabus.</h2></header>
    <p>The <a href="https://www.credly.com/org/sap/badge/sap-certified-implementation-consultant-sap-s-4hana.4">SAP-issued certification badge</a> identifies the exam as <strong>C_S4CS</strong>. The <a href="https://learning.sap.com/learning-journeys/implementing-sap-s4hana-cloud-public-edition-sales">official Sales Learning Journey</a> is the starting point for preparation. Open the current certification offering through SAP Learning before booking to confirm its exact title, assessment format, language, version and requirements. This page does not invent a release suffix, question count, pass mark or guaranteed exam format.</p>
    <p><strong>Keep the editions separate.</strong> SAP identifies <strong>C_TS462</strong> in its <a href="https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-s-4hana-cloud-private-edition-sales">Private Edition Sales certification-maintenance course</a>. That is a different target. Our classic SD material is useful for understanding mechanisms, but a transaction, configuration path or process variant described there must not automatically be treated as available in Public Edition.</p>
    <p><strong>Preparation is not renewal.</strong> The <a href="https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-s-4hana-cloud-public-edition-sales">Public Edition Sales Stay Certified course</a> is for maintaining an existing certification; it is not a replacement for first-time preparation. Likewise, a collection number in the <a href="https://learning.sap.com/helpcenter/learninghub-subscription/practice-systems">practice-system maintenance schedule</a> identifies a learning environment, not by itself an exam version.</p>
    <p>Use three reading priorities throughout this map: <strong>Core</strong> means a topic in the official Sales learning route; <strong>Support</strong> means knowledge needed to implement or explain it; <strong>Depth</strong> means broader SD, troubleshooting or architecture material. A page being in the library does not make it an exam requirement.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="method">
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Study method</p><h2>Make each reading session produce something observable.</h2></header>
    <p>For a new topic, first read the relevant official lesson and the linked explanation. Close both. Draw the document flow or determination chain, work one example, then explain it aloud. Reopen the source only to check a specific uncertainty. Reading recognition is not the same as being able to reconstruct the process.</p>
    <p>Use a private study log with five fields: <strong>topic; explanation in my words; example or system evidence; mistake or uncertainty; next check</strong>. Do not put client data, credentials, customer screenshots or exam content in this public repository. The <a href="/labs/assessment/progress/">assessment progress workspace</a> is another available study entry point; its score is not an SAP exam result.</p>
    <p>Rate a topic <strong>0</strong> when unfamiliar, <strong>1</strong> when recognizable while reading, <strong>2</strong> when explainable with prompts, and <strong>3</strong> when you can independently explain, apply and handle a changed example. Revisit weak topics in a later session without the article open. This is a self-assessment convention, not an official pass threshold.</p>
    <p>Without access to a training system, do the document and data exercises on paper, but mark them <strong>traced, not executed</strong>. Complete the available official hands-on exercises when system access is available. A paper walkthrough must not be recorded as successful system execution.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="capstone">
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Original study exercise</p><h2>One order, two confirmations, a billing step and a return.</h2></header>
    <p>This is an original learning case, <strong>not an SAP exam question</strong>. The numbers and process choices below are explicit exercise assumptions, not SAP defaults. Use a training system only; do not change production data.</p>
    <p>A customer orders <strong>100 units at EUR 30 each</strong>. Assume a valid <strong>5% item discount</strong>, no tax or freight, ATP confirmations of <strong>60 units now and 40 next week</strong>, partial deliveries allowed, and delivery-related billing after the relevant execution steps. The ship-to and payer are different business partners from the sold-to. After the first 60 units are delivered and invoiced, the customer returns 5. For the arithmetic exercise only, assume an approved credit at the original net unit price.</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Model it:</strong> draw the order, its confirmation/schedule information, the delivery and billing references, and the return/credit path. Mark where the data for the different partner roles comes from.</li>
      <li><strong>Calculate it:</strong> show the gross value, discount base, net order value, value of the first and later quantities, and the assumed credit for 5 units. Keep quantity, price and value distinct.</li>
      <li><strong>Execute or trace it:</strong> record the actual reference documents and relevant statuses. State which steps you executed and which you only traced. Record the source or configuration assumptions behind your expectation.</li>
      <li><strong>Change it:</strong> remove the discount, add a delivery block, or introduce a partner difference that might affect invoice grouping. For each change, identify the first evidence you need; do not prescribe an unconditional unblock or repricing.</li>
      <li><strong>Explain it:</strong> give a three-minute walkthrough to someone who knows the business but not SAP. Then answer: “What would change for a third-party sale, a free-of-charge replacement or a financial correction without a physical return?”</li>
    </ol>
    <details>
      <summary>Worked arithmetic and expected reasoning</summary>
      <p><strong>Under the stated assumptions:</strong> gross order value = 100 × 30 = EUR 3,000; discount = EUR 150; net order value = EUR 2,850; net unit price = EUR 28.50. The first 60 units represent EUR 1,710 and the later 40 EUR 1,140. The assumed credit for 5 units is EUR 142.50. These are calculation checks, not a claim that the system will create those follow-on documents automatically.</p>
      <p>A good explanation keeps the two confirmations distinct from the eventual delivery documents, uses the chosen billing reference, and separates physical return processing from the financial correction. It checks accounting-transfer status rather than concluding that the presence of a billing document proves the entire process is financially complete.</p>
      <p>For a missing discount, use the <a href="/labs/enterprise-context/pricing/casebook/">pricing casebook</a> to trace the selected procedure and condition search/result before changing records. For a block, use <a href="/atlas/diagnostics/sap-delivery-block-analysis/">block analysis</a> to establish the reason and authorized owner. For unexpected invoice grouping, compare the relevant documents and <a href="/atlas/diagnostics/sap-invoice-split-analysis/">split evidence</a>; a partner difference is a hypothesis to inspect, not a universal explanation.</p>
      <p><strong>Evidence to keep:</strong> your flow drawing, the assumptions, calculations, sanitized test observations, one failed expectation and its explanation, and the source used to resolve it. You are not finished when the numbers match but the document relationships remain unclear.</p>
    </details>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="readiness">
    <header><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Readiness check</p><h2>Finish with evidence, not a page count.</h2></header>
    <p>As a personal study gate, aim for level 3 on the core topics: explain without notes, work the example, then explain what changes in a variant. An unresolved application or edition gap remains open even when you recognize the terminology. This gate does not predict an exam score or guarantee a pass.</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Explain:</strong> give a short, accurate account of the complete Sales flow, its organizational/master-data prerequisites, the controlling document decisions and the main variants.</li>
      <li><strong>Apply:</strong> complete the available official exercises and the original case above, retaining the distinction between system execution and a paper trace.</li>
      <li><strong>Diagnose:</strong> analyze a missing price, failed confirmation, blocked delivery, billing problem and complaint without jumping straight to a configuration change.</li>
      <li><strong>Respect scope:</strong> identify the named edition and check uncertainty in official documentation. Know which advanced material is useful background rather than an assumed Public Edition capability.</li>
      <li><strong>Prepare the attempt:</strong> reopen the current certification offering and its stated assessment requirements; confirm access, language and environment, and review your remaining errors. Do not substitute remembered historic exam rules.</li>
    </ol>
    <details>
      <summary>Explain-back questions for a final oral review</summary>
      <p>Use these as prompts, not as an alleged exam-question bank. A strong answer names the mechanism, follows a small example, states a boundary and identifies evidence.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>What does the sales order commit us to, and what is still determined or executed later?</li>
        <li>Which data and decisions explain this item's delivery and billing behavior?</li>
        <li>How was this price assembled, and where would you look when one condition is missing?</li>
        <li>How do requested dates, confirmations and actual shipping steps relate?</li>
        <li>What makes a document billable, and how would you investigate an unexpected invoice split?</li>
        <li>How would you distinguish incompleteness, a delivery block, a credit block and an output problem?</li>
        <li>What changes in third-party and intercompany Sales compared with Sell from Stock?</li>
        <li>When does a complaint need a goods movement, a financial correction, or both?</li>
        <li>Which part of a Sales implementation requires configuration, master data, authorization, integration or an extension?</li>
        <li>What action should follow a fulfillment alert, a planning result or an AI-proposed order entry?</li>
      </ul>
      <p>For each weak answer, return to its numbered stage, not to the top of the entire library. The goal is a coherent model of Sales that you can use and explain, with the official route providing the certification boundary.</p>
    </details>
