      <h1>Same pattern.<br />Different operating reality.</h1>
      <p>Many AI workflow patterns repeat across industries. Teams review documents, prepare summaries, answer questions, investigate issues, make decisions, and coordinate work. The pattern may repeat. The conditions around the work often do not.</p>
      <a class="research-canvas__button" href="#vertical-evidence">Read the five evidence areas <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">arrow_downward</span></a>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">domain</span>
    <p><strong>Vertical solution application</strong> is the process of adapting a recommendation to the customer's real operating environment.</p>
    <p><strong>Vertical context</strong> is the set of industry-specific realities that shape an AI opportunity: workflows, stakeholders, risks, governance expectations, and success metrics.</p>
    <p>You do not need to become an industry expert before making progress. You need enough context to make a credible recommendation, avoid unsupported assumptions, and identify what still needs validation.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="vertical-evidence" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Five evidence areas</p>
      <h2>Read the operating context before adapting the route.</h2>
      <p>An industry label is only a starting clue. Use five evidence areas to understand what the label means for this customer and this workflow.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Evidence area</th><th scope="col">What it means</th><th scope="col">Why it matters</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Workflow</th><td>The process, handoffs, systems, decisions, exceptions, and outputs the customer relies on.</td><td>Keeps the recommendation grounded in real work instead of an industry label.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Stakeholders</th><td>The people who sponsor, use, approve, govern, support, or operate the solution.</td><td>Shows who cares, who decides, who can block progress, and who must validate the opportunity.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Risk</th><td>Business, operational, legal, regulatory, data, safety, or reputational concerns that may change the acceptable design.</td><td>Shows what cannot be assumed and where stronger evidence or specialist review may be required.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Governance</th><td>Approval steps, oversight, permissions, auditability, escalation, and operating controls.</td><td>Helps determine what must be validated before a route can move into delivery or handoff.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Success metrics</th><td>The outcomes the customer will use to judge whether the workflow improved.</td><td>Connects the opportunity to value and prevents a technically successful pilot from being mistaken for a useful business result.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="context-effects" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Recommendation effects</p>
      <h2>Use context to change the decision, not decorate the story.</h2>
      <p>Vertical context matters only when it changes what you ask, what you validate, how you describe value, or which route can be defended.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Decision area</th><th scope="col">Vertical question</th><th scope="col">Possible effect</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Discovery</th><td>Which workflow steps, decision rights, exceptions, or controls are specific to this operating environment?</td><td>Changes who should join discovery and which evidence needs to be collected.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Readiness</th><td>Which approvals, data conditions, controls, or operating owners must exist before the customer can move?</td><td>May make an otherwise plausible route premature.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Value</th><td>Which operational or business outcome matters most in this environment?</td><td>Changes the value emphasis and the metric used to test the hypothesis.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Solution fit</th><td>Does the proposed surface, capability, authority level, integration model, and deployment approach fit the operating environment?</td><td>May strengthen one route, weaken another, or require a combined solution.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Controls and handoff</th><td>Which specialist review or governance evidence is required before implementation?</td><td>Determines whether discovery can continue, a validation session is needed, or the opportunity should move to a technical, security, legal, data, or deployment owner.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="same-pattern-different-context" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Pattern versus context</p>
      <h2>Do not route from the industry name.</h2>
      <p>Industry names hide variation. Two organizations in the same sector can have different systems, approval models, data boundaries, operating risks, and definitions of success.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="ecg-decision-columns">
      <div>
        <h3>Same workflow pattern</h3>
        <p>Document review, case investigation, summarization, recommendation, and workflow coordination appear in many organizations. The AI job can look similar while the surrounding controls and operating model are very different.</p>
        <p>A document-review use case may require different reviewers, approved sources, approval steps, retention rules, escalation paths, or success measures depending on the customer.</p>
      </div>
      <div>
        <h3>Different recommendation</h3>
        <p><em>“Healthcare,” “banking,” “manufacturing,”</em> or <em>“public sector”</em> is not enough context to select a solution. Ask which workflow is involved, who owns it, which controls matter, what data is used, and how success is judged.</p>
        <p>If an industry-specific requirement is unknown, record it as a validation gap rather than filling the gap with confidence.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="vertical-discovery" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Vertical discovery</p>
      <h2>Ask enough to understand the environment.</h2>
      <p>The goal is not a complete industry study. The goal is to identify the conditions that could change fit, readiness, value, controls, or the next owner.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Context question</th><th scope="col">Evidence to capture</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">How does this workflow actually run here?</th><td>Inputs, systems, handoffs, decisions, exceptions, approvals, and outputs.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Who has decision rights?</th><td>Users, process owner, approvers, control owners, technical owners, and executive sponsor.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">What makes an error material?</th><td>Operational impact, financial exposure, customer impact, compliance concern, safety issue, or reputational effect.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Which controls cannot disappear?</th><td>Permissions, segregation of duties, human review, audit evidence, escalation, rollback, or another required safeguard.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Which data can be used?</th><td>Source systems, sensitivity, permissions, retention expectations, quality, and access constraints.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">How will the customer judge success?</th><td>Cycle time, quality, risk, cost, throughput, service, user effort, or another agreed outcome.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">What is still unknown?</th><td>Explicit proof gaps with a next validation owner rather than inferred requirements.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="vertical-handoff" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Lead handoff</p>
      <h2>Carry vertical evidence into the next decision.</h2>
      <p>Do not hand off only the industry and product labels. Pass the operating conditions that made the recommendation credible.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="research-canvas__table-wrap">
      <table>
        <thead><tr><th scope="col">Capture</th><th scope="col">What the next owner needs</th></tr></thead>
        <tbody>
          <tr><th scope="row">Workflow reality</th><td>Where the AI capability sits in the real process, including systems, handoffs, exceptions, and approvals.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Stakeholder map</th><td>Who uses, owns, approves, governs, sponsors, or operates the solution.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Material risks</th><td>Which risks could change scope, authority, deployment, validation, or the decision to proceed.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Required controls</th><td>Which permissions, review, audit, escalation, or operating controls must remain visible.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Success evidence</th><td>Which metric or observable outcome should improve and what baseline or validation evidence is needed.</td></tr>
          <tr><th scope="row">Proof gaps</th><td>Which vertical assumptions are still unproven, who should validate them, and what decision depends on the answer.</td></tr>
        </tbody>
      </table>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__method" data-reveal>
    <div><p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Lead vertical check</p><h2>Five questions before adapting the recommendation.</h2></div>
    <ol>
      <li><span>01</span><strong>Workflow</strong><p>What is different about how this customer performs the work?</p></li>
      <li><span>02</span><strong>Stakeholders</strong><p>Who uses, owns, approves, governs, and operates the workflow?</p></li>
      <li><span>03</span><strong>Risk and governance</strong><p>Which errors, permissions, approvals, or audit needs could change the acceptable route?</p></li>
      <li><span>04</span><strong>Success</strong><p>Which outcome matters here, and how would the customer know the workflow improved?</p></li>
      <li><span>05</span><strong>Gap</strong><p>Which vertical assumption still needs evidence, and who is the right person to validate it?</p></li>
    </ol>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">rule</span>
    <p><strong>Vertical application rule:</strong> adapt the questions and recommendation to the customer's environment, but do not invent industry facts. Use known context to improve the route and turn unknown context into explicit validation work.</p>
