    <p><strong>Important:</strong> ISO develops international standards. ISO does not certify organizations and does not issue ISO certificates. Certification is performed by independent certification bodies; accreditation gives additional confidence that a certification body is competent to perform that work.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="iso-map" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Enterprise map</p>
      <h2>One standard, one main management question.</h2>
      <p>Do not memorize a list of numbers. Connect each standard to the enterprise risk it is designed to manage.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">9001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Quality management</p>
          <h3>ISO 9001:2015 — can the organization manage quality consistently?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> quality management, customer focus, process discipline, corrective action, performance review, and continual improvement across a supplier or service organization.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> supplier qualification, managed-service governance, delivery organizations, implementation partners, manufacturing and service operations.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> that one SAP configuration is correct, one cloud service is secure, or one release is defect-free.</p>
          <p><strong>Version check:</strong> ISO 9001:2008 is withdrawn. ISO 9001:2015 is still the current published edition on 28 August 2026. ISO is preparing the next edition, expected in September 2026.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Information security</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — is information security managed as a system?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> information-security governance, risk treatment, security objectives, controls, review, internal audit, corrective action, and continual improvement.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> cloud vendors, SAP hosting, BTP services, integration platforms, SaaS suppliers, managed services, and organizations that handle sensitive enterprise data.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> that every application, role design, interface, customer tenant, or custom extension is secure.</p>
          <p><strong>Ask for:</strong> certificate scope and, where available and appropriate, the Statement of Applicability or other evidence that explains which controls are applicable to the certified ISMS.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">22301</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Business continuity</p>
          <h3>ISO 22301:2019 — can the organization prepare for disruption and recover?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> continuity governance, impact analysis, response planning, recovery capability, exercises, review, and improvement.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> critical cloud services, SAP managed services, shared service centers, outsourced operations, and providers supporting time-critical business processes.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> your project-specific RTO, RPO, failover design, recovery sequence, or the recovery of customer-side integrations. Those must still be designed and tested.</p>
          <p><strong>Version note:</strong> the 2019 edition remains current on 28 August 2026, while revision work is underway.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">20000</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">IT service management</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 — can IT services be planned, operated, measured, and improved?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> service management systems, service planning, transition, delivery, supplier management, measurement, and improvement.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> AMS, managed cloud operations, service desks, infrastructure operations, and outsourced IT services.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> that one incident will meet your SLA or that a specific support team has the skills your SAP landscape needs. Check the service contract, operating model, staffing, KPIs, and evidence.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27701</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Privacy information management</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 27701:2025 — is privacy managed systematically?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> organizations acting as PII controllers or processors and needing a structured privacy information management system.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> HR systems, customer platforms, SaaS, cloud services, analytics, support services, and integrations carrying personal data.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> GDPR compliance for your complete business process. Legal role, lawful basis, retention, residency, data-subject rights, contracts, and actual data flows still matter.</p>
          <p><strong>Version check:</strong> the 2025 edition replaced the 2019 edition and is now a standalone management-system standard.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">42001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">AI management</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — is AI governed through a management system?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> organizations that develop, provide, or use AI and need defined responsibilities, risk management, lifecycle controls, monitoring, review, and continual improvement.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> AI providers, enterprise AI platforms, SAP Business AI scenarios, agents, document intelligence, predictive services, and internal AI operating models.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> that one model answer is correct, one use case is safe, or one deployment satisfies every part of the EU AI Act. Use-case risk, data, evaluation, human oversight, access, logging, and regulation remain separate work.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">37301</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Compliance management</p>
          <h3>ISO 37301:2021 — can the organization manage compliance obligations systematically?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> governance of compliance obligations, responsibilities, controls, monitoring, reporting, investigation, and improvement.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> regulated organizations, complex supplier networks, global operations, and programs where many legal and internal obligations must be tracked.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> compliance with every law. A management system helps organize compliance work; legal applicability and evidence still need to be checked obligation by obligation.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">55001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Asset management</p>
          <h3>ISO 55001:2024 — are assets managed to deliver value across their lifecycle?</h3>
          <p><strong>Useful for:</strong> asset-management systems, lifecycle value, risk, performance, governance, and decision-making.</p>
          <p><strong>Enterprise use:</strong> utilities, manufacturing, transport, infrastructure, maintenance organizations, EAM environments, and SAP Asset Management programs.</p>
          <p><strong>Does not prove:</strong> that SAP master data, maintenance plans, work orders, or asset accounting are correctly designed. It provides a management-system frame, not SAP configuration evidence.</p>
        </div>
      </article>
    </div>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Certificate reading</p>
      <h2>Eight checks before you accept “ISO certified”.</h2>
      <p>A certificate is useful only when you can connect it to the service and risk under review.</p>
    </header>
    <ol class="research-canvas__steps">
      <li><span>01</span><strong>Standard and edition</strong><p>Read the full reference: for example, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 rather than simply “ISO 27001”.</p></li>
      <li><span>02</span><strong>Certified legal entity</strong><p>Confirm that the entity on the certificate is the entity providing or operating the service you buy.</p></li>
      <li><span>03</span><strong>Scope statement</strong><p>Check products, services, operations, sites, organizational units, and boundaries included in certification.</p></li>
      <li><span>04</span><strong>Locations and delivery model</strong><p>Do not assume one certificate covers every data center, country, affiliate, subcontractor, or cloud region.</p></li>
      <li><span>05</span><strong>Certification body</strong><p>Identify who issued the certificate and, where relevant, whether the body is accredited for that certification activity.</p></li>
      <li><span>06</span><strong>Validity</strong><p>Check issue date, expiry date, surveillance status, and whether the certificate can be validated through the issuer or accreditation ecosystem.</p></li>
      <li><span>07</span><strong>Exclusions and shared responsibilities</strong><p>Record what is outside the certified boundary and which controls remain with the customer or another provider.</p></li>
      <li><span>08</span><strong>Decision fit</strong><p>Ask whether management-system certification is enough evidence or whether you also need an assurance report, technical test, contract, resilience evidence, or regulatory assessment.</p></li>
    </ol>
  </section>

    <p><strong>Assessment correction:</strong> if someone says “ISO 9001:2008”, do not repeat it as current. Say that the 2008 edition is withdrawn, identify the current edition, and check whether a new edition or transition period affects the evidence being reviewed.</p>
