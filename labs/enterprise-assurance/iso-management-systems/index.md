      <h1>A certificate describes a management system, not your whole risk.</h1>
      <p>ISO management-system certification can be strong evidence that an organization has defined and audited ways of managing a particular area. The useful question is not simply “is the vendor ISO certified?” It is which management system was assessed, whose operations are in scope, and whether that scope matches the service and risk we care about.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">fact_check</span>
    <p><strong>Start with the boundary:</strong> ISO develops the standards but does not certify organizations. Independent certification bodies perform certification. A certificate therefore needs to be read together with its standard, edition, certified organization, scope and validity.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="management-systems" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">The useful map</p>
      <h2>Different standards answer different management questions.</h2>
      <p>They are related because they use a management-system approach, but they are not interchangeable. Information-security certification does not prove service quality; business-continuity certification does not prove a specific recovery time; AI-management certification does not prove that one model response is correct.</p>
    </header>

    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">9001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Quality management</p>
          <h3>ISO 9001:2026 — how does the organization manage quality?</h3>
          <p>The new sixth edition was published on 16 September 2026. It defines requirements for establishing, operating and continually improving a quality management system. In vendor assurance, it can support confidence in process discipline, customer focus, performance review and corrective improvement.</p>
          <p>It does not establish that a particular SAP design is correct, that a release is defect-free, or that a cloud service meets your security requirements.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Information security</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — how is information-security risk governed?</h3>
          <p>An ISMS connects security risk assessment, treatment, objectives, controls, monitoring, internal audit and improvement. This makes the standard relevant to cloud providers, managed services, SaaS, hosting and integration services that handle enterprise information.</p>
          <p>The certificate boundary still matters. It does not automatically cover every product, tenant, data center, interface, custom extension or customer-side authorization design.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">22301</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Business continuity</p>
          <h3>ISO 22301:2019 — how does the organization prepare for disruption?</h3>
          <p>ISO 22301 defines requirements for a business continuity management system. The 2019 edition remains the published standard, although ISO has started work on its revision. For a critical SAP or cloud service, this is useful evidence about the provider's continuity discipline.</p>
          <p>It is not a substitute for checking your contractual availability, RTO and RPO commitments, recovery architecture, dependency chain, exercises and customer-side recovery steps.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">20000</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">IT service management</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 — how are IT services managed?</h3>
          <p>The standard covers requirements for a service management system, including planning, transition, delivery and continual improvement of services. It is therefore relevant to AMS, service desks, managed cloud operations and outsourced IT services.</p>
          <p>Certification does not tell us whether one support team has the SAP skills we need or whether a particular incident will meet its SLA. Those questions belong to the service contract, operating model, staffing, measurements and actual delivery evidence.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27701</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Privacy</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 27701:2025 — how is privacy information managed?</h3>
          <p>The 2025 edition is an independent privacy information management system standard for organizations that collect, process, store or control personally identifiable information. It can complement security evidence when an HR, customer, SaaS, analytics or integration service handles personal data.</p>
          <p>It does not by itself establish legal compliance for an end-to-end business process. Controller and processor roles, lawful basis, retention, transfers, contracts, data-subject rights and the actual data flow still need their own analysis.</p>
        </div>
      </article>

      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">42001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy">
          <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">AI management</p>
          <h3>ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — how does the organization govern AI?</h3>
          <p>ISO/IEC 42001 specifies requirements for an AI management system for organizations that develop, provide or use AI-based products and services. It gives us evidence about organizational governance of AI risk and opportunity rather than a quality score for one model.</p>
          <p>For an enterprise AI use case we still need architecture, data, access, evaluation, human-oversight, logging, incident and regulatory evidence at the use-case level.</p>
        </div>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Reading a certificate</p>
      <h2>The certificate becomes useful when its boundary matches the decision.</h2>
      <p>We can usually reduce the review to four questions rather than treating the certificate as a pass/fail badge.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-memory-grid">
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>WHAT</span><strong>Which standard and edition?</strong><h3>Confirm the exact management system that was assessed.</h3><p>Edition matters when standards change. For example, ISO 9001:2026 replaced the 2015 edition in September 2026; a migration period may still affect existing certificates, so the certificate's own validity and transition status must be checked rather than guessed.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>WHO</span><strong>Which organization?</strong><h3>Match the certified legal entity to the organization delivering or operating the service.</h3><p>A certificate for a parent company or another affiliate is not automatically evidence for the entity in your contract.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>WHERE</span><strong>What is inside the scope?</strong><h3>Read the scope statement, sites, services and organizational boundary.</h3><p>Do not infer coverage for every region, data center, product or subcontractor from the vendor brand alone.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>SO WHAT</span><strong>What remains unproven?</strong><h3>Connect management-system evidence to the concrete enterprise decision.</h3><p>A critical service may also need a SOC or ISAE report, resilience tests, technical security evidence, contractual commitments, regulatory evidence or customer-control testing.</p></article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">cloud</span>
    <p><strong>Cloud-specific standards belong one layer deeper.</strong> ISO/IEC 27017:2026 adds cloud-security control guidance and ISO/IEC 27018:2025 addresses PII protection in public cloud services acting as PII processors. They are covered in the <a href="/labs/enterprise-assurance/cloud-industry-assurance/">Cloud and Industry Assurance</a> page so this guide can stay focused on management systems.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Enterprise use</p>
      <h2>Choose evidence from the risk, not from the vendor's certificate list.</h2>
    </header>
    <p>A managed SAP service may need quality and service-management evidence, an SAP cloud service may need information-security and continuity evidence, and an AI service may add AI-management evidence. None of those combinations is automatic. The service boundary, data, business criticality, regulatory context and customer responsibilities determine what evidence is proportionate.</p>
    <p>That is why ISO certification is most useful as one part of an assurance chain. The next step is to connect it to the exact provider service and to the controls that remain with us.</p>
