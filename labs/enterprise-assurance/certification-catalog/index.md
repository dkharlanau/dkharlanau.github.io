      <h1>Know the certificate.<br />Then find the system impact.</h1>
      <p>Certifications matter when they change how an enterprise controls quality, data, suppliers, production, safety, continuity, security, or evidence. This catalog connects the certificate name to the business question and to the ERP or platform capabilities that usually carry part of the evidence.</p>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">rule</span>
    <p><strong>Important boundary:</strong> a company can be certified to a management-system standard. That does not mean every SAP process, cloud tenant, interface, plant, legal entity, product, or supplier is automatically inside the certificate scope. Always read the certificate scope statement.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="catalog" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">General enterprise management</p>
      <h2>Standards that shape how the organization operates.</h2>
      <p>These are broad management-system certifications. They often create requirements for workflows, evidence, ownership, corrective action, measurement and management review.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">9001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 9001:2015 — Quality management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> can the organization deliver products and services through a controlled, repeatable quality system?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> quality records, complaints, nonconformities, CAPA, supplier performance, approvals, document control, KPIs and management review.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> ISO 9001:2015 remains the current published edition on 28 August 2026. ISO is preparing the next edition for publication in September 2026. ISO 9001:2008 is withdrawn.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">14001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 14001:2026 — Environmental management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> how does the organization identify, control and improve its environmental impacts?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> waste, emissions, materials, legal obligations, environmental incidents, targets, supplier requirements, measurements and evidence. SAP EHS, procurement, production, maintenance and sustainability data can all become relevant.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> ISO 14001:2026 was published in April 2026 and replaced ISO 14001:2015.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">45001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 45001:2018 — Occupational health and safety</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> how does the organization control OH&amp;S risks and improve workplace safety?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> incidents, hazards, training, contractor qualification, permits, maintenance, corrective actions and workforce records.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> ISO 45001:2018 remains current; a second edition is under development.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">50001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 50001:2018 — Energy management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> can the organization systematically improve energy performance?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> meters, energy baselines, consumption, production volumes, facilities, equipment efficiency, procurement and performance indicators.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">55001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 55001:2024 — Asset management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> are physical and other assets managed through lifecycle value, risk, performance and cost?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> asset master data, maintenance, investment, criticality, lifecycle cost, work management and reliability. This connects directly to enterprise asset management and SAP PM/EAM scenarios.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">37001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 37001:2025 — Anti-bribery management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> does the organization have controls to prevent, detect and respond to bribery?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> third-party due diligence, supplier onboarding, approvals, financial and non-financial controls, gifts, conflicts, investigations and audit trails.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">37301</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 37301:2021 — Compliance management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> how does the organization identify and manage legal, regulatory and ethical obligations?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> obligation registers, controls, approvals, policy evidence, training, monitoring, issues and remediation. The standard was reviewed and confirmed in 2026.</p></div></article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Digital trust</p>
      <h2>Security, privacy, AI, continuity and service management.</h2>
      <p>These certifications are especially common in SaaS, cloud, outsourcing, AMS, platform and AI-provider due diligence.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Information security management</h3><p><strong>System impact:</strong> risk management, access, assets, suppliers, incidents, security controls, governance and continual improvement. Use the Statement of Applicability and certificate scope to understand what is actually covered.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">27701</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO/IEC 27701:2025 — Privacy information management</h3><p><strong>System impact:</strong> personal-data inventories, controller/processor responsibilities, retention, access, privacy controls and evidence. The 2025 edition is a standalone management-system standard.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">42001</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI management system</h3><p><strong>System impact:</strong> AI inventory, responsibilities, risk, impact assessment, lifecycle controls, monitoring, supplier use, data and governance. It does not prove that a specific model output is correct.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">20000</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO/IEC 20000-1:2018 — IT service management</h3><p><strong>System impact:</strong> service design, transition, incidents, requests, changes, suppliers, service levels, measurement and improvement. Strong fit for AMS and managed-service governance.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">22301</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 22301:2019 — Business continuity management</h3><p><strong>System impact:</strong> business impact analysis, continuity strategies, recovery dependencies, exercises and evidence. The 2019 edition remains current but is under revision in 2026.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">HIT</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>HITRUST CSF Certification — Healthcare and high-assurance cybersecurity</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> does the organization have independently assessed controls against a harmonized cybersecurity and compliance framework?</p><p><strong>2026 portfolio:</strong> HITRUST offers e1, i1 and r2 certifications with increasing depth, plus AI-related assurance options.</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> healthcare SaaS, cloud, integrations, patient or regulated data, access, logging, third parties and evidence reuse.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">FED</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>FedRAMP Certified — US federal cloud evidence</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> can US federal agencies reuse a standardized cloud security assessment package for an agency authorization decision?</p><p><strong>2026 terminology:</strong> FedRAMP uses <em>FedRAMP Certification / FedRAMP Certified</em>. The certification is reusable security evidence; the agency still authorizes its own information system and accepts risk for its specific use, configuration and integrations.</p></div></article>
    </div>
  </section>

    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Supply chain and regulated production</p>
      <h2>Where ERP data becomes certification evidence.</h2>
      <p>In these industries, batch, lot, supplier, production, inspection, traceability and change records can be part of the evidence chain.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">28000</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 28000:2022 — Supply-chain security management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> how does the organization manage security and resilience risks, including risks relevant to the supply chain?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> supplier and carrier data, logistics locations, incidents, routes, access, partner risk, business continuity and security controls.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">22000</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 22000:2018 — Food safety management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> can an organization in the food chain systematically manage food-safety hazards?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> batches, expiry, recipes, suppliers, quality inspections, HACCP evidence, storage conditions, traceability, complaints and recalls.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> ISO 22000:2018 remains current but a replacement is under development.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">FSSC</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>FSSC 22000 Version 7 — Food-safety certification scheme</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> does the food-sector organization meet the FSSC scheme, which builds on ISO 22000, sector prerequisite programmes and additional scheme requirements?</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> Version 7 was released in May 2026. Version 6 audits can continue until 30 April 2027, followed by the defined upgrade window.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">13485</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 13485:2016 — Medical-device quality management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> can a medical-device organization demonstrate a quality system designed for regulatory requirements?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> device and material traceability, supplier quality, validation, design changes, production records, complaints, CAPA and document control.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> the 2016 edition was reviewed and confirmed in 2025 and remains current.</p></div></article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card"><div class="ecg-determination-card__index">IATF</div><div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>IATF 16949:2016 — Automotive quality management</h3><p><strong>Enterprise question:</strong> does an automotive production or service-parts organization operate a quality management system aligned to automotive customer and supply-chain requirements?</p><p><strong>System impact:</strong> PPAP evidence, control plans, FMEA links, supplier quality, production traceability, changes, nonconformities, corrective action and customer-specific requirements.</p><p><strong>2026 status:</strong> IATF still publishes and certifies against IATF 16949:2016, with current sanctioned interpretations, FAQs and Rules 6th Edition.</p></div></article>
    </div>

    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Do not call everything a certificate</p>
      <h2>Some important enterprise obligations use a different evidence model.</h2>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-memory-grid">
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>LAW</span><strong>GDPR, DORA, NIS2, EU AI Act</strong><h3>Regulations create obligations, not a universal official “compliance certificate”.</h3><p>Certifications can support evidence, but legal responsibility remains with the organization.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>AUDIT</span><strong>SOC 1, SOC 2, ISAE 3402, BSI C5</strong><h3>These are assurance or attestation models, not ordinary ISO management-system certificates.</h3><p>Read period, scope, opinion, exceptions and customer controls.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>GUIDE</span><strong>ISO/IEC 27017 and ISO/IEC 27018</strong><h3>Cloud-specific control and privacy standards deepen an ISO/IEC 27001 assessment.</h3><p>Do not assume they behave like a standalone organization-wide certificate without checking the certification scheme and scope.</p></article>
      <article class="ecg-memory-card"><span>GXP</span><strong>GMP / GxP / FDA / EU regulatory controls</strong><h3>Regulated-life-sciences compliance is built from regulations, validation, inspections, records and controlled processes.</h3><p>Do not reduce it to one generic “GxP certificate”.</p></article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">SAP Lead lens</p>
      <h2>Translate the certificate into process evidence.</h2>
      <p>A Lead does not need to become the certification auditor. The useful skill is to identify which system capabilities support the certified management process and which controls remain outside SAP.</p>
    </header>
    <ol class="research-canvas__steps">
      <li><span>01</span><strong>Read the scope</strong><p>Legal entity, sites, products, services, activities and exclusions.</p></li>
      <li><span>02</span><strong>Map controlled processes</strong><p>Procurement, production, quality, maintenance, sales, HR, EHS, finance, security, ITSM or AI governance.</p></li>
      <li><span>03</span><strong>Map evidence objects</strong><p>Master data, approvals, documents, batches, inspections, changes, logs, incidents, training, CAPA, supplier records and KPIs.</p></li>
      <li><span>04</span><strong>Separate system from operating model</strong><p>SAP can provide workflow and evidence. Ownership, policy, competence, management review and many controls remain organizational responsibilities.</p></li>
      <li><span>05</span><strong>Design for auditability</strong><p>Keep ownership, change history, retention, access, traceability and evidence retrieval explicit.</p></li>
    </ol>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">record_voice_over</span>
    <p><strong>Assessment answer:</strong> “I would first check whether the standard certifies a management system, an industry scheme, or a service assessment. Then I would read its scope and translate the relevant requirements into process controls and evidence objects. In SAP that can mean master data, approvals, quality records, batches, change history, supplier evidence, incidents or audit logs — but certification still belongs to the organization and defined scope, not to an individual transaction.”</p>
  </section>
