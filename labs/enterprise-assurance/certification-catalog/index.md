      <h1>A standard matters when it changes what must be controlled.</h1>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" id="operations" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Operational management systems</p>
      <h2>These standards turn broad responsibilities into controlled operating systems.</h2>
      <p>The SAP connection is indirect but important. The standard defines the management discipline; enterprise systems may carry the records, measurements, approvals and history that make the discipline observable.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">14001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 14001:2026 — Environmental management</h3><p>The 2026 edition is the current environmental management-system standard. It is concerned with how an organization identifies environmental aspects, obligations, objectives, operational controls and improvement.</p><p>Relevant system evidence can include waste and emissions data, materials, incidents, environmental measurements, supplier requirements and operational records. SAP may hold part of that evidence, but the certified system is wider than the ERP record.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">45001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 45001:2018 — Occupational health and safety</h3><p>ISO 45001 remains the published OH&amp;S management-system standard while a new edition is under development. It addresses the way an organization manages workplace hazards, risks, incidents and continual improvement.</p><p>Operational evidence may involve hazard and incident records, contractor controls, competence and training, permits, maintenance history and corrective actions. None of those records alone proves that the management system is effective.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">50001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 50001:2018 — Energy management</h3><p>This standard remains current for energy management systems. It focuses on systematic improvement of energy performance rather than on a one-time efficiency project.</p><p>Useful enterprise data can include meters, energy baselines, production volumes, facilities, equipment, procurement data and performance indicators. The important link is between measured consumption and the operating context that explains it.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">55001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 55001:2024 — Asset management</h3><p>ISO 55001 connects asset-management policy and objectives to lifecycle value, risk, performance and cost. For asset-intensive organizations, that makes data quality and maintenance history part of the management conversation rather than only a technical concern.</p><p>Equipment and asset master data, criticality, work history, condition information, investment decisions and lifecycle cost can all contribute evidence when they match the certified operating model.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">37001</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 37001:2025 — Anti-bribery management</h3><p>The 2025 edition addresses an anti-bribery management system: policies, due diligence, financial and non-financial controls, reporting, investigation and improvement.</p><p>In enterprise systems this can touch supplier and business-partner onboarding, approvals, purchasing and payment controls, gifts or conflict records and audit history. System controls support the program; they do not replace governance, investigation or accountability.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">37301</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 37301:2021 — Compliance management</h3><p>ISO 37301 remains the published compliance management-system standard and was confirmed in 2026. It provides a structure for identifying obligations, assigning responsibilities, operating controls, monitoring and improving the compliance system.</p><p>ERP and platform evidence may include approvals, access, policy acknowledgements, training records, control results, issues and remediation. The legal interpretation of an obligation still sits outside the transaction log.</p></div>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Supply chain and regulated production</p>
      <h2>Here, traceability and process evidence become part of the product story.</h2>
      <p>These schemes differ in scope, but they often depend on reliable links between suppliers, materials, production, inspection, changes and released product.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">28000</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 28000:2022 — Security and resilience</h3><p>ISO 28000 specifies requirements for a security management system and includes aspects relevant to the supply chain. It is not limited to one industry.</p><p>For enterprise operations, useful evidence can span suppliers, carriers, locations, incidents, access, continuity and security controls. The standard is broader than shipment tracking: it is a management system for security risk.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">22000</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 22000:2018 — Food safety management</h3><p>The 2018 edition remains the published food-safety management-system standard, although a replacement is under development. It applies to organizations in the food chain that need a systematic way to control food-safety hazards.</p><p>ERP evidence can become important around suppliers, recipes or specifications, batches, expiry, inspections, storage conditions, complaints, recalls and traceability.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">FSSC</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>FSSC 22000 Version 7 — Food-safety certification scheme</h3><p>FSSC 22000 Version 7 was released on 1 May 2026. The scheme combines ISO 22000 with sector prerequisite programmes and additional FSSC requirements. Version 6 audits can continue during the published transition period before Version 7 becomes mandatory for upgrade audits.</p><p>The distinction matters: FSSC 22000 is a certification scheme with its own rules, not simply another name for ISO 22000.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">13485</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>ISO 13485:2016 — Medical-device quality management</h3><p>The 2016 edition remains current after its 2025 review. It defines a quality management system for organizations involved in medical devices and related services, with a strong regulatory and product-safety context.</p><p>Supplier quality, material and device traceability, validation, production records, change control, complaints, CAPA and document control can all depend on consistent enterprise data.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">IATF</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>IATF 16949:2016 — Automotive quality management</h3><p>IATF 16949:2016 remains the automotive quality-management reference used by the IATF certification scheme. Certification also depends on current IATF rules, sanctioned interpretations and customer-specific requirements, so the standard number alone is not the whole operating contract.</p><p>Supplier quality, control plans, product and process changes, production traceability, nonconformities and corrective action are typical areas where ERP and quality-system records need to connect.</p></div>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">Special assurance programs</p>
      <h2>Some widely used programs do not fit neatly into the ISO catalog.</h2>
      <p>The same reading rule still applies: identify the assessment route, the system or service in scope, the version and the evidence the program actually produces.</p>
    </header>
    <div class="ecg-determination-list">
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">HIT</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>HITRUST CSF certifications</h3><p>HITRUST currently offers e1, i1 and r2 certification paths with different depth, as well as AI-related assurance options. The right path depends on risk and assurance needs; the HITRUST name by itself does not tell us which assessment was completed.</p><p>For a healthcare or regulated-data service, match the certified scope to the actual service, data and third-party dependencies before treating the result as vendor evidence.</p></div>
      </article>
      <article class="ecg-determination-card">
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__index">FED</div>
        <div class="ecg-determination-card__copy"><h3>FedRAMP Certification — US federal cloud</h3><p>FedRAMP uses <em>FedRAMP Certification</em> and <em>FedRAMP Certified</em> as the official program label in 2026. It gives US federal agencies reusable cloud-security assessment evidence and a common authorization framework.</p><p>That evidence still has a boundary. An agency must evaluate its own system, integrations, data, configuration and accepted risk rather than treating the provider label as an authorization for every use.</p></div>
      </article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__inventory" data-reveal>
    <header>
      <p class="research-canvas__eyebrow">SAP connection</p>
      <h2>Translate the standard into evidence objects, not SAP product slogans.</h2>
    </header>
    <p>Environmental, safety, energy and asset-management systems may draw evidence from EHS, maintenance or EAM, production, procurement, measurements and sustainability data. Food, medical-device and automotive quality schemes can depend on supplier and product master data, batches or serials, inspections, nonconformities, production history, change records and traceability. Anti-bribery and compliance systems may use business-partner onboarding, approvals, purchasing and payment controls, access records and audit history.</p>
    <p>Those examples show where evidence can live. They do not mean that an SAP module automatically satisfies a standard. The certified process, organizational ownership, configuration, operating discipline and evidence boundary still have to fit together.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="research-canvas__boundary" data-reveal>
    <span class="material-symbols-outlined" aria-hidden="true">rule</span>
    <p><strong>Not every obligation is a certification.</strong> Laws such as GDPR, DORA, NIS2 and the EU AI Act create legal duties. SOC and ISAE reports are assurance reports. BSI C5 and CSA STAR use their own assessment models. Keep those evidence types separate before comparing providers.</p>
  </section>
