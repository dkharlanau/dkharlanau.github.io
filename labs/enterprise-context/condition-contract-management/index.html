Enterprise Context Lab / Settlement Management

A rebate is not one discount. It is an agreement settled against accumulated business volume.

Condition Contract Management (CCM) is the Settlement Management model used for subsequent settlement scenarios such as customer and supplier rebates. The condition contract holds the commercial agreement; SAP then determines the eligible business volume, calculates accruals or settlement amounts, and creates settlement documents at defined points in the contract lifecycle.

Follow the lifecycle arrow_downward
contract

Keep the agreement separate from the transactions that earn it. A sales invoice, supplier invoice, or goods receipt can contribute business volume, but the rebate terms live in the condition contract and are evaluated later during settlement.

This is the key difference from an ordinary document condition. An invoice discount is normally calculated on that document. A condition contract can depend on activity accumulated across many documents and over a longer validity period.

The core model

Agreement, business volume, calculation, settlement.

Most CCM questions become easier once these four layers are kept separate.

01

Condition contract

The contract defines the business partner, validity, settlement rules, relevant conditions, settlement calendar, and the criteria used to determine business volume. It represents the agreement that will be settled later.

02

Business volume

SAP selects the transactional evidence that belongs to the agreement. For sales rebates this is commonly customer billing data. For purchasing rebates, the business-volume basis can be supplier invoices or goods receipts, depending on the configured process and contract type.

03

Calculation and accruals

The condition records in the contract determine how the eligible volume is valued. Accruals estimate the expected obligation or claim before final settlement; they are not the final commercial settlement itself.

04

Settlement

A settlement run evaluates the contract for a settlement date, calculates the amount due, and creates settlement documents. Those documents connect the commercial agreement to the accounting result.

Lifecycle

The contract becomes meaningful only when business volume is interpreted correctly.

A practical CCM flow is not “create contract, then run settlement.” The business-volume definition in the middle determines which transactions are counted, when they count, and therefore which amount is eventually settled.

Agreement and validity→ Business-volume selection→ Rate, scale and accrual logic→ Settlement date type→ Settlement document and FI effect

That middle step is where many design errors hide. A contract can contain the right percentage and still produce the wrong amount if the wrong customer, material, date, invoice, goods receipt, or other selection criterion enters the business volume.

Settlement timing

Accrual, partial settlement, and final settlement answer different questions.

The settlement calendar controls when the agreement is evaluated, but the settlement date type controls what that evaluation means.

Delta accruals

Recalculate accumulated accrual requirements from the relevant business volume and post the difference. This updates the estimate without closing the commercial agreement.

Partial settlement

Provisionally settle a period while the condition contract remains active. SAP can take previous settlements into account when later volume or scale levels change.

Final settlement

Permanently settle a defined period of the contract. A contract can contain more than one final-settlement period, so “final” does not always mean that the whole contract history is represented by one document.

Sales and procurement

The same CCM idea uses different transactional evidence.

Do not memorize sales and purchasing rebates as two unrelated tools. The shared pattern is agreement plus accumulated business volume plus subsequent settlement. What changes is the commercial direction and the source of that volume.

SDSales rebatesCustomer agreement, billing-based business volume, rebate calculation, accruals, and customer-side settlement.arrow_forward MMPurchasing rebatesSupplier agreement with business volume based on the configured purchasing process, including supplier-invoice or goods-receipt scenarios.arrow_forward

SAP's current purchasing-rebate documentation explicitly supports both supplier-invoice-based and goods-receipt-based business volume. That distinction is not cosmetic: it changes when an entitlement becomes measurable and which operational documents explain the settlement result.

A concrete example

Why a 3% rebate can still be a data-selection problem.

Assume a customer receives a year-end rebate once eligible sales pass a threshold. The contract contains the correct scale and rate, but one product family was accidentally excluded from the business-volume criteria. Billing documents are correct, pricing on those invoices is correct, and the settlement program runs successfully. The result is still too low because SAP evaluated the wrong population of transactions.

The useful diagnostic sequence is therefore: confirm the contract and validity, inspect the business volume that SAP selected, then inspect the condition calculation, and only after that trace the settlement document and accounting posting. Starting from the percentage often sends the investigation to the wrong layer.

S/4HANA boundary

Condition contracts are the S/4HANA settlement model for new rebate processing.

SAP introduced Settlement Management as the successor architecture to classic SD rebate processing. Older SAP ERP knowledge about rebate agreements and the VBOX index is therefore useful mainly as migration context, not as the model to apply to a new S/4HANA rebate design.

That does not mean every rebate scenario is identical. Contract types, business-volume sources, settlement methods, tax behavior, application availability, and configuration options are release- and scenario-sensitive. Verify those details against the target S/4HANA edition and release instead of copying a transaction or condition-type list from another implementation.
