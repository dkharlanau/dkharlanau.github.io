Deep vertical / Sales Billing

Billing starts before the invoice
and ends after it is saved.

In SAP S/4HANA Sales, billing turns a billable commercial reference into an invoice, credit memo, debit memo, or another billing document. The useful model is a chain: establish what is billable, create the correct billing document, then prove the accounting and correction state.

60-second answer: Billing starts in the source process, where the item becomes relevant and due for billing. Depending on the scenario, the reference can be the sales order, delivery, billing-plan date, or another billable object. Copying control governs whether the source may create the target billing document and how quantity, data, and pricing are transferred. Compatible references can be combined; incompatible header data creates a split. After the billing document is saved, I still prove the FI transfer, because the Sales invoice and the accounting posting are separate states. If the original billing event is wrong, cancellation reverses it; credit or debit processes adjust the commercial result.

The business chain

First decide what the system is allowed to bill.

Billing does not begin with VF04 or with an invoice type. It begins in the source process, where SAP determines whether an item is relevant for billing and which document should be the billing reference.

For a standard sales item, the item category can make billing delivery-related or order-related. In delivery-related billing, the outbound delivery is the billing basis. In order-related billing, the sales document is the basis. SAP also supports other patterns, including billing plans and third-party scenarios, so “post goods issue, then invoice” is common but not a universal billing rule.

This distinction matters because it changes the evidence we expect. If a delivery-related item does not become billable, the delivery and its status are central. If an order-related item is missing from billing, searching only in delivery processing sends the investigation in the wrong direction.

Due list

The billing due list is a worklist, not an invoice.

VF04 and the corresponding billing apps select source documents that are due for billing. Processing that selection is the separate step that creates billing documents.

That separation is useful in both operations and design. A source item can fail to appear because it is not yet due, is blocked, has the wrong billing relevance, or does not satisfy process-specific prerequisites. An item can also appear in the due list and still fail during billing creation. SAP therefore supports simulation and a billing-run log rather than treating due-list membership as proof of successful invoicing.

When users say “VF04 is wrong,” the better question is whether the problem is selection or creation. Those are different stages with different causes.

Copying control

The invoice inherits a controlled version of its source.

Creating a billing document is not a blind copy. Copying control defines which source and target document combinations are allowed and how business data moves between them.

At this boundary SAP can control requirements, billing quantity, field transfer, document flow, and whether pricing is copied or redetermined. For a delivery-related invoice, for example, the quantity can come from the delivery while prices can come from the sales order. This is why an apparently simple question such as “why is the invoice different from the order?” may belong to billing copy logic rather than to the pricing record itself.

Collective billing adds one more decision. Several references can only converge into one billing document when the resulting header state is compatible. If the system creates two invoices instead of one, split analysis is usually more informative than forcing convergence with custom code.

Boundary with Pricing: the Pricing guide explains how the commercial value is determined. Billing decides how that value is carried or recalculated when the billing document is created.

Financial Accounting

A billing document can exist before the financial posting succeeds.

Sales Billing and Financial Accounting are connected, but they are not the same persistence step.

When an accounting-relevant billing document is posted, SAP transfers the relevant billing data to Financial Accounting and creates the journal entry. The transfer can be blocked or fail, for example because of account-assignment, incompleteness, or pricing errors. In that state the invoice exists in Sales, but the expected accounting result is not yet complete.

This boundary is important during reconciliation. “Invoice created” proves the commercial billing document exists. It does not by itself prove that receivables, revenue, tax, and other configured accounting effects have been posted correctly. The posting status and the related accounting document are separate evidence.

Boundary with Finance: account determination and journal-entry design belong to the FI/CO for Logistics guide. Billing supplies the commercial values and accounting context that the finance handoff consumes.

Cancellation

Cancellation preserves history; it does not erase the invoice.

SAP cancels a billing document by creating a corresponding cancellation billing document.

The cancellation document neutralizes the original billing document. If the original was already posted to Financial Accounting, the cancellation must also produce the corresponding offsetting accounting entry. If the original was not posted, SAP can mark the billing and cancellation documents as canceled without creating journal entries. The billed reference becomes open for billing again so that a correct document can be created.

This makes cancellation different from a commercial adjustment. A credit memo or debit memo expresses a new adjustment to the customer account; cancellation reverses the original billing event. The right choice depends on what is wrong and on the business and localization context.

A concrete investigation

“The delivery is complete, but there is no invoice.”

This symptom is useful because it shows why a single billing transaction is not enough to diagnose the process.

Start with the item’s billing model. If it is delivery-related, prove that the correct delivery and quantity are eligible for billing. Then establish whether the source is actually due and whether a billing block, date, status, or other prerequisite prevents selection. If it appears in the due list, move to billing creation and inspect the creation result rather than revisiting shipping.

If the billing document is created, the question changes again. Check whether it has been posted to Financial Accounting and whether the expected accounting document exists. Finally, if the problem is customer-facing rather than financial, move to output or electronic-invoice processing. Each step has its own owner and evidence.

This sequence is more reliable than jumping directly to copy control, pricing, or a transaction code because it finds the first state that differs from the expected process.

Process boundaries

Read billing as a document chain.

The same customer invoice crosses several domains, but each domain answers a different question.

Sales and fulfillment

What business event is billable, which document is the reference, and what quantity is ready?

Billing

Which billing type is created, how is source data copied, and should references combine or split?

Finance and external evidence

Was the billing document posted correctly, and was the customer-facing or legally required invoice issued through the relevant downstream process?

A green status in one layer is not proof that the entire order-to-cash event is complete. Reconcile the reference, billing document, accounting result, and—where relevant—the output or electronic-document state.
