SAP Logistics / Kanban

Replenish because material was consumed.
Not because a plan says it might be consumed.

Kanban is a short-cycle pull-control method. A container or signal represents usable material at the demand point. Consumption changes the signal, the signal starts replenishment, and the replenishment method decides whether SAP creates production, purchasing, or stock-transfer work.

The pull loop

One simple signal can drive several very different supply processes.

The visible Kanban is only the front of the process. Behind it, SAP still needs a supply source, master data, document logic, stock postings, and exception handling.

01

Consume

Production takes material from the demand-side container or supply area.

The physical need appears at the point of use.
02

Signal

The container moves to EMPTY, or a quantity signal reaches its trigger.

The demand side asks for replenishment.
03

Replenish

SAP can create or trigger internal production, external procurement, or stock transfer.

The strategy determines the execution object.
04

Return

Material arrives, the required posting happens, and the container becomes available again.

FULL means the next consumption cycle can start.

Fit

Kanban is powerful when the operating conditions support it.

It is not a universal replacement for planning. It works best when the shop-floor replenishment loop is repetitive, fast, and stable enough to be controlled by consumption.

GOOD FIT

Short, repeatable loops

Demand is regular enough to size containers, replenishment lead time is predictable, and the source can react quickly in small lots.

GOOD FIT

Material close to use

A production supply area or similar demand point acts as a controlled buffer near the work center or line.

GOOD FIT

Fast execution

Setup times, source capacity, and component availability allow replenishment without building large protective stock.

POOR FIT

Long or unstable supply

Highly volatile demand, long lead times, rare components, large campaign production, or weak source capacity can make a fixed pull loop unreliable.

Lead signal: if the process needs large safety buffers to survive, first fix the replenishment design. Adding more Kanban cards only hides the problem.

Planning boundary

MRP and Kanban solve different time horizons.

MRP can still plan the wider material picture. Kanban can control the short-cycle movement between a demand point and its supply source.

MRP

  • Plans from requirements, dates, lead times, BOMs, lot sizes, and planning parameters.
  • Creates or proposes supply elements before consumption reaches the shop floor.
  • Best for wider planning and dependency calculation.

Kanban

  • Starts from an actual consumption signal.
  • Uses container quantity and control-cycle rules.
  • Best for decentralized short-term replenishment close to production.

Together

  • Use planning to understand future demand and capacity.
  • Use the Kanban loop to execute repetitive local replenishment.
  • Decide explicitly whether the Kanban-managed storage-location segment participates in MRP.

Lead diagnostic

Debug Kanban in four layers.

This model prevents a common mistake: treating a red container, a missing purchase order, a stock mismatch, and an accounting issue as the same failure.

SIGNAL

1. Kanban state

What status changed? Was the transition allowed? Did a barcode, board action, or quantity signal fire?

Proof: status history and control-cycle sequence.
SUPPLY

2. Replenishment object

Was the expected production order, schedule line, purchase order, reservation, or transfer object created?

Proof: object type, reference, quantity, and source.
STOCK

3. Physical movement

Did goods issue, transfer posting, or goods receipt actually move book stock as intended?

Proof: material document and before/after stock state.
COST

4. Value and consumption

Did backflush, cost-center consumption, order settlement logic, or valuation follow the business event?

Proof: consumption and accounting evidence.
