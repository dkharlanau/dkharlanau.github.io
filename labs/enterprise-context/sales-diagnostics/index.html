Diagnostic contract

Use the casebook to narrow the owner, not to guess a fix.

The cases are deliberately symptom-first. Start with the document, message or business result that is wrong. Then decide which process boundary can own the symptom. A billing problem may begin in the sales order, move through shipping, and only become visible in billing. A price difference belongs first to the pricing model. A JIT quantity problem may belong to the Automotive JIT flow or to message processing in integration operations.

For each case, collect evidence before changing configuration. Compare one good document with one bad document. Capture the relevant document flow, statuses, partner and organizational data, message identity, quantities, dates and copied values. Name the first field or event where the two cases diverge. That point is normally more useful than a long list of possible Customizing settings.

Product behavior is checked separately from the authored diagnostic path. For example, SAP documents how billing split analysis compares differing header and business-partner fields, while the casebook adds a practical ownership sequence for investigation. See SAP Help for Split Analysis and Billing Document Split and Convergence. The casebook does not turn these product facts into a universal root-cause list. It keeps the evidence, the hypothesis and the recommended change as separate steps.
