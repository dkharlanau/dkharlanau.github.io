Memory model

The verb usually tells us what kind of work the app owns.

This is not a strict SAP naming rule, but it is a useful way to navigate a system you do not know yet.

Manage

Work with business objects: search, open, create, edit, release, or continue processing.

Track

Follow one object through statuses and document flow. Good for “where is my order now?” questions.

Analyze

Look across many documents to find a pattern, bottleneck, exception, or KPI movement.

Create

Execute the next operational step, for example creating outbound deliveries or billing documents.

Schedule

Run repeatable background processing when manual worklists no longer scale.

Mass Change

Change many business documents in a controlled job instead of editing them one by one.

Core working set

These apps cover most day-to-day Sales support conversations.

You do not need to memorize every tile. Know the job, the business object, and the few apps that let you inspect the process from order to cash.

Use the document-specific app when the business job is clear.

Fulfillment and delivery

Separate status visibility from execution.

Tracking tells us what happened. A due list tells us what can happen next. An exception app helps us find why the expected step did not happen.

Lead lens: “The order is not in the delivery app” is a symptom, not a root cause. Prove delivery relevance, due date, confirmation, block status, organizational selection, and execution ownership before changing configuration.

Billing, returns, and claims

Creation apps and management apps answer different questions.

Before a billing document exists, investigate billing relevance and the due list. After it exists, investigate the billing document itself, posting, output, cancellation, or accounting hand-off.

Classic boundary

Fiori-first does not mean “classic tools no longer exist”.

Private Edition and on-premise projects still expose classic transaction-based apps, and some cloud scopes also surface transaction-style UIs. A Lead should recognize the familiar objects without making transaction codes the mental model.

Sales order

VA01 / VA02 / VA03 still appear in many landscapes. Think create / change / display order, not “the VA transaction family”.

Delivery

VL06O and VL02N remain useful reference points in classic execution and diagnostics. First identify whether SD, EWM, or TM owns the current state.

Billing

VF04 and VF01 remain familiar billing tools. The same reasoning still applies: due-list eligibility before creation, billing-document analysis after creation.

Lead use

Three questions before opening another app.

The goal is not to become fast at clicking tiles. The goal is to know which layer should contain the next piece of evidence.

  1. Which business object owns the current state? Order, schedule line, delivery, billing document, credit account, pricing record, availability result, or another object?
  2. Am I doing transaction work, exception monitoring, or analysis? A manage app, a fulfillment cockpit, and a KPI app may all show the same order but answer different questions.
  3. If the app shows the wrong result, what upstream input created it? Trace master data, configuration, status, availability, integration, enhancement logic, or a downstream execution owner before changing anything.
