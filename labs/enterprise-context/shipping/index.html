Lead answer frame

Trace the date back to the first logistics decision.

A final delivery date is a downstream result. The Lead-level task is to identify which plant, shipping, route, calendar, or scheduling decision first moved away from the expected business path.

1. Execution context

  • Confirm the delivering plant before debugging Shipping.
  • Trace plant + shipping condition + loading group into shipping point.
  • Trace origin, destination, shipping policy, and transportation group into route.

2. Date construction

  • Separate route/transit data from pick-pack, loading, and calendar effects.
  • Trace requested delivery date backward to material availability, loading, GI, and transportation dates.
  • Check whether ATP is consuming the date you actually intended.

3. Execute and prove

  • Distinguish delivery-due state from warehouse or transport execution.
  • Identify the handoff to EWM or TM instead of changing SD to repair their downstream state.
  • Prove the corrected dates and the executable delivery path end to end.

Boundary with ATP: Shipping builds important logistics dates and execution context. ATP evaluates the promise against its requirement date, supply-demand scope, and policy controls. Diagnose the first wrong owner before changing lead times or confirmations.

Times vs dates

A duration says how long. A calendar says when.

This distinction is basic, but it explains many “SAP added too many days” incidents. A weekend or closed working window can move a calculated date without changing the stored duration.

{% for item in graph.scheduling_dates %}

Requested Delivery− Transit Goods Issue− Loading Loading Date− Pick/Pack Material Availability

Transportation planning lead time is calculated in the transportation-scheduling branch. Calendars and working times adjust the activities while the system calculates the dates.

Backward vs forward

One asks what must happen. The other asks what can happen.

I use this difference to explain date shifts without making scheduling sound mysterious.

BACKWARD

Start from the requested delivery date.

Calculate the dates required to deliver on time.

PAST?

Check whether required activities fall before the feasible start.

A requested date can be commercially desired but operationally impossible.

FORWARD

Start from the earliest feasible point.

Calculate the earliest material availability, shipping, and resulting delivery dates.

ATP boundary

Scheduling tells ATP when the material must be available.

The material availability date is a bridge between shipping logic and the promise engine. If the date entering ATP is wrong, an availability check can be technically correct and still produce the wrong business promise.

Requested Delivery Date→ Shipping Scheduling→ Material Availability Date→ ATP / aATP→ Confirmed Qty / Date

Delivery due

A correct date does not guarantee a delivery.

Before delivery creation, the order still has to be due, delivery relevant, unblocked, and inside the selection context used by operations.

Schedule Line→ Delivery Relevance + Due Date+ No Delivery / Credit Block+ Due-list Selection→ Outbound Delivery

Lead lens: “Not in VL10” is a symptom. I classify it as date, delivery relevance, block/status, organizational selection, or execution grouping before changing configuration.

Delivery split

A split is evidence that two items cannot stay in one execution document.

The important question is where and why the divergence appeared. Sales, TM, EWM, and physical loading can all be part of that story.

One Sales Order→ Compare Shipping Point / Route / Header Context→ Delivery Creation→ TM / EWM / Loading Changes→ One or Multiple Deliveries

My rule: I first identify the exact process stage where the split happened. Otherwise an EWM load split can easily be “fixed” in SD, which is a creative way to move the defect into another team.
