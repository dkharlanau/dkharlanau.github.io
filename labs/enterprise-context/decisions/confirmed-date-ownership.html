Decision Card / Sales + ATP + Shipping

Who owns a wrong
confirmed delivery date?

A date shown in a sales order can combine customer request, product availability, plant choice, loading and transit assumptions, and commercial promise. I first find which date decision became wrong.

Diagnosis path

REQRequestWhat did the customer ask?
ATPConfirmWhen can supply fulfill it?
SHIPScheduleCan logistics execute it?
One date field can hide several decision engines.
rule

Decision in one sentence: do not overwrite the confirmed date until you separate the customer request, ATP confirmation, shipping/transport scheduling, and any commercial commitment that may intentionally differ from the raw system result.

Five owners

Ask which engine moved the date.

Customer request

Start with requested delivery date, quantity, ship-to context, and whether the requirement itself was entered correctly.

Supply / ATP

Availability determines which quantity can be confirmed and on which date under the active scope and confirmation rules.

Plant and sourcing

A different delivering plant or supply alternative can change availability, lead time, stock ownership, and the date that can be promised.

Shipping and transportation

Pick/pack, loading, route, transit, calendars, and transportation planning can move the execution date even when stock is available.

Commercial promise

Sales owns communication and exception handling when the business promise differs from the technically calculated date.

Failure map

The same late date has different first checks.

SymptomFirst questionDo not start with
Confirmed quantity is lateDid ATP fail to find sufficient supply on the requested date?Changing route or transit time without proving availability.
Stock exists but date is still lateIs the stock in the ATP scope and at the relevant plant/segment?Assuming physical stock equals confirmable stock.
ATP date looks correct, delivery date does notWhich scheduling, route, calendar, or transportation step moved execution?Re-running ATP as the only diagnosis.
System date is acceptable, customer promise is notWas a commercial promise made outside the system result?Calling the calculation wrong before checking the commitment.
warning

Anti-pattern: manually changing the requested or confirmed date until the order looks correct. That can hide the real shortage, wrong plant, schedule assumption, or customer commitment and makes later rescheduling harder to explain.

Lead check: finish with proof that the confirmed quantity/date, logistics execution date, and customer-facing promise are intentionally aligned.
