Decision Card / Cross-functional Logistics

STO or
intercompany sales?

Both can cross company-code boundaries and both can include intercompany billing. The stronger question is what starts the process and where the goods are meant to arrive.

My default question

01Who orders?Purchasing or Sales?
02Where to?Receiving plant or customer?
03Who settles?Physical and financial chain
Billing alone does not identify the process.
rule

Decision in one sentence: use an STO when a receiving plant or purchasing organization orders stock from another plant; use intercompany sales when a sales organization sells to a customer and a plant assigned to another company code delivers the goods to that customer.

Decision drivers

Start from the initiating relationship and destination.

1. What starts the process?

An STO is purchase-order driven. Intercompany sales is sales-order driven. This usually reveals the business owner before the shipping step appears.

2. Where do the goods go?

In stock transfer, the delivering plant supplies the receiving plant. In intercompany sales, the delivering plant supplies the external customer of the selling sales organization.

3. What is the business intent?

Replenishment and redistribution point toward STO. Customer fulfillment through another company code points toward intercompany sales.

4. Does billing decide the answer?

No. Cross-company stock transfer can also create intercompany billing. Billing is a consequence of the company-code boundary, not the main discriminator between the two process models.

5. Who owns exceptions?

Short shipment, goods in transit, customer delivery, price differences, invoice mismatches, and returns must follow the chosen process ownership model.

Comparison

Two intercompany flows, two different business purposes.

warning

Important boundary: an STO can itself be intercompany. Do not use the word “intercompany” as shorthand for intercompany sales.

I change the design when: advanced intercompany processes, tax, transfer pricing, customs, export control, valuation, goods-in-transit design, third-party ownership, or regional legal requirements create additional documents or control points.

Failure ownership: reconcile physical movement, customer or receiving-plant completion, and financial settlement separately. A correct goods issue does not prove that the business chain is complete.
