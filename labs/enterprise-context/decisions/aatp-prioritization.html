Decision Card / aATP

Who should own
scarce-supply priority?

When supply is short, the wrong answer is to let the loudest order win. Priority should come from an explicit business policy that aATP can apply consistently across new demand and existing confirmations.

My default

CAPPALUpper consumption limit
KEEPSUPProtected minimum supply
MOVEBOPReprioritize confirmations
Sales owns the promise, not ad hoc supply stealing.
rule

Decision in one sentence: the business owns the scarce-supply policy, Product Allocation limits consumption, Supply Protection reserves minimum supply for defined groups, and Backorder Processing applies prioritization rules when existing confirmations need to be recalculated.

Choose the control

Different supply problems need different mechanisms.

QuestionDefault mechanism
How much may a customer, region, channel, or group consume?Product Allocation as an upper limit.
Which demand group must retain minimum access to supply?Supply Protection.
How should existing confirmations change after supply or demand changes?Backorder Processing with explicit confirmation strategies and segments.
What quantity and date can one order receive now?PAC and configured aATP checks.
What do we tell the customer?Sales / commercial owner, using the confirmed result and known execution constraints.

Lead checks

Make priority explainable before making it automatic.

Business segment

Which customer, region, channel, order type, or demand group receives different treatment and why?

Policy horizon

Is the rule a stable allocation policy, a protected minimum, or a temporary reprioritization after disruption?

Interaction

PAL and SUP can complement each other, but poorly aligned characteristics can also block supply in ways the business did not intend.

Existing promises

BOP can improve one confirmation by reducing or delaying another. Treat that as a customer-impact decision, not only an ATP batch job.

Evidence

Prove the segment, strategy, allocation or protection object, confirmation before and after the run, and affected customer promises.

warning

Anti-pattern: manually increase one order confirmation because the customer is important, without updating the policy that should govern all comparable demand.

I change the default when: legal, contractual, safety, or emergency priorities require a different rule. The exception should still be explicit, auditable, and time-bounded.

Lead proof: explain who owns priority, which aATP control represents it, what other demand loses or gains supply, and how customer communication changes.

Scenario + evidence

Protect policy, not one order.
