Decision Card / Sales + Tax

Who owns wrong tax
in a sales order?

Wrong tax can appear in Sales, but the first wrong decision may be legal policy, customer or material classification, departure or destination context, pricing determination, or an external tax service.

My default

RULETax policyFinance / Tax owner
INPUTMaster dataTaxability and location
RUNPricingDetermine, do not invent
Do not fix tax by overwriting the final amount.
rule

Decision in one sentence: Tax or Finance owns the legal policy, master-data owners maintain the reusable tax inputs, Sales pricing applies the configured determination, and integration owns only the contract when an external tax engine is authoritative.

Diagnosis

Trace inputs before changing a tax condition.

Legal rule

Which country, jurisdiction, exemption, registration, or business rule should apply? This is not a developer decision.

Transaction context

Check departure, destination, ship-to, plant, company, and the business event that determines the tax context.

Master data

Check customer and material tax classifications and any reusable taxability indicators used by the process.

Pricing path

Trace the tax condition, access, condition record, tax code, and calculation path. A correct final percentage can still come from the wrong rule.

External service

If an external tax engine is authoritative, prove the request inputs, response, mapping, fallback, and version used for the document.

warning

Anti-pattern: manually change the tax amount or hard-code a customer exception in Sales because one invoice looks wrong.

I change the default when: a country-specific legal solution or external tax engine owns rate and jurisdiction determination. Even then, business policy, input ownership, audit evidence, and fallback stay explicit.

Lead proof: explain the legal rule, show the determination inputs, reproduce the tax result, and confirm the billing and FI outcome.

Scenario + evidence

Separate legal ownership from technical determination.
