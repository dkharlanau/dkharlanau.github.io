From demand to commitment

Read procurement as a chain of decisions that become documents.

A requirement says what the business needs, where it is needed, in what quantity, and by when. The requirement may come from planning, a manual purchase requisition, a project, maintenance, production, or another process. A purchase requisition can formalize that internal demand before Purchasing turns it into an external commitment.

The purchase order then combines organizational responsibility, supplier, items, quantities, dates, commercial conditions, item behavior, and accounting context. Some values are entered, some are copied from a preceding document, and some are determined from master data or configuration. That is why a wrong value in the PO is often the visible result of an earlier decision rather than the place where the defect started.

Requirement

Prove the material or service, plant or receiving context, quantity, required date, and consumption intent before changing purchasing data.

Commitment

Establish which source is valid, which terms apply, how the item should behave, and whether approval is required before the order is sent.

Execution

Follow receipt or service acceptance, invoice verification, and the financial state that remains after those events.

SAP's current Create Purchase Requisition - Advanced documentation describes the requisition as the object used to request materials, services, and limits with the required quantity, location, and timing. The exact user interface can differ by deployment and process, but the business distinction between internal requirement and purchasing commitment remains useful.

Purchase order

The PO turns a sourcing decision into one controlled commitment.

A purchase order can be created with reference to a purchase requisition, quotation, contract, or existing purchase order, or it can be entered without a reference. The reference reduces manual entry, but the buyer still has to check the proposed values.

Reference

Reference documents can carry requirement, source, quantity, dates, conditions, and other purchasing context into the PO. Use the reference chain when you need to explain where a PO value came from.

Defaults

Supplier data can propose the ordering address, payment terms, and Incoterms. Material data can propose the short text, order unit, and material group. A purchasing info record can propose a price.

Source type

An external supplier normally leads to a standard purchase order. If the supplying source is an internal plant, the process is a stock transport order rather than normal external procurement.

account_tree

Organizational rule: one PO can contain items for different plants, but those plants must be valid for the purchasing organization in the PO header. The PO itself also has one company-code context. This is why cross-plant purchasing does not mean “any plant can be mixed into any PO.”

LayerTypical dataLead check
Header Supplier, company code, purchasing organization, purchasing group, currency, payment and commercial context. If the whole PO is wrong, check the header and organizational assignment before item-level configuration.
Item Material or service, quantity, delivery date, plant, storage or delivery context, item category, account assignment, price conditions. If only one item behaves differently, identify which item-level control changed the process.

Fiori: Manage Purchase Orders is the main app for creating, finding, monitoring, displaying, and changing purchase orders. Current SAP Learning also uses references such as contracts, info records, and existing purchase orders to reduce data entry. App scope and supported item categories can vary by release.

Item and accounting context

Item category and account assignment answer different questions.

The purchasing item category controls important process behavior. SAP documents, among other things, whether the item needs a material number, whether account assignment is required, whether it is managed as stock, and whether goods receipt and invoice receipt are expected. That is why the item category changes the later document flow rather than merely changing how the item is displayed.

Account assignment answers a different question: where should the value be charged when the purchase is for consumption rather than ordinary valuated stock? Depending on the process, the receiver can be a cost center, order, project, asset, sales-order context, or another supported object. SAP also supports multiple account assignment for one purchasing item where the cost has to be distributed.

Keep the two layers separate. A wrong cost center does not prove the source-of-supply logic is wrong. A correct account assignment does not prove the item has the intended receipt and invoice behavior.

Outline agreements

Contract and scheduling agreement solve the same long-term sourcing problem in different ways.

An outline agreement stores negotiated terms for a longer period. The main design decision is how operational demand will be called off: by creating purchase orders against a contract, or by maintaining dated schedule lines under a scheduling agreement.

Decision pointContractScheduling agreement
Long-term commitment Uses a target quantity or target value for a validity period. Uses a total quantity and purchasing conditions for a validity period.
How demand is released Create a purchase order with reference to the contract. The PO is a contract release order. Create delivery schedule lines with quantities and dates. The supplier can receive the schedule directly or through explicit releases, depending on the document type.
Delivery dates The contract itself does not define the exact quantity for one delivery date. Schedule lines are the operational delivery plan and contain the required quantities and dates.
MRP integration MRP can assign the contract as a source, but the resulting requisition still has to become a release PO. MRP can generate schedule lines directly, which removes the extra PO-conversion step.
Best mental model “Terms first, PO call-off later.” “Terms plus a continuously maintained delivery schedule.”

A contract controls releases, not one delivery.

The contract header holds data that applies to the whole agreement, such as supplier, validity period, agreement type, and header conditions. Each item defines what is covered. A contract item can also be used as a source of supply in a purchase requisition. When that requirement becomes a PO with contract reference, the PO is a contract release order.

Quantity contract

The main control is a target quantity. Release documentation lets the buyer compare the target with the quantity already released and the quantity still open.

Value contract

The main control is a target value. The buyer monitors how much value has already been released and how much remains available.

Validity

Quantity, value, and validity are checked when a release order is created. System messages can be configured as warnings or, where required, as blocking errors.

Material-group contract items: M and W

Contract-specific item categories M and W let Purchasing negotiate for a group of materials without fixing one material number in the contract. The difference is mainly whether the items share one price or need their price to be decided at release time.

Item categoryUseWhat the contract storesWhat is decided in the release PO
M — Material unknown Quantity or value contract. Best when several similar materials have the same price. Short text, material group, target quantity, order unit, and price, but no material number. The exact material or description. A material master record can be used if its material group and PO unit match the contract item.
W — Material group Value contract only. Best when materials belong to one material group but have different prices. Material group and broad description of what the agreement covers. The exact article, quantity, price, and material number when applicable. An info record can propose conditions. A free-text item needs a valid account assignment.
psychology
Assessment shortcut

M = same group, same price. W = same group, different prices. M works in quantity and value contracts; W works only in value contracts.

Header conditions can still apply to release orders. For example, a supplier-wide discount maintained at contract header level can be calculated automatically when the buyer creates a release PO.

Scheduling agreements turn the agreement into a dated delivery plan.

A scheduling agreement is always plant-specific. Its total quantity is split into schedule lines, each with a quantity and delivery date. Schedule lines can be entered manually, created with reference to a purchase requisition, or generated by MRP.

RuleWhat to rememberWhy it matters
Plant scope A scheduling agreement is plant-specific. The delivery plan belongs to one receiving plant rather than acting as a cross-plant umbrella agreement.
M and W Item categories M and W are contract-specific and are not used in scheduling agreements. A scheduling agreement needs a more concrete item basis for continuous delivery scheduling.
Conditions Conditions can cover the full validity period or, with the appropriate document type, several time-dependent validity periods. Long-running agreements can model commercial changes without creating a new agreement for every price period.
Central contract reference If the scheduling agreement references a centrally agreed contract, the central contract remains the pricing authority. Changing the local scheduling-agreement conditions would break the intended central-price control.
Subcontracting Components to be provided can be maintained for the delivery dates. The schedule controls not only the finished delivery but also the component timing needed for subcontracting.

Account-assignment boundary: the current SAP Learning contract-management lesson states that account assignment category U — Unknown is not allowed in scheduling agreements. Current SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS01 Help for the Manage Purchase Scheduling Agreements app lists Unknown among the available account-assignment categories. Treat this as release and UI scope, not as a universal rule. For a course-based assessment, answer in the course scope; for a project, verify the target release and app.

With or without release documentation

The document type controls whether the scheduling agreement works with release documentation. The common learning examples are LP without release documentation and LPA with release documentation.

Without release documentationWith release documentation
The saved schedule lines are the supplier-facing delivery plan. Changes should be communicated quickly because the system does not keep the same detailed release history. Schedule lines first act as the internal plan. The supplier is informed only when an explicit scheduling-agreement release is created.
Useful when the current schedule itself is the agreed message to the supplier. Useful when Purchasing needs a traceable history of what was sent and when.
No separate forecast or JIT release is required for every transmission. Releases can be created as forecast schedules or JIT schedules, depending on the process.

MRP can remove the manual call-off step.

Scheduling agreements are especially useful when demand is repetitive and transaction volume is high. In the SAP Learning scope, MRP can generate schedule lines automatically when the material and planning settings allow it. A source list is mainly needed to control priority when more than one valid scheduling agreement exists for the same material and plant. With MRP Live, a valid scheduling agreement source leads directly to schedule lines instead of an extra requisition-to-PO conversion step.

schedule

Delivery-date edge case: SAP Learning notes that a goods receipt before the schedule-line date is only possible when confirmations are used or the over-delivery tolerance is unlimited.

Lead decision: use a contract when each call-off should remain a separate purchasing commitment. Use a scheduling agreement when stable, repeated demand benefits from one agreement plus a continuously updated delivery schedule.

Stock vs direct consumption

The key difference is where value should live after procurement.

Stock procurement creates inventory first. Direct consumption procurement charges a known receiver such as a cost center, asset, order, or project. This one decision changes master-data requirements, account assignment, goods-receipt behavior, and FI postings.

Decision pointProcurement for stockDirect consumption
Material master Required. The material must exist because SAP manages the stock quantity and usually its value. Optional. You can buy with a material master or enter a short text, material group, and order unit manually.
Account assignment No manual account assignment for normal valuated stock procurement. Stock account determination follows the material and valuation context. Required for the consumption purpose. The PO identifies the account assignment category, receiver, and relevant consumption account.
Value at receipt A valuated GR increases inventory value and normally posts against GR/IR. A valuated GR charges the assigned object instead of ordinary stock. A non-valuated GR records receipt without posting cost at that moment.
Quantity and value update Stock quantity and stock value are updated according to the material's inventory and valuation settings. No stock-value update. Quantity may also stay outside inventory management, depending on the material type and process design.
Business meaning “Receive an asset into inventory now; consume it later.” “We already know what business object should carry the cost.”
psychology

Assessment memory: stock procurement separates receipt from later consumption. Direct consumption decides the receiver during purchasing.

A stock material can also be purchased directly for consumption. The material type alone does not decide the business intent; the PO account assignment changes the value flow.

Consumable material can exist with or without a material master.

OptionInventory behaviorWhy use it
No material master No reusable material record. Description, material group, and PO unit are entered in the purchasing document. Useful for occasional consumables that do not justify a dedicated master record.
NLAG — Non-stock material Standard S4500 example with no quantity-based or value-based inventory management. Useful when the same consumable is purchased often and reusable purchasing data should be stored.
UNBW — Non-valuated material Quantity can be managed, but inventory is not managed on a value basis. Useful when physical stock should be visible even though financial stock valuation is not required.
Normal stock material + account assignment The material normally supports stock management, but this PO item is procured for direct consumption. Useful when a standard material is needed for a specific cost center, asset, project, sales-order context, or other receiver.

Configuration boundary: NLAG and UNBW are standard examples. Material types and their quantity/value update controls can be configured, so verify the target system before treating a material-type rule as universal.

Account assignment

The category says what receives the cost; the object identifies the receiver.

CategoryRequired business objectG/L account behaviorTypical use
K — Cost center Cost center The consumption G/L account is required. Customizing can allow SAP to propose it automatically. Office supplies, operating consumables, and other departmental costs.
A — Asset Asset number The G/L account is derived from the asset. In the standard learning scenario, the user does not enter it manually. Capital purchases that should be posted to an asset.

One PO item can be split across several receivers.

Multiple account assignment is useful when one ordered quantity or value belongs to more than one cost object. The planned distribution can be defined by quantity, percentage, or amount.

Distribution basisWhat the buyer definesExample
QuantityEach receiver gets a defined quantity.60 EA to Cost Center A and 40 EA to Cost Center B.
PercentageEach receiver gets a percentage of the total.70% to Operations and 30% to Engineering.
AmountEach receiver gets a defined share of the PO value.USD 7,000 to one project and USD 3,000 to another.

If only part of the order is delivered or invoiced, SAP also needs a rule for distributing that partial value. The two standard learning patterns are proportional distribution, which follows the planned ratio, and progressive fill-up, which completes the first account assignment before moving to the next one.

The partial-invoice indicator can be defaulted from the account assignment category in Customizing. For a PO item with multiple account assignment and a valuated GR, this indicator can also influence how a partial GR value is distributed. Treat the exact behavior as configuration-dependent and verify the PO and account-assignment settings before troubleshooting the FI result.

For an account-assigned PO item, the buyer can also control whether goods receipt is valuated, non-valuated, or not expected. With a valuated GR, the commitment is reduced already at goods receipt. This timing matters when Finance or Controlling asks why commitment and actual values changed before the supplier invoice arrived.

Extension boundary: BAdI MB_ACCOUNTING_DISTRIBUTE can change the distribution among account-assignment objects during goods receipt, but it cannot replace the account-assignment objects themselves. BAdI MRM_DISTRIBUTION_MAA supports customer-specific distribution logic during invoice verification. A subcontracting item with multiple account assignment requires a valuated goods receipt.

Purchase requisition → source → purchase order

The requisition states the internal need. The purchase order must resolve the commercial commitment.

A purchase requisition is an internal request for a material or service, quantity, and required date. It can be created manually by the requesting department or generated by another process. Standard examples include MRP, maintenance orders, production orders, and networks.

1. Requirement

The PR records what is needed and when. It can later become an RFQ, purchase order, or outline agreement.

2. Source of supply

Purchasing determines where the requirement should be sourced. The source can be an external supplier or an internal supplying plant.

3. Commitment

The PO resolves supplier or supplying plant, price, account assignment, delivery terms, and the other values needed for execution.

PR situationWhat happens during PO creationLead-level point
Account assignment category U — Unknown The PR can keep the receiver open. In the current S4500 learning path, U is allowed in the PO only with Service and Limit item types; otherwise a valid account assignment must be entered before the PO can be completed. Unknown is a temporary requirement state, not a license to leave normal consumption accounting unresolved.
Material master exists; no source assigned If a supplier/material info record exists, SAP can propose its purchase price. If no info record exists, the buyer enters the price. The PR valuation price is not simply copied as the PO purchase price in this case. Do not confuse PR valuation with the final commercial price source.
No material master and no source assigned SAP can propose the valuation price from the PR as the PO purchase price. The buyer can change the proposal. Without master-data price context, the PR estimate can become the starting point for the PO.

External sources can be represented by purchasing info records and outline agreement items. These objects can provide both the supplier and commercial context. An internal plant can also be the source, which moves the process into stock transport rather than normal external procurement.

Fiori recall: Process Purchase Requisitions (V2) is one standard app for processing requisition items, assigning a source of supply, and creating follow-on purchasing documents. App scope can vary by release and deployment.

rule

Diagnostic rule: when the PO supplier or price looks wrong, first ask whether the PR already had a source. Then separate source determination from price determination. Fix the rule that produced the value instead of editing the PO output without evidence.

Source determination

First identify the runtime. MRP and manual purchasing do not use one universal search sequence.

Source determination answers where the requirement should be supplied from. It is separate from price determination: SAP does not use condition technique to choose the source, so there is no pricing-style condition analysis that explains why a source was selected.

alt_route

Lead rule: start with the runtime, material, plant, relevant date, and procurement type. Then reconstruct the valid quota, Source List, outline agreement, and Purchasing Info Record evidence.

The EFB user parameter for purchasing function authorizations can also be used to prevent selected users from manually assigning sources of supply.

Automated procurement

Automate the handoffs only when every next document can be created without a missing decision.

The useful end-to-end model is MRP → purchase requisition with source → automatic purchase order → goods receipt → evaluated receipt settlement. Each step removes manual work, but each step also needs stronger master data and clearer process rules.

memory

Automation chain: MRP finds the shortage and plans supply. Source determination assigns where to buy. Automatic PO creation converts an eligible requisition. Goods receipt proves the delivered quantity. ERS creates the supplier invoice from PO and GR data instead of waiting for a supplier invoice.

Lead rule: automation does not remove decisions. It moves them into master data, source rules, document defaults, and scheduled jobs.

1. MRP automates requirement determination.

MRP monitors stock and requirements, calculates shortages, determines the procurement quantity, schedules the proposal, and can determine a source of supply. In SAP S/4HANA, the planning level is the MRP area. A plant always has a plant MRP area; additional MRP areas can represent storage locations or subcontractors.

TopicWhat SAP decidesAssessment point
Net requirements Whether available stock and fixed receipts are enough for the requirement. A shortage is the trigger for a procurement proposal; MRP does not start with supplier selection.
Lot sizing How much to procure, for example lot-for-lot, fixed lot size, or replenishment to maximum stock. The shortage quantity and the procurement quantity can be different because the lot-sizing procedure controls the proposal.
Scheduling When Purchasing must act, when the supplier should deliver, and when the material becomes available. Purchasing processing time, planned delivery time, and goods-receipt processing time are important scheduling components.
Source determination For external procurement, which valid source can supply the material. Typical source objects are a purchasing info record, an outline agreement item, or a supplying plant.

For externally procured material, procurement type F tells MRP to plan external supply. MRP Live creates delivery schedule lines when a valid scheduling agreement exists and otherwise creates purchase requisitions for externally procured material. In classic MRP, the planning setup can create a planned order or a purchase requisition; scheduling agreement schedule lines can also be generated directly when the process is configured for them.

MRP 2 data controls important parts of the proposal.

route

MRP sequence to remember: shortage → lot size → dates → source. By the time an automatically convertible PR is ready, quantity, date, source, and commercial data must be sufficiently complete for Purchasing.

2. Automatic PR-to-PO conversion needs a complete purchase requisition.

A requisition can be converted automatically only when the system does not need a buyer to supply a missing decision. A valid source of supply must be assigned, the supplier must be known, commercial data must be available, and the requisition must be ready for follow-on processing.

PrerequisiteWhy it matters
Automatic PO indicator in material masterFor a normal material-based PR, the plant-specific purchasing data must allow automatic PO generation.
Automatic PO indicator in supplier masterThe supplier purchasing data for the relevant purchasing organization must allow automatic PO generation.
Valid, unique assigned sourceThe PR must already know which source should be used. Automatic PO creation is not a replacement for an unresolved sourcing decision.
Commercial data availableThe assigned source must provide the data needed for the PO, such as supplier and price. An info record is common, but it is not universally mandatory when another valid purchasing reference provides the commercial context.
PR approved / releasedThe requisition item must have completed the applicable approval workflow and be eligible for follow-on processing.
No missing manual entriesAccount assignment, tax-relevant data, document defaults, and other mandatory fields must be complete enough for conversion without user input.

Important distinction: a Purchasing Info Record is not a universal prerequisite for every PR-to-PO conversion. A contract or another valid purchasing reference can provide the commercial context. For the separate PO-at-goods-receipt mechanism described below, however, a valid supplier-material info record is required.

3. Automatic PO creation at goods receipt is a different mechanism.

SAP can also generate a purchase order when goods arrive without an existing PO reference. This is an Inventory Management scenario, not ME59N and not PR conversion. The goods receipt and the new PO are created together when the required configuration and master data are complete.

warning

Assessment trap: “automatic PO” can mean two different processes. PR → PO starts from a released requisition and uses ME59N or a scheduled purchasing job. GR → PO starts from an unexpected warehouse receipt without a PO reference and needs movement-type configuration plus a valid info record.

4. ERS automates invoice creation after goods receipt.

Evaluated Receipt Settlement (ERS) is a self-billing process. The supplier agrees not to send an invoice for the relevant purchase orders. SAP creates the supplier invoice from the purchase order and the related receipt data, and the supplier can receive a settlement message instead.

The amount is based on four main inputs: the unsettled goods-receipt quantity from PO history, the PO price, the tax information in the PO item, and the payment terms in the PO header.

Control pointWhat must be trueWhy ERS can fail
Supplier master The Evaluated Receipt Settlement indicator is set in the supplier purchasing-organization data. Without supplier approval for ERS, the PO item is not prepared for automatic settlement.
PO header A valid Terms of Payment key is present. In the classic configuration model, the key must support a default baseline-date rule. The automatically created invoice needs complete payment conditions.
PO item ERS relevance, GR-based invoice verification, and a valid tax code are available. GR-based invoice verification links settlement to the individual receipt history instead of only to the PO quantity.
Estimated Price The Estimated Price indicator is not set in the PO item. ERS needs a firm purchasing price for automatic valuation.
Goods receipt The GR is posted with reference to the purchase order. ERS selects unsettled receipt quantities from PO history; an unrelated stock posting is not enough.
Purchasing info record The No ERS indicator is not blocking the supplier/material relationship unless the user intentionally overrides that proposal. This indicator can suppress ERS defaulting for one material even when ERS is active in the supplier master.
Settlement output The required settlement-message/output settings exist for the deployment. Classic Logistics Invoice Verification uses message determination; the exact output framework can differ by deployment and release.

Execution: use Create Evaluated Receipt Settlement for an online run or Schedule Supplier Invoice Jobs - Advanced (F1683) for scheduled processing. In classic backend processing, transaction MRRL is the ERS transaction and program RMMR1MRS can run the settlement in the background.

Document split: ERS can create settlement documents per supplier, per purchase order, per PO item, or per delivery document / service entry sheet. Run a test selection first when changing selection or split logic; MRRL stores a settlement log that shows processed and rejected items.

local_shipping

Lead rule: ERS works best when the supplier agreement is explicit, PO prices are current, and goods receipts are posted quickly and accurately. The automation removes supplier invoice entry; it does not remove the supplier invoice document, tax, payment, or reconciliation controls.

Self-service procurement

The requester can create demand and follow it without becoming the buyer.

Self-service procurement gives employees a simple Fiori entry point for their own purchasing needs. The employee creates the purchase requisition and follows its status. Purchasing still owns the sourcing and purchase-order commitment unless the process is configured differently.

Create

My Purchase Requisitions - New lets an employee create a requisition from catalog content, material or service data, free text, or a limit item. Ordering the shopping cart creates the purchase requisition.

Track

The requester can see the requisition status and follow-on documents. Own requisitions can be edited or deleted only while their processing status still allows it.

Receive

After a purchase order exists and the material arrives, the requester can confirm the receipt. A return can also be started when delivered goods have an issue.

OptionUseAssessment point
Catalog / cross-content search Select a predefined material or service from content available to the requester. The catalog improves control and reduces free-text demand. The exact catalog solution is landscape-specific.
Create Item Create a material, service, or free-text requisition item. A material master is not required for every self-service request; free-text procurement is possible where the process allows it.
Create Limit Item Request spend within a value limit when the exact material or service cannot be described completely in advance. Do not confuse a limit item with normal quantity-based stock procurement.

Defaults reduce manual input, but they are still process data.

User defaults can provide values such as plant, purchasing group, account assignment category, or cost center. A user can maintain selected defaults, and administrators can maintain defaults for several users with the Default Settings for Users app. In landscapes integrated with HR organizational structure, self-service procurement attributes can also be derived from the employee assignment.

The requester can also be changed when the app is used on behalf of another employee, for example when an assistant creates a requisition for a manager. The important control is the business requester, not only the person who entered the document.

For a stock material, the self-service process can also create a reservation together with the purchase requisition when this function is configured.

Catalog integration is not limited to the self-service app. Catalogs can also be connected to purchase-requisition and purchase-order processing such as ME51N and ME21N. In classic Customizing, the connection is maintained under Purchasing → Environment Data → Web Services: ID and Description; selected catalog items are then copied back into the purchasing document.

The dedicated Confirm Receipt of Goods - New app shows self-service material requisition items that are ready for requester confirmation. Commercial terms

Price determination is a condition-technique problem, not a single price field.

A purchase-order price can depend on supplier, purchasing organization, reference document, purchasing conditions, order unit, quantity, scales, and validity date. The useful Lead question is therefore not only “What is the price?” but “Which pricing rule found this value, and why?”

ObjectJobExample question
Condition record Stores the maintained commercial value and, for time-dependent conditions, its validity and scales. Which supplier/material record, contract condition, or general condition was valid on the pricing date?
Condition type Defines what the condition means and how it behaves: price, discount, surcharge, calculation rule, sign, scale basis, and other controls. Is this the gross price, a discount, freight, or another price element?
Access sequence Defines where SAP searches, and in which order, for a valid condition record for a condition type. Did SAP find a plant-specific agreement before a contract or purchasing info record?
Calculation schema Orders the relevant condition types and controls how they contribute to subtotals, net price, and the final purchasing price. Which conditions were allowed, in what sequence, and on which calculation base?

Standard recall: PB00 is the standard quantity-dependent gross-price condition type used in the SAP Learning example. RM0000 is the standard calculation schema for purchase-price determination in purchasing documents. In classic MM purchasing configuration, schema determination uses the schema groups of the purchasing organization and supplier. The supplier schema group is maintained in the purchasing-organization data of the Business Partner.

Purchase-order control

A complete PO still has to become an executable commitment.

The purchase order brings the sourcing and commercial decisions into a supplier-facing document, but it can still be subject to approval and output controls. In current SAP S/4HANA, flexible workflow is available for purchase requisitions and purchase orders alongside other approval mechanisms that may exist in a customer landscape. The active model therefore has to be identified before an approval problem is diagnosed.

Keep document validity, approval, and supplier communication distinct. A PO can be structurally correct but still wait for approval. An approved PO can still have a communication problem. Conversely, receiving a supplier response does not prove that the accounting or receipt design of the item is correct.

Approval and output are different states.

After the PO is created and approved, the supplier still needs the intended document version. Supplier confirmations and exception handling

Output proves what we sent. Confirmation proves what the supplier says will happen.

Keep four layers separate: purchase-order output, supplier confirmation, delivery execution, and exception notification. They are connected, but each layer has its own evidence and control logic.

Receipt and acceptance

Execution depends on what the item was designed to represent.

For a standard stock purchase, goods receipt records that the ordered material arrived and updates the relevant inventory and purchase-order history. For direct consumption, the same business event can post value to an assigned object instead of ordinary stock. Service procurement can introduce an acceptance or service-entry step. Special item categories intentionally change the baseline again.

For the plain material process, post the goods receipt with reference to the purchase order. SAP proposes the open PO items, so receiving can check the material, delivered quantity, over- or underdelivery, and — when configured — shelf-life requirements. One PO item can produce several GR items when the delivery is split by batch or storage location.

The posting automatically updates PO history. A material document proves the goods movement; if the receipt is valuated, SAP also creates an accounting document. The standard baseline movement type for a goods receipt against a PO is 101.

PO / GR designQuantity effectValue effect at GRDocuments
Stock material + valuated GR Stock quantity increases. Inventory value is updated and FI is posted, normally with stock against GR/IR. Material document + accounting document.
Consumable item + valuated GR The receipt is recorded for the PO item. The value is charged to the assigned consumption or balance-sheet object rather than normal stock. Material document + accounting document.
Consumable item + non-valuated GR The receipt and PO history are updated. No cost is posted by the GR. The invoice becomes the later value-posting event. Material document; no FI document for the GR itself.
Consumable item with no GR planned No goods-receipt event is posted. Invoice verification is the first normal value-posting event for the purchase. Invoice document + accounting document at IR.

The exact G/L accounts still depend on account assignment, valuation, and automatic account determination. Use the Material Valuation deep dive when the assessment question moves from process design to the FI entries created by GR and IR.

The movement type is not just a label. It is a three-character control key that influences quantity and value updates, field selection, stock or consumption behavior, and — together with other account-determination factors — which G/L accounts are affected.

inventory_2

Fiori baseline: use Post Goods Receipt for Purchasing Document (F0843) for the simple PO-referenced receipt. The reference is important because it connects receiving, Purchasing, invoice verification, and PO history to the same business event.

SAP's standard item-category model makes this visible. Standard items can have both goods and invoice receipts. Stock-transfer items expect a goods receipt but no supplier invoice receipt. Consignment items receive supplier-owned stock without a normal PO invoice receipt at that point. Subcontracting adds the provided-component relationship to the receipt process. The useful question is therefore not “Why is there no GR or IR?” until the intended item category and process variant are known.

If warehouse execution or quality inspection sits between physical arrival and unrestricted stock, continue with the Inventory Management, EWM, or Quality Management guide rather than forcing those details into the PO model.

Supplier invoice

Invoice verification compares a commercial claim with the purchasing history.

The supplier invoice is a separate business event from receipt. With purchase-order reference, SAP can allocate an invoice to purchasing documents and, depending on the scenario, to related references such as delivery notes or service entry sheets. Quantity, value, tax, and tolerance checks determine whether the invoice can be posted cleanly or needs exception handling.

In the plain PO process, the user enters the supplier invoice header data such as gross amount, tax, invoice date, and supplier reference. With PO reference, SAP can propose the invoicing party, payment terms, material, open invoice quantity, and expected item amount from the PO and receipt history.

Posting creates the logistics invoice document and an accounting document and updates the purchasing history. Logistics Invoice Verification checks the commercial claim; it does not execute the supplier payment or manage the open liability after posting. Those tasks belong to Financial Accounting.

If price, quantity, or other configured variances exceed tolerance, SAP can still post the invoice but block it for payment, depending on the tolerance setup. This is an important distinction: “posted” and “payable” are not the same status.

The reference model matters. Where goods-receipt-based invoice verification is active, the invoice is matched against the relevant goods receipts rather than only against the ordered quantity. Other procurement variants have different matching rules. Do not infer the intended invoice behavior from the existence of a PO alone.

See SAP's current Entering Invoices with Purchase Order Reference documentation for the supported allocation choices in Logistics Invoice Verification.

Execution and verification tools

MIGO proves a goods movement, MMBE proves the stock view, and MIRO processes the supplier invoice.

These tools answer different questions. Using the right evidence prevents a common support mistake: checking one screen and assuming it proves the whole procure-to-pay chain.

ToolPrimary questionEvidence
MIGO What goods movement should be posted, displayed, or reversed? Material document, movement type, posting date, quantities, reference document, account assignment, and accounting document when the movement is valuated.
MMBE Where is the current stock of one material? Stock quantities by organizational level and stock type, with views for plant, storage location, batch, valuation type, and special stock where relevant.
MIRO How should the supplier invoice be matched and posted? Invoice header, PO or other allocation reference, proposed invoice items, quantity/value comparison, balance, invoice status, PO history, and the resulting FI liability.

MIGO: choose the business action and the reference.

MIGO is a single-screen goods-movement transaction. Depending on the chosen action, it can handle goods receipt, goods issue, return delivery, transfer posting, display, or cancellation. Its overview tree shows recently used documents; it is not a document search tool. Use the dedicated search function when you need to find a document.

MMBE: stock state is not the same as purchasing history.

MMBE gives a stock overview for one material across organizational levels and stock types. It is useful to confirm the inventory result after a movement. If the question is whether a specific PO item was received or invoiced, PO history and the follow-on documents remain the stronger evidence.

MIRO: zero balance proves arithmetic, not the full business correctness.

MIRO can process an invoice, credit memo, subsequent debit, or subsequent credit. With a PO reference, the system proposes relevant items and shows the balance and invoice status. A zero balance means the invoice is arithmetically balanced; tolerance checks can still influence whether the invoice is accepted normally or blocked for payment.

warning

Exam trap: the Booking OK indicator in MIRO is not the same as the OK indicator in MIGO. In MIRO it marks an item as checked/processed and does not decide whether the item is considered when the invoice is posted.

Lead habit: trace PO history and documents first; use transaction screens as evidence views, not as the process model.

Financial reconciliation

GR/IR is a timing bridge, not a generic error bucket.

In procurement scenarios where goods receipt and invoice receipt are both value-relevant, the two events can occur on different dates or for different quantities. GR/IR carries that temporary difference. An open balance can therefore mean that the complementary event has not happened yet, not that the account is broken.

SAP's current GR/IR documentation makes the expectation explicit: if invoiced quantity is greater than received quantity, the system expects further goods receipts; if received quantity is greater than invoiced quantity, it expects further invoices. Manual clearing belongs to the case where the business has established that no further receipt or invoice will arrive.

This is where procurement hands the story to Finance. Use the Material Valuation deep dive for standard price, moving average price, stock coverage, and the FI postings at GR and IR. Procurement analytics

KPIs show where spend, contracts, requisitions, and purchase orders need attention.

Procurement analytics turns purchasing documents and accounting data into control signals. For assessment questions, do not only name the KPI. Explain what population it measures, what business risk it exposes, and what action a buyer should take next.

Spend and control

Check where money is spent, whether purchasing uses purchase orders and contracts, and whether document values remain aligned.

Contracts

Check expiry, usage, leakage, and how much of a quantity or value contract has already been consumed.

Execution

Check requisition touch, cycle time, overdue purchase orders, delivery performance, and invoice-price variance.

psychology
Assessment shortcut

Non-managed spend means spend without a purchase order. Off-contract spend means purchasing outside contract coverage. Contract leakage is more specific: a valid contract existed and could have been used, but the purchase order did not reference it.

One connected example

The supplier changed, then the price changed too.

A buyer expects Supplier A for a material, but a requisition is converted with Supplier B and a higher PO price. It is tempting to repair both values directly in the PO. That produces a clean-looking document and weak evidence.

Start with the requirement context: material, plant, purchasing organization, quantity, and date. If the source list makes Supplier B fixed for that validity period, the supplier result is explained. Now inspect the commercial context for B. If the purchasing info record has conditions for a different order unit or a current scale that produces the higher value, the price result is explained too.

The important point is not the exact master-data combination. It is the separation of decisions. Source determination answers why B was selected. Price determination answers why B's commercial value was used. If the business expected A, fix the source rule or its validity. If B is correct but the price is wrong, fix the relevant purchasing condition or reference. Editing both outputs without proving either decision leaves the next requirement exposed to the same defect.

Specific procurement processes

Change the baseline deliberately: invoice timing, quantity control, component ownership, or financial settlement.

These processes are easier to remember when we ask which standard procurement event has changed. The PO still provides the commercial control point, but receipt, invoice timing, account assignment, or component flow can work differently.

Invoicing plans: periodic versus partial

An invoicing plan is part of a purchase order; it is not an independent purchasing document. It defines planned invoice dates independently of the actual goods receipt or service performance. You cannot request an invoicing plan in a purchase requisition, and the Manage Purchase Orders Fiori app does not create or edit invoicing plans.

PlanBest fitValue logicAssessment point
Periodic Recurring charges such as rent, leasing, or subscriptions. The recurring amount is settled on the generated due dates. Think repeat the charge by period, not split one project total into milestones.
Partial Projects or other staged procurement. The PO item total is split across dates by percentage (billing rule 1) or amount (billing rule 2). Down-payment dates can be marked with billing rules 4 or 5; those dates are not automatically settled as normal invoice-plan items.

Partial-plan dates can also come from network invoicing or billing plans when payment is event-dependent. After an invoicing-plan type is assigned to the item, it cannot simply be changed to another plan type.

Settlement: classic processing uses MRIS; Fiori provides Create Invoicing Plan Settlement. Periodic plan dates can be updated with MLRP or Update Invoicing Plans – Purchase Order. Credit-memo output for the classic process can be handled with MR90. Automatic settlement also depends on the relevant supplier, account-assignment, tax, and goods-receipt settings, so the plan date alone is not enough.

Blanket and limit purchase orders: control value and validity instead of exact quantity.

A blanket or limit item is useful for repeated low-value procurement when creating a separate quantity-based PO item for every transaction would cost more than it controls. The process normally has no goods receipt or service entry for each individual consumption; invoices are checked against the PO validity and value limits.

Subcontracting: buy the finished result while providing components.

Item category L — Subcontracting tells SAP that the supplier manufactures or processes the ordered material with components provided by the buying company. The ordered material itself does not need a material master, but the components that we provide do.

MRP prerequisites

  • Set the Subcontracting special procurement type in the material master MRP 2 view when MRP should create the subcontracting proposal.
  • Provide a valid subcontracting source: for example, a subcontracting info record or an outline-agreement item with category L. Use a source list or quota arrangement when several sources compete.
  • For automatic component determination, maintain a BOM, a matching production version, and the production version in the subcontracting info record at purchasing-organization/plant level.

Planning and execution

  • MRP explodes the BOM and creates dependent component requirements shown as SubReq.
  • MRP Live (MD01N) creates purchase requisitions for externally procured materials; classic MRP (MD01/MD03) can still create planned orders.
  • A supplier MRP area can separate component planning. ME2ON / the Subcontracting Cockpit helps monitor provided stock and requirements.
  • Goods receipt of the subcontracted material automatically posts consumption of the provided components. Proposed consumption can be corrected at GR or later.

Change behavior: changing the PO or schedule-line quantity changes component quantities proportionally unless Fixed Quantity is set. Changing the delivery date recalculates the component requirement date, but it does not select a new component structure; run a new BOM explosion when the BOM must be redetermined.

Process variants

Use standard external procurement as the baseline, then name what changes.

The standard material flow is useful because it shows all major boundaries: requirement, external supplier, PO, goods receipt, invoice receipt, and reconciliation. Special procurement is easier to understand when we compare each variant with that baseline instead of memorizing a separate list of transactions.

Stock transfer

The supplying source is internal. SAP's standard item category expects goods receipt but not a supplier invoice receipt.

Consignment

Material is received into separately managed supplier-owned stock. The ordinary PO receipt is not the invoice event.

Subcontracting

The supplier performs work using components provided by the customer. Receipt of the subcontracted material is connected to consumption of those provided components.
