Special sales / immediate execution

Rush Order accelerates delivery. Cash Sales accelerates payment.

Both processes are fast, but they optimize different business events. Rush order keeps normal customer receivables and later invoicing. Cash sales assumes immediate payment and uses a different billing and accounting model.

Rush Order

C1000 needs P-100 today, but normal billing still applies.

The order is designed to create or trigger the delivery immediately. Warehouse execution and PGI happen quickly, while the invoice follows the delivery later.

Rush sales order→ Immediate delivery→ Pick / PGI→ Delivery-related invoice→ Customer AR

Cash Sales

The customer pays now, so the accounting model changes.

In the classic standard process, order type BV uses immediate delivery type BV. The system can create the delivery in the background and print the cash-sale invoice/receipt from the order. The later order-related billing run posts the transaction to Financial Accounting without creating a normal open customer receivable.

BV cash sale→ Immediate payment / receipt→ BV delivery→ PGI→ Order-related billing / cash posting
receipt_long

Subtle point: the customer can receive a printed cash-sale invoice immediately, while the accounting billing document is processed later. Do not confuse the printed receipt with the timing of the FI posting document.

Configuration map

Immediate delivery and immediate payment are configured in different places.

Pitfalls

“Fast” is not a configuration requirement by itself.

Rush does not mean cash

A rush customer can still have ordinary payment terms and AR.

Cash does not bypass logistics

Stock still has to be available, delivered and posted as goods issue.

Immediate delivery needs clean master data

A missing shipping point, ATP confirmation or delivery block defeats the whole purpose of the process.

Printed receipt ≠ FI posting timing

Cash-sale output can be immediate while order-related billing completes the accounting process later.
