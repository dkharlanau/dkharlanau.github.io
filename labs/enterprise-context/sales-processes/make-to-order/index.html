Special fulfillment / SD + PP + CO

The sales order does not consume finished stock. It creates the reason to produce.

Make-to-Order links customer demand to production and, in common designs, to sales-order-specific stock. The central control is not one item category. It is the requirements type and requirements class that connect Sales to planning, stock assignment, valuation and costing.

Memory model

01DemandSales order
02SupplyProduction / procurement
03ESales order stock
Valuated and non-valuated variants exist. Do not assume every MTO design posts costs the same way.

Assessment recall

Start from the demand-to-supply link.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: In Make-to-Order, the customer sales order creates an individual requirement instead of being fulfilled from anonymous finished-goods stock. The requirements type and requirements class control how this demand is planned, whether sales order stock is used, how that stock is valuated, and whether costs and revenues are collected on the sales order item. Production or procurement creates the supply for that order. After goods receipt, the stock is available for the same sales order, then normal delivery, goods issue, and billing follow. If MTO fails, I check requirements determination before I check the production order.

Synthetic example

C1000 orders a customer-specific machine from DE01.

Material M-500 is not built for anonymous warehouse stock. The order in sales organization DE10 creates the individual demand. Plant DE11 plans and produces the machine for this order.

Sales order→ Individual requirement→ MRP / planned order→ Production order→ GR to sales order stock E→ Delivery → PGI → F2
lock

Core consequence: sales order stock with special stock indicator E belongs to a specific sales order item. Another customer order should not silently consume it as ordinary unrestricted stock.

Configuration map

Requirements control is the center of MTO.

Strategy group 20 is a common standard Make-to-Order strategy, but the exact design can differ. For assessment and troubleshooting, explain the control chain instead of treating one strategy key as the process definition.

Important variants

MTO is a family, not one fixed accounting model.

Valuated sales order stock

Stock quantity and value can be managed specifically for the sales order. SAP supports separate valuation with reference to the sales document.

Non-valuated sales order stock

Quantity remains sales-order-specific while cost collection follows the sales-order cost object design.

Variant Configuration

The configured sales item can drive characteristics into production. Keep configuration logic separate from the basic MTO stock and requirements logic.

External procurement

An MTO item can still procure components or even the finished item externally. That does not automatically make it a third-party customer shipment.

Troubleshooting

Read the chain from sales demand to usable supply.

SymptomFirst checks
No planned order / production proposalPlant, requirements type/class, strategy group, transfer of requirements, MRP run and planning file.
Production exists but GR is ordinary stockSales order assignment on the production/procurement object, requirements class, special stock indicator E.
Sales delivery cannot see finished stockGR quantity in the correct sales order stock, order/item assignment, batch/serial status, delivery due date.
ATP behaves like make-to-stockAvailability context and special-stock requirement segment; do not change ATP before proving the requirements model.
Costs or margin are wrongValuation indicator, consumption posting, sales-order costing, settlement and account assignment.

Extension readiness

Keep custom logic outside the core demand signal when possible.

Good extension candidates are additional configuration data, custom fields carried from the sales order into production, external configuration services, or side-by-side orchestration. Avoid replacing standard requirements determination with opaque code unless the business rule truly cannot be modeled in standard planning and classification.
