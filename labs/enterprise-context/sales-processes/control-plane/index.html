Assessment mode

A 60-second answer has a shape.

This is the structure I use when a question sounds deceptively simple: “Explain third-party processing” or “How does MTO work?”

01

Name the business change

Start with what differs from normal sell-from-stock: external supplier, order-specific stock, another company code, or delayed ownership transfer.

02

Name the control chain

Document type → item category → schedule line / requirements type. State the important standard key only when it helps explain behavior.

03

Follow the physical and financial flow

Where does the material go, whose stock is it, which movement or transfer-of-control event occurs, and what makes billing possible?

04

Finish with one failure and one test

Show that you can operate the design: “If no PR is created, I trace TAS → CS → PR control; my test asserts the generated requisition and document flow.”

High-value contrasts

Similar names hide different ownership models.

SD.TPO ↔ SD.PTO

Where does the supplier ship?

TPO: supplier → customer; your warehouse is skipped. PTO: supplier → your sales-order stock → customer; your inventory and delivery remain in the chain.

SD.ICO ↔ SD.AIC

Internal billing vs explicit orchestration

ICO: another company delivers and internal billing bridges the legal entities. AIC: generated intercompany PO/SO, VSiT and value-chain monitoring make the internal leg explicit.

SD.SFS ↔ SD.MTO

Same-looking SD item, different supply logic

TAN/CP can appear in both documented examples. MTO emerges from requirements type and planning strategy, proving why memorizing only item categories is insufficient.

SD.CON.FILL ↔ SD.CON.ISSUE

Location vs ownership

Fill-up: stock moves to customer special stock but remains yours. Issue: customer consumption reduces that stock and becomes a billing event.
