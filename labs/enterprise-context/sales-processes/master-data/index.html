Sales / data layer / {{ md.index.version }}

Sales behavior starts
before the order exists.

A sales document does not invent most of its business context. It reads a customer in a sales area, a product in the relevant distribution and plant views, customer-product relationships, hierarchy assignments, texts and validity-based records. Configuration then decides how those maintained inputs are interpreted. The useful question is not simply “which master record is wrong?” but “which maintained source supplied this value, at what scope, and which mechanism consumed it?”

The important boundary: data and decision logic are not the same thing.

A Business Partner or product is business master data. A customer-material record describes a maintained relationship. A price condition record is validity-based data. A sales area is organizational context. A pricing procedure or access sequence is configuration. A sales order is transactional data that receives, derives and can later carry its own values.

Scope before fields

The same customer or product can behave differently because the maintained scope is different.

Customer Sales data is maintained for a sales area, which combines sales organization, distribution channel and division. SAP documents that pricing data, delivery priority and shipping conditions can differ between sales areas. Product Sales data also has distribution-chain context, while plant data adds another layer for fulfillment. A value that looks “global” in an order may therefore have come from a much narrower source.

A concrete order

Several maintained layers can contribute to one sales item.

Imagine a customer ordering one product in a particular sales area. The Business Partner supplies customer and partner context. The product contributes its Sales and plant-specific attributes. A customer-material record can add a more specific customer-product reference such as the customer’s own material number. Pricing condition records can then be selected through configured condition technique. The resulting sales item contains document data; it should not be mistaken for the original master-data source.

Maintained source

Ask where the value is owned and at which key level it is valid: BP, sales area, product distribution chain, plant, customer-product relationship, hierarchy or condition record.

Runtime result

Then ask whether the sales document copied the value, derived it through a mechanism, or redetermined it later. Once it is in the document, its lifecycle is no longer identical to the master record.

Typed relationships

The graph shows which data specializes, feeds or constrains another layer.

This distinction matters more than memorizing a list of tables. A customer-material record specializes a customer-product relationship; a partner relationship supplies a candidate party; a condition record carries a value; a mechanism decides how those inputs are searched or interpreted.

Where this graph stops

Not every Sales-relevant object belongs in a master-data map.

Commercial agreements, output rules, product compliance and industry-specific assortments have different ownership and lifecycle. Keeping those boundaries visible makes this graph more useful: it can point to an adjacent layer without pretending that all Sales behavior is master data.
