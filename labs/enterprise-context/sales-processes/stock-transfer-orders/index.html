Cross-module process / MM + Shipping + Billing

An STO moves company stock. It is not a customer sale.

The receiving plant creates the demand through a stock transport purchase order. Shipping can execute the physical movement, and cross-company variants add internal billing because stock crosses a legal-entity boundary.

Decisive question: who requests the stock? In an STO, a receiving plant requests stock from a supplying plant. In intercompany sales, an external customer order drives fulfillment. Both can create outbound deliveries, but the business contract is different.

Intra-company STO

DE12 replenishes from DE11 inside company code DE01.

Synthetic example: DE12 is a distribution plant. It needs 50 pieces of P-100 from production plant DE11. Both plants belong to DE01, so there is no internal sale and no intercompany invoice.

Stock transport PO→ Delivery due list→ NL outbound delivery→ PGI 641→ Stock in transit→ GR 101 at DE12

No sales revenue is created. The accounting effect is an internal inventory movement inside the same company code.

Intercompany STO

FR11 requests stock from DE11, so the legal boundary adds billing.

Synthetic example: receiving plant FR11 belongs to FR01. Supplying plant DE11 belongs to DE01. FR01 creates the stock transport order. DE01 ships the stock and internally invoices FR01.

Stock transport PO in FR01→ NLCC delivery from DE11→ PGI 643→ Internal billing DE01 → FR01→ GR 101 at FR11→ Invoice verification

Configuration map

The STO setup belongs to Purchasing first, then Shipping and Billing join.

Do not memorize one purchase-order document type as universal. Classic systems often use UB for stock transport, while standard cross-company content can use another configured purchasing document type. The important object is the stock transport purchase order and its plant/shipping assignments.

Advanced Intercompany Stock Transfer

The stock transfer becomes a monitored cross-company value chain.

Advanced Intercompany Stock Transfer is designed for stock moves between different legal entities with explicit internal purchase and sales documents, intercompany customer and supplier invoice steps, stock in transit, and Value Chain Monitoring. Current SAP cloud content uses scope item 5HP.

Intercompany PO→ Background intercompany sales order→ Outbound delivery→ GI to stock in transit→ IC customer + supplier invoice→ GR / physical stock
timeline

Key advanced control: transfer-of-control dates can drive automated stock-in-transit movements. If an advanced process stops, open Monitor Value Chains (F4854) before manually repairing downstream documents.

Troubleshooting

“STO does not create a delivery” has a short diagnostic path.

  1. Confirm the purchasing document is really configured as an STO and has a supplying plant.
  2. Check the assigned delivery type and checking rule for the STO / supplying plant.
  3. Check shipping data for the supplying plant and the customer representing the receiving plant where required.
  4. Check material extension, loading group, shipping condition, and shipping point assignment.
  5. Run the delivery due list with the correct supplying plant and selection dates; read the log.
  6. For cross-company STO, check internal customer/supplier, supplying-plant sales area, billing copy control, and internal pricing.
  7. For Advanced Intercompany Stock Transfer, use Monitor Value Chains and inspect the first missing process step.
