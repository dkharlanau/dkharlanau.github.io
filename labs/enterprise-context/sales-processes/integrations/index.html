Sales integration / cross-application map

One order.
Several contracts.

A sales process can cross the system boundary as an IDoc, a B2B document, an API call, a SOAP message or a business event. These are not interchangeable names for the same thing. The useful question is: which business document moves, who waits for an answer, and who owns the next decision?

Production and project contrasts

“Made for the customer” is not one process.

The important distinction is which execution and cost object the customer demand creates. That is more useful than calling everything MTO and hoping nobody asks a second question.

SD.MTO

Ordinary Make-to-Order

Sales demand creates individual planning and customer-specific supply. MRP remains a central planning step.

SD.MTO.ATO

Assembly Processing

The sales order can create the assembly object directly. Component availability and production scheduling feed the customer promise early.

SD.ETO

Engineer-to-Order

The WBS/project becomes the engineering, stock, cost and execution backbone. Sales is the contract; project manufacturing owns the complex build.

SD.PROJ.PBS

Project-Based Services

The customer project records work, costs and billing evidence. Stock movement may not matter at all.

SD.SUB.RECUR

Recurring Physical Delivery

The long-lived subscription-style contract owns lifecycle. Generated sales orders execute individual delivery cycles.

SYNC ↔ ASYNC

The customer waiting is an architecture signal.

Pricing, ATP or credit may need a synchronous answer before the promise is shown. Notifications, indexing and side-by-side reactions usually do not. Making everything synchronous is a strangely expensive way to discover network latency.
