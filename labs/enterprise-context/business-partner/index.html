Deep vertical / SAP Business Partner

One party.
Several SAP contexts.

Business Partner gives SAP S/4HANA a shared identity for a person, organization, or group. Customer and supplier roles then add the accounting, sales, and purchasing data needed by real processes. CVI connects these layers to the customer and supplier master-data objects that SAP applications still use.

Assessment recall

Explain Business Partner as five layers, not as one master record.

For a Lead answer, keep the model compact: identity → role → organizational data → CVI → process use. This is enough to explain most design questions and to locate the first missing layer in an incident.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: In SAP S/4HANA, Business Partner is the leading object for customer and supplier master data. I separate the BP category, the business roles, and the organizational extensions because they answer different questions. The grouping controls the BP number-range strategy, while customer or supplier account groups belong to the synchronized customer or supplier object. CVI maps these objects and their numbering. A BP can therefore exist but still be unusable in Sales if the required FLCU01 sales-area data is missing. I also keep BP relationships separate from Sales partner functions: a relationship connects identities, while partner functions define who acts as sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, payer, or another process partner.

What is global?

The core identity: category, name, addresses, communication data, and other shared BP attributes.

What is contextual?

Company-code, sales-area, and purchasing-organization data. Process readiness can differ by organizational unit.

What is synchronized?

CVI connects Business Partner with the customer and supplier objects used by application processes.

Identity and roles

Business Partner is not a renamed customer or supplier screen.

The BP category describes the identity itself. Standard BP roles add the business context in which that identity can act. This is why one organization can be both a customer and a supplier without duplicating its shared identity data.

Category is about identity

Person, Organization, and Group describe what kind of party the BP represents. Category affects the available identity fields; it is not the same concept as a customer or supplier role.

Role is about business use

FLCU00 and FLVN00 add company-code accounting context. FLCU01 adds customer sales-area data. FLVN01 adds supplier purchasing-organization data. General BP data remains shared across those contexts.

A concrete example

The same company can sit on both sides of the trading relationship.

Suppose Alpine Components GmbH buys finished goods from us and also supplies packaging. If it is the same governed legal identity, a common design is one Organization BP with customer and supplier roles. Its name and address are shared; its Sales data belongs to a sales area, Purchasing data belongs to a purchasing organization, and accounting data belongs to company codes.

What stays shared

Identity data such as the legal name and general address should not be copied merely because the party has more than one business role. A change here can affect several processes.

What stays contextual

Sales, purchasing, and company-code data can be complete for one organizational unit and missing for another. “The BP exists” therefore does not prove that the partner is process-ready everywhere.

CVI and numbering

CVI synchronizes related objects; it does not collapse them into one table.

In SAP S/4HANA, Business Partner is the leading object for creating customer or supplier master data. Customer/Vendor Integration synchronizes BP with the customer and supplier objects used by downstream applications. The design therefore has to keep several concepts separate.

BP grouping

Grouping controls the BP number-range strategy. It belongs to the Business Partner layer.

Customer / supplier account group

Account groups classify the customer or supplier master record and influence its number assignment, field status, and permitted partner functions.

CVI mapping

CVI connects the BP grouping and role design to the customer or supplier account group and number assignment. Equal BP and customer/supplier numbers are possible, but they are a design choice with prerequisites rather than proof that the mapping is correct.

Current SAP documentation describes Business Partner as the leading object for customer/supplier master data and documents both grouping-to-account-group mapping and same-number or different-number assignment. See Business Partner Approach (Customer/Supplier Integration) and Number Assignment for Processing Direction BP to Customer/Vendor.

Organizational readiness

General data answers who the party is. Organizational data answers where it can transact.

This distinction explains many incidents that look mysterious from the BP overview. The identity can be correct while one required organizational slice is absent or incomplete.

Finance

FLCU00 and FLVN00 carry customer or supplier accounting data at company-code level, including controls required for posting and payment processes.

Sales

FLCU01 carries customer data for the sales area. A customer can therefore work in one sales organization / distribution channel / division and still be unavailable in another.

Procurement

FLVN01 carries supplier purchasing data by purchasing organization. A valid supplier identity does not automatically make the supplier usable by every purchasing organization.

Partner model

Partner function and BP relationship answer different questions.

In Sales, partner functions such as sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, and payer describe who performs a role in the commercial process. Procurement has its own partner functions. A BP relationship instead connects two BP identities — for example, an organization and a related contact or another organization. Treating the two mechanisms as interchangeable makes master-data design harder to reason about.

Process role

Use partner determination when the question is “who should act as ship-to, payer, goods supplier, or another process partner?”.

Identity relationship

Use BP relationships when the question is “how are these two business partners related?”. The relationship does not by itself replace Sales or Purchasing partner determination.

When the BP exists but the process still fails

Trace the missing context before changing shared master data.

A successful save proves that a BP record could be stored. It does not prove that every customer or supplier segment, organizational view, partner assignment, field requirement, or downstream replica is complete.

SDsales area

The customer works in one sales area but not another

Start with the FLCU01 sales-area extension and its required data. Do not change the general BP merely because the failing document uses the same party.

MMpurchasing org

The supplier exists but cannot be used by a purchasing organization

Check FLVN01 and the exact purchasing-organization data. Compare the failing organizational context with one that works.

CVImapping

The BP exists but the expected customer or supplier object does not

Check the BP role, grouping-to-account-group mapping, number assignment, mandatory fields, and the synchronized customer or supplier object before treating the issue as an application error.

Governance, extensibility and distribution

Shared identity data deserves wider impact analysis than a local sales or purchasing field.

Names, addresses, tax identifiers, bank data, and relationships can be reused by several roles. Governance should therefore distinguish shared identity from company-code, sales-area, and purchasing-organization ownership instead of applying one approval model to every BP change.

Custom fields

SAP exposes separate extensibility contexts for Business Partner, customer company, customer sales, supplier company, supplier purchasing organization, and other BP-related entities. Put a new attribute at the level that owns its meaning, then verify that the required UI and interface exposure are supported.

Integration

SAP's Business Partner integration documentation covers OData, IDoc, and SOAP interfaces, and Business Partner data can also participate in governed replication scenarios. Treat interface success as transport evidence; then reconcile keys, roles, organizational data, relationships, and extensions in the target.

MDG

SAP Master Data Governance can govern Business Partner data and replicate approved master data to client systems. The governance workflow should reinforce clear ownership; it should not hide uncertainty about whether a field is global or organizational.

Deployment boundary

Keep the data model stable; verify the configuration surface for the target system.

This article uses SAP S/4HANA 2025 FPS01 on-premise / private-edition documentation for concrete BP and CVI behavior. The underlying distinctions — identity, role, organizational data, customer/supplier synchronization, and process readiness — are durable. Exact configuration activities, apps, transactions, and extensibility options depend on the deployment model and release, so implementation steps should always be checked against the target system's current documentation.

FAQ / fast repetition

Six questions that expose the model quickly.

01numbering

BP grouping or customer account group?

Grouping belongs to the Business Partner layer and controls BP numbering. The customer or supplier account group belongs to the synchronized customer or supplier object and controls its classification, numbering, and field behavior.

02roles

FLCU00 or FLCU01?

FLCU00 is the customer company-code role for accounting data. FLCU01 is the customer Sales role and carries sales-area data.

03numbers

Must BP and customer numbers be identical?

No. Same-number assignment is supported, but it is a configuration choice with number-range prerequisites. Different numbers are also supported.

04partners

BP relationship or Sales partner function?

A BP relationship describes how two identities are related. A Sales partner function describes which party acts as sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, payer, or another role in the document process.

05diagnosis

The BP exists, but the sales order cannot use it. What first?

Check the required customer role and FLCU01 extension for the exact sales area, then the required sales data, partner setup, field completeness, and CVI synchronization state.

06design

Can one organization be both customer and supplier?

Yes. When it is the same governed identity, one Organization BP can carry both customer and supplier roles while keeping Sales, Purchasing, and accounting data in their own organizational contexts.
