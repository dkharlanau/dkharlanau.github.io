SAP S/4HANA / Enterprise Structure

Organizational structure is a network of assignments, not a hierarchy to memorize.

Sales is our starting point, but a real order crosses Procurement, Inventory, Planning, Warehouse, Transportation, Finance, and sometimes Maintenance. The useful question is not only “Which organizational units exist?” We need to know which combinations are valid, which module owns each boundary, and what changes when an assignment is missing or wrong.

Memory rule: COMPANY CODE = financial owner · SALES ORGANIZATION = who sells · PURCHASING ORGANIZATION = who buys · PLANT = where logistics happens · STORAGE LOCATION = where stock is separated · MRP AREA = what is planned independently · WAREHOUSE NUMBER = who manages the warehouse · SHIPPING POINT = who executes outbound shipping.

Lead rule: before changing master data, pricing, ATP, shipping, or code, prove that the organizational combination itself is valid.

Mental model

Read the structure as three connected layers.

The same order crosses a financial boundary, a commercial context, and a fulfillment context. SAP keeps these layers separate because they answer different business questions.

1. Financial ownership

Company Code is the accounting boundary. A Sales Organization is assigned to one Company Code. This connects the commercial transaction to the legal entity that owns receivables and revenue posting.

2. Commercial context

Sales Organization + Distribution Channel + Division form the Sales Area. This tells us who sells, through which route, and for which product line.

3. Fulfillment execution

Plant is the logistics and supply node. Shipping Point is the outbound execution unit. These units connect the order to stock, ATP, delivery creation, picking, goods issue, warehouse, and transport processes.

Do not collapse the layers. A Sales Organization is not a Plant. A Plant is not a Shipping Point. A Sales Area is not a legal entity. Each object has a different owner and purpose.

Cross-module map

One enterprise structure, several module views.

SAP does not create a separate company for every module. The same Company Code and Plant are reused across processes, while each module adds only the organizational objects it needs.

hub

The central hinge is Plant. Sales uses the delivering Plant, Procurement buys for Plants, Inventory stores stock in Plant/Storage Location, MRP plans inside a Plant, QM stores material quality data at Plant level, EWM maps Plant/Storage Location into a Warehouse Number, and Asset Management can use the same Plant as a maintenance location.

MM / Procurement / Inventory

Purchasing adds a second commercial responsibility: who buys.

Sales Organization answers who sells. Purchasing Organization answers who negotiates and procures materials or services. Both can meet at the same Plant.

MM1purchasing

Purchasing Organization ↔ Plant

Who is allowed to procure for this Plant?

Rule

The Plant assignment is mandatory. One Purchasing Organization can serve several Plants, and one Plant can work with several Purchasing Organizations.

Runtime effect

Purchasing master data, contracts, info records, conditions, supplier data, source determination, and purchase documents are read in this organizational context.

MM2legal

Purchasing Organization → Company Code

Is purchasing local to one legal entity or centralized across companies?

Plant-specific / cross-plant

If the Purchasing Organization is assigned to a Company Code, its Plants must belong to that Company Code.

Cross-company purchasing

For cross-company purchasing, the Purchasing Organization is not assigned to one Company Code; Plants from different Company Codes can be assigned to it.

MM3buyer

Purchasing Group

Who is the buyer or buyer team?

Important exception

A Purchasing Group is created at client level and is not structurally assigned to one Purchasing Organization or Plant.

Design meaning

Do not draw it as a mandatory child node under Purchasing Organization. It is a responsibility key used across purchasing processes and master data.

MM4determination

Standard and Reference Purchasing Organizations

How do we reuse purchasing conditions or determine a Purchasing Organization automatically?

Standard Purchasing Organization

Can be assigned to a Plant for processes where SAP must determine a Purchasing Organization automatically, for example selected stock-transfer, consignment, pipeline, or automatic-order scenarios.

Reference Purchasing Organization

Allows purchasing conditions and contracts to be referenced across Purchasing Organizations. Use it to reduce duplicate maintenance when the commercial policy is genuinely shared.

IMstock

Plant → Storage Location

Where is stock logically separated inside the Plant?

Rule

Storage Location belongs to a Plant. Inventory quantity and physical inventory can be managed at Plant/Storage Location level.

Sales effect

A Sales Order normally starts higher at Plant level, but picking, warehouse execution, returns, stock transfers, and goods movements often need the Storage Location.

compare_arrows

Memory contrast: Sales Organization owns the customer-facing sale. Purchasing Organization owns supplier-facing procurement. Plant is where those two flows can meet.

PP / MRP / QM

Plant remains the base, but planning can be more granular.

Production and Quality reuse the logistics Plant instead of creating a Sales-like organizational chain. MRP adds the MRP Area when supply must be planned independently inside the Plant.

Plant

Plant is the central logistics unit for production, procurement, maintenance, and materials planning. Product planning and many production parameters are Plant-specific.

MRP Area

MRP Area is the independent planning level in SAP S/4HANA. It cannot span Plants. A Plant MRP Area exists for each Plant; separate MRP Areas can also cover one or more Storage Locations or a subcontractor.

Quality Management

QM material data is managed at Plant level. Inspection setup and Plant-specific inspection plans mean that the same product can have different quality behavior in different Plants.

manufacturing

Sales connection: a Sales demand can be confirmed at one Plant, planned in an MRP Area, produced or procured there, and then blocked or released by Quality. The Sales Area did not change, but the supply state did.

Open Production arrow_forward

EWM / Warehouse execution

Storage Location is not the physical warehouse.

This distinction is important. Inventory Management uses Plant and Storage Location to identify stock. EWM adds the warehouse structure used to execute physical warehouse work.

WM1integration

Plant + Storage Location → ERP Warehouse Number

Which warehouse-management solution controls this stock?

Rule

A Plant/Storage Location combination is linked to an ERP Warehouse Number when warehouse management is used. One Warehouse Number can be connected to several Plant/Storage Location combinations.

Why it matters

This assignment decides whether stock stays in Inventory Management only or is handed to a warehouse-management solution such as embedded or decentralized EWM.

WM2EWM

ERP Warehouse Number → EWM Warehouse Number

Which EWM warehouse executes the work?

Boundary

The ERP warehouse number is three characters; the EWM Warehouse Number is a separate four-character warehouse object. The integration maps the ERP warehouse context to EWM.

Execution

Inside EWM, Warehouse Number is the highest warehouse level. Storage Types, Storage Sections, Storage Bins, and Activity Areas describe the internal warehouse structure.

Storage Location

Logical Inventory Management separation of stock inside a Plant. It does not describe racks, doors, or bins.

Storage Type / Bin

EWM physical or logical warehouse subdivisions. A Storage Bin is the smallest addressable warehouse location.

Activity Area

Logical grouping of bins for warehouse activities such as picking or putaway. It can cross Storage Types.

warehouse

Diagnostic rule: if ERP says stock exists but picking fails, separate the ERP stock identity (Plant/Storage Location) from the EWM execution identity (Warehouse Number, stock type, Storage Type, Activity Area, Bin).

Transportation Management

Freight adds its own organizational responsibility.

Transportation Management can reuse enterprise structure values, but its organizational objects are not always technically the same objects as MM or SD. The exact model depends on deployment.

Company organization

In SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition TM, the company organization corresponds to the ERP Company Code and is used for charging and invoicing.

Purchasing Organization

TM uses a Purchasing Organization for freight procurement. It negotiates freight agreements and is used in freight documents for charge calculation and settlement.

Purchasing Group

TM uses a Purchasing Group below its Purchasing Organization for transportation purchasing responsibility.

local_shipping

Deployment boundary: in Public Edition TM these organizational units are technically different from the MM objects but are automatically replicated from the enterprise structure. Private Edition and older standalone TM models can expose additional organizational-model concepts, so do not copy one deployment's setup blindly into another.

FI / CO / Profitability

Legal ownership and management accounting are different boundaries.

Company Code owns external accounting. Controlling Area owns internal cost accounting. Operating Concern sits above them when profitability analysis needs a common market view.

FIexternal

Company Code

Where are legal financial statements produced?

Role

Independent accounting entity for external reporting. Sales Organizations and Plants are connected to this financial boundary through enterprise-structure assignments.

Sales effect

Customer receivables, revenue, taxes, COGS, and intercompany postings eventually use Company Code context.

COinternal

Company Code → Controlling Area

Which companies share the same cost-accounting boundary?

Rule

One Controlling Area can contain one or several Company Codes. This enables cross-company cost accounting when the required accounting prerequisites are aligned.

Plant consequence

Because Plant belongs to a Company Code, the Plant also inherits the relevant Controlling Area relationship for cost flows.

PAprofitability

Controlling Area → Operating Concern

At which level do we analyze profitability across market segments?

Role

Operating Concern is the organizational level for profitability analysis. It can combine profitability information from one or more Controlling Areas.

Sales connection

Sales characteristics such as customer, product, region, division, and organizational fields can flow into profitability analysis without becoming new Sales organizational units.

account_balance

Do not confuse reporting objects with enterprise structure. Cost Centers and Profit Centers are essential management-accounting master/account-assignment objects, but they solve a different problem from Company Code, Sales Organization, or Plant.

Open Finance / Logistics integration arrow_forward

Credit, Service and Projects

Some areas add an organizational layer; others reuse the existing one.

This is useful for architecture discussions because not every SAP application creates another enterprise-structure hierarchy.

Credit Management

Credit Segment is an organizational unit in SAP Credit Management used for credit assignment and control. A business partner can have different limits and credit data by segment. Sales documents receive a credit segment during credit processing, so organizational changes can affect exposure and blocks.

SAP S/4HANA Service

Service integrates the SD structure with a service-specific organizational model. A Service Organization can represent service responsibility; integration maps this responsibility back into logistics concepts such as the maintenance planning plant where required.

Project System

Project System does not need a separate enterprise structure of its own. Projects reuse organizational units from Finance, Controlling, Sales, Purchasing, Production, and Plant logistics. This is a good example of SAP integration by reuse rather than duplication.

credit_score

Sales Lead point: when an order is credit blocked, the Sales Area may be correct. The problem can sit in the Credit Segment, credit account, exposure, or the mapping that derives the credit context.

Open Credit Management arrow_forward

Asset Management

The same Plant can also be a maintenance boundary.

Asset Management usually starts from Plants that already exist for Logistics, then adds maintenance-specific responsibility.

Maintenance Plant

The Plant where the technical objects are installed. It represents the operating location from a maintenance perspective.

Maintenance Planning Plant

The Plant where maintenance requirements are planned. One Planning Plant can plan for its own Maintenance Plant or for other assigned Maintenance Plants.

Planner Group / Work Center

Planner Groups organize maintenance planning responsibility. Maintenance Work Centers represent the teams or capacity that perform the work and are assigned to Maintenance Plants.

Why it belongs on this page: when a production Plant has maintenance downtime, the effect can move through capacity, production, ATP, delivery, and finally the customer promise.

groups

Scope boundary — HCM: Human Capital Management adds Personnel Area, Personnel Subarea, and its own Organizational Management hierarchy. These are important enterprise objects, but they are a separate people/HR model rather than another step below Sales Area or Plant. For logistics assessment, know the boundary and follow the dedicated HCM model only when employee or workforce organization is in scope.

Key components

Know what each unit owns before you learn the assignments.

This is the Sales view of the enterprise structure. Finance and Logistics units are included because Sales depends on them at runtime.

Element Links to What it means Why Sales cares
Company Code Sales Organization, Plant Legal entity and independent accounting unit. It is the smallest unit for which complete statutory financial statements can be produced. Every Sales Organization belongs to one Company Code. A Plant also belongs to one Company Code, so Sales and fulfillment can be checked against legal ownership.
Sales Organization Company Code, Distribution Channel, Division Central Sales organizational unit. It is responsible for selling goods and services, negotiating sales conditions, and commercial responsibility such as product liability and rights of recourse. All Sales and Distribution documents are assigned to a Sales Organization. It is a major key for pricing, master data, authorizations, reporting, and integration.
Distribution Channel Sales Organization Route to market: for example direct sales, wholesale, retail, or another channel. The same seller can use different prices, customer data, product data, or process rules depending on how the customer is served.
Division Sales Organization Product or service line used in Sales. It completes the Sales Area and can separate responsibility, customer agreements, pricing, and reporting by product line.
Distribution Chain Sales Organization + Distribution Channel The two-part commercial combination used to connect Sales to product sales data and delivering Plants. Plants are assigned to this level. This is one of the most important bridges from commercial design to logistics.
Sales Area Sales Organization + Distribution Channel + Division The core commercial context: who sells, how, and what. Customer sales data is maintained for a Sales Area. Sales employees can be authorized for one or more Sales Areas. The Sales Area is also an input into pricing-procedure determination.
Plant Company Code, Distribution Chain, Shipping Point Central logistics unit for production, planning, procurement, distribution, or service provision. The delivering Plant is a major input for availability, stock, sourcing, shipping-point determination, fulfillment, and intercompany boundaries.
Storage Location Plant Subdivision of stock inside a Plant. It becomes relevant in inventory, warehouse, picking, and goods-movement execution. It is not part of the Sales Area.
Shipping Point Plant Highest-level Sales shipping organizational unit. Each outbound delivery is processed by one Shipping Point. It defines the outbound shipping execution context. In the standard determination model, Plant, shipping condition, and loading group are key inputs.
Sales Office / Sales Group Sales Area / Sales Office Responsibility overlays for regional offices and sales teams. They support responsibility, reporting, selection, and authorization without changing the core Sales Area.

Assignment map

The assignments are the real architecture.

A Lead should be able to trace the valid path from financial ownership to commercial scope and then to fulfillment execution.

01finance

Sales Organization → Company Code

Which accounting entity owns the selling unit?

Rule

Each Sales Organization is assigned to exactly one Company Code. One Company Code can have several Sales Organizations.

Why it matters

This anchors Sales to financial ownership. Do not create a new Company Code only because Sales needs another market or reporting view.

02channel

Distribution Channel ↔ Sales Organization

Which routes to market can this seller use?

Rule

A Sales Organization can use several Distribution Channels, and the same Distribution Channel can be assigned to several Sales Organizations.

Why it matters

The pair becomes the Distribution Chain. It scopes product sales data and connects the selling route to possible delivering Plants.

03division

Division ↔ Sales Organization

Which product lines can this seller represent?

Rule

Several Divisions can be assigned to one Sales Organization. A Division can also be used by several Sales Organizations.

Why it matters

Division completes the Sales Area. Use it for a durable Sales-level product responsibility, not as a random reporting tag.

04commercial

Sales Organization + Channel + Division → Sales Area

Which seller, route, and product-line combinations are valid?

Rule

The three-part combination defines a Sales Area. It is the central commercial context for Sales processing.

Why it matters

Every additional Sales Area can create more customer-data scope, pricing scope, authorizations, interfaces, reporting combinations, testing, and support work.

05ownership

Plant → Company Code

Which legal entity owns the logistics location?

Rule

Each Plant belongs to one Company Code. One Company Code can own several Plants.

Why it matters

If the selling Sales Organization and delivering Plant belong to different Company Codes, the process crosses a legal boundary and intercompany logic becomes relevant.

06fulfillment

Distribution Chain ↔ Plant

Which Plants may deliver for this Sales Organization and Channel?

Rule

A Plant can serve several Distribution Chains, and a Distribution Chain can use several Plants. Plants can belong to different Company Codes.

Why it matters

A master record can propose a Plant, but the enterprise structure must still allow that Plant for the selling Distribution Chain.

07shipping

Plant ↔ Shipping Point

Which shipping units can execute outbound work for the Plant?

Rule

A Plant can have several Shipping Points. A Shipping Point can also serve more than one Plant when the operating model supports it.

Why it matters

The final Shipping Point is determined later from logistics inputs. Do not treat Plant and Shipping Point as the same organizational object.

08stock

Plant → Storage Location

Where inside the Plant is stock managed?

Rule

Storage Locations exist inside a Plant and subdivide its stock.

Why it matters

The Sales Area does not own Storage Locations. Their role appears later in Inventory Management, picking, warehouse execution, and goods movements.

09office

Sales Office ↔ Sales Area

Which regional office is responsible in this commercial context?

Rule

Sales Offices can be assigned to Sales Areas.

Why it matters

This adds responsibility and reporting structure without creating another Sales Organization or Sales Area.

10team

Sales Group → Sales Office

Which sales team works under the office responsibility?

Rule

Sales Groups represent teams of sales employees and fit below the Sales Office responsibility layer.

Why it matters

A people reorganization should normally be cheaper than redesigning the legal, commercial, or fulfillment structure.

Important distinction: assigning a Shipping Point to a Plant only makes the relationship possible. Shipping-point determination decides which Shipping Point is proposed for a concrete item.

Sales Area at runtime

Sales Area is more than a header field.

The Sales Area is a major context key. It decides which customer data is valid and participates in pricing, authorizations, reporting, and process control.

Customer master scope

Customer Sales Area data can contain payment terms, Incoterms, shipping conditions, customer pricing procedure, account-assignment fields, delivery controls, price groups, and other Sales defaults.

Authorization scope

SAP Learning describes Sales Area as the context used to control who can sell, how, and what. Sales employees can be authorized for one or more Sales Areas.

Document context

Sales and Distribution documents are assigned to a Sales Organization. The Sales Area provides the wider commercial context that starts in the sales document and remains relevant as the process moves to delivery and billing.

Sales Area → Pricing

Sales Area helps select the pricing procedure, but it is not the only key.

This distinction is easy to miss in an interview. Sales Area provides the organizational part of pricing-procedure determination; document and customer pricing procedures complete the key.

calculate

Pricing Procedure Determination: Sales Area + Document Pricing Procedure + Customer Pricing Procedure → Pricing Procedure.

The Document Pricing Procedure comes from the sales document type. The Customer Pricing Procedure is maintained in customer/BP Sales Area data.

The fulfillment hinge

Plant is where Sales turns into Logistics.

Many problems that look like ATP, shipping, delivery, or intercompany problems start one step earlier: the wrong Plant was proposed, or the right Plant is not valid for the Distribution Chain.

fork_right

Typical Plant proposal sequence: Customer-Material information record → Ship-to Party master data → Product master data. A user can also enter a Plant manually where the process allows it.

Then validate the structure: the Plant still has to be allowed for the Sales Organization + Distribution Channel combination.

Availability

The delivering Plant provides the logistics context for stock and availability. A Plant change can change the supply picture even when customer and product stay the same.

Shipping

The Plant is an input into Shipping Point determination. In the standard model, shipping condition and loading group are the other main inputs.

Legal boundary

If the Sales Organization belongs to Company Code A and the Plant belongs to Company Code B, the process crosses company-code ownership and intercompany processing becomes relevant.

Diagnostic shortcut: if the final Shipping Point or confirmation looks wrong, inspect Plant provenance first. The wrong downstream result can be only the visible consequence.

Shipping Point

Plant says where supply is; Shipping Point says who executes outbound shipping.

These objects are linked, but they are not the same. A Plant can need several shipping responsibilities, for example parcel, truck, rail, or different loading areas.

local_shipping

Standard determination memory: Plant + Shipping Condition + Loading Group → Shipping Point.

Where the inputs usually come from: Plant from item/master-data logic, Shipping Condition from the customer/BP sales data or document context, and Loading Group from the product/material master.

Intercompany boundary

A Plant in another Company Code changes more than logistics.

This is a high-value Lead point. The selling company can create the customer order while a Plant owned by another company code delivers the goods. That creates an intercompany relationship between the legal entities.

Selling company

The Sales Organization belongs to its Company Code and owns the external customer relationship.

Delivering company

The Plant belongs to another Company Code and physically fulfills the order.

Internal commercial flow

In intercompany sales processing, the delivering company bills the selling company with an intercompany billing document. The exact orchestration depends on the intercompany process variant.

rule

Do not overstate the rule: “Plant in another Company Code” is the signal to check intercompany design. It is safer than saying that any manual cross-company Plant entry automatically creates the complete internal billing flow in every S/4HANA deployment and process variant.

Reduce maintenance

More organizational combinations do not always require duplicated master data.

Classic Sales Customizing supports common Distribution Channels and common Divisions so selected master data and condition data can be shared instead of maintained repeatedly.

Common Distribution Channel

Useful when several channels need separate process or reporting context but can reuse selected customer, product, or condition data from a reference channel.

Common Division

Useful when several Divisions exist in the organizational model but some master data can be maintained once against a reference Division.

Lead trade-off

Sharing reduces maintenance, but it also creates dependency. Before reusing data, confirm that the channels or divisions really follow the same commercial policy.

Deployment note: classic SPRO paths are most relevant to S/4HANA on-premise and Private Edition. Public Cloud configuration uses configuration activities and can expose a different setup path.

Keep the layers separate

Organizational structure, master data, determination, and runtime data are different things.

Lead design decisions

Every new organizational unit creates operating cost.

Enterprise structure decisions shape master data, pricing, authorizations, integrations, reporting, testing, and support long after go-live. Create a new unit only when it represents a real and durable business boundary.

New Sales Organization?

Use it for a stable commercial selling responsibility that needs its own organizational ownership and Company Code assignment. Do not use it only to get another report column.

New Distribution Channel?

Use it when the route to market needs a durable difference in data, pricing, responsibility, process behavior, authorizations, or reporting.

New Division?

Use it when a product or service line needs a stable Sales-level distinction. Do not replace Product Hierarchy with dozens of Divisions.

New Plant?

Use it for a real production, inventory, procurement, planning, service, valuation, location, or legal requirement. Plant is not a reporting dimension.

New Shipping Point?

Use it when outbound execution needs another operational responsibility, location, loading method, schedule, or capacity model.

Reuse data?

If the organizational distinction is real but the commercial policy is shared, evaluate common master-data settings instead of copying the same records into every combination.

Diagnostic playbook

When an organizational problem appears, check in this order.

Find the first invalid context. Do not repair the final field before you know why SAP arrived there.

01document

Capture the actual Sales Area

Which Sales Organization, Channel, and Division are on the document?

Check

Header values, source document, interface payload, user entry, and copy/reference behavior.

Reason

If the Sales Area is wrong, downstream master-data lookups can be correct for the wrong context.

02structure

Verify the Sales Area combination

Is the seller + channel + division combination valid?

Check

Sales Organization–Channel assignment, Sales Organization–Division assignment, and the configured Sales Area.

Reason

This proves the commercial path before you inspect customer-specific data.

03customer

Check BP/customer data at the same Sales Area

Does the customer have the required data in this context?

Check

Shipping condition, payment terms, Incoterms, customer pricing procedure, account-assignment data, and other relevant Sales Area fields.

Reason

“The customer exists” is not enough. Sales data can be missing for the current Sales Area.

04product

Check product data at Distribution Chain and Plant level

Is the product extended to the organizational scopes required by this process?

Check

Sales Organization + Distribution Channel data, Plant data, loading group, delivering Plant proposals, and status fields.

Reason

A product can exist globally and still be unusable in this commercial or logistics context.

05plant

Trace Plant provenance

Why did this Plant appear?

Check

Customer-Material record, Ship-to Party, Product master, manual entry, enhancement, or interface logic.

Reason

Do not change the final Plant until you know which source won.

06assignment

Validate Distribution Chain ↔ Plant

Is this Plant allowed to fulfill for this seller and channel?

Check

Enterprise-structure assignment and Plant Company Code.

Reason

This separates a bad master-data proposal from an invalid enterprise-structure combination.

07shipping

Reconstruct Shipping Point determination

Which input is wrong: Plant, shipping condition, or loading group?

Check

Actual item Plant, customer/document shipping condition, product loading group, and allowed Shipping Point.

Reason

The final Shipping Point is an output. Diagnose the inputs first.

08legal

Check the Company Code boundary

Does the selected Plant belong to another Company Code?

Check

Sales Organization Company Code, Plant Company Code, process variant, intercompany settings, and billing relationship.

Reason

A fulfillment choice can become an accounting and intercompany design issue.

record_voice_over

Answer: In SAP S/4HANA Sales, I see the organizational structure in three layers. Company Code is the financial owner. Sales Organization, Distribution Channel, and Division build the Sales Area, which is the commercial context for customer data, authorizations, pricing inputs, and sales processing. The Distribution Chain then connects Sales to possible delivering Plants. The Plant gives us the supply and availability context, while the Shipping Point owns outbound execution. The important part is the assignments: Sales Org to Company Code, Channel and Division to Sales Org, Plant to Company Code, Distribution Chain to Plant, and Plant to Shipping Point. If the Sales Organization and delivering Plant belong to different Company Codes, I check the intercompany process because the fulfillment crosses a legal boundary.

Lead questions

Questions to ask before changing the structure.

These questions move the discussion from Customizing to business architecture.

What business boundary are we representing?

Legal entity, selling responsibility, route to market, product line, supply location, or shipping execution?

Which master data will multiply?

Customer Sales Area data, product Distribution Chain data, pricing records, output rules, tax data, partner data, or integrations?

Which process variants appear?

Will a new assignment introduce intercompany fulfillment, another Plant, another shipping flow, or another billing relationship?

What is the reporting requirement?

Can analytics, Sales Office, Product Hierarchy, or another reporting dimension solve it without creating a new core organizational unit?

Who owns maintenance?

Who creates and governs the new organizational data and the related BP/product/condition records?

What is the regression surface?

Which pricing, ATP, delivery, billing, tax, accounting, authorization, interface, and output scenarios must be retested?
