SAP Product Compliance / Logistics

Dangerous Goods: classify once, control transport across the value chain.

Dangerous Goods Management connects regulatory classification with logistics execution. The key question is not only whether a product is dangerous. SAP must also know which regulation applies, whether the classification is released, how the product is packaged, where it moves, and by which mode of transport.

Start with the mental model arrow_downward
rule

Important architecture boundary: SAP S/4HANA documentation contains both the classic EHS-DGP Dangerous Goods Management model and the newer Dangerous Goods capability in SAP S/4HANA for Product Compliance. They solve related transport-compliance problems, but their data model, apps and configuration are not the same.

For an assessment, first state which model and deployment you mean. Do not mix a classic material DG indicator profile and check schema with Product Compliance assessment/status logic as if they were one configuration path.

Mental model

Trace six decisions from product to document.

A dangerous-goods block normally becomes understandable when these decisions are checked in order.

01

Relevance

Is the product relevant for Product Compliance or, in the classic model, relevant for dangerous-goods checks?

02

Regulation

Which dangerous-goods regulation and version apply to the countries, route and mode of transport?

03

Classification

Is a released classification available, including the UN-number data and transport permission required by the regulation?

04

Packaging

Is the product represented as a valid packaged dangerous good or supported enclosure, with the required packaging data?

05

Runtime check

Does the business document pass transport permissibility and, where supported, mixed-loading checks?

06

Execution and output

Can the document continue, and does the required dangerous-goods description reach forms, transport documents, EDI or downstream systems?

account_tree

Lead rule: “The material is dangerous” is not enough to explain a block. The runtime result depends on regulatory scope, classification release, packaging, route/countries, mode of transport, document configuration and the exact process step.

Classification process

Classify the substance first, then the transport package.

This sequence is one of the most important Product Compliance concepts.

01

Activate regulatory scope

Make the required dangerous-goods compliance requirements available and active for the business scope.

02

Create compliance context

Mark the product compliance-relevant and assign the required compliance view and compliance purposes.

03

Classify unpackaged product

For each applicable regulation version, decide whether the product is a dangerous good. If yes, select the relevant UN-number data set and maintain required regulation-specific information.

04

Release basic classification

Check the generated dangerous-goods description and release the classification. Only released classification data should drive follow-on logistics processes.

05

Classify packaged product

After the basic classification is released, classify the related packaged product. Maintain enclosure variant, packaging details and any regulation- or mode-specific data.

06

Consume in logistics

Sales, Purchasing, Delivery or TM determines the applicable regulation and checks whether transport is allowed for the concrete document context.

inventory_2

Why packaging matters: SAP documents that an unpackaged product classified as dangerous goods is not allowed to be transported. A valid packaging or enclosure context is therefore not a cosmetic detail; it is part of transport eligibility.

Regulation determination

The same product can have different transport answers.

A dangerous-goods classification is regulation-specific. Road, rail, sea and air transport can require different regulatory data or permission.

Countries and regions

Departure, destination and relevant transit countries help determine which regulation applies.

Mode / shipping type

Road, rail, sea or air changes the regulation context. In supported S/4HANA releases, the shipping type in a sales document can contribute to regulation determination.

Regulation version

Classification is versioned. A regulatory update can create a reassessment need even when the product itself has not changed.

alt_route

Assessment example: a packaged solvent may be transportable from Germany to France by road under the released road classification, but the same product planned for ocean transport can require an IMDG-specific classification and different packaging or marking data. “Same material” does not mean “same transport result”.

Value-chain checks

Know which process checks what.

Product Compliance carries the regulatory decision into the business document. The exact check set is process-specific.

Two main runtime checks

Transport permission and mixed loading solve different problems.

Transport permissibility

Checks whether the product may be transported in the determined regulatory context. Missing released assessment, forbidden transport or unsupported product/package context can prevent further processing.

Mixed loading

Checks whether dangerous goods can be transported together in the same transport context. The system compares relevant products for regulatory incompatibilities. Availability and reaction depend on process, regulation and configuration.

Status aggregation

Item results are reflected in document-level status. A document can therefore be fully allowed, restricted/warning, partially blocked or blocked depending on the affected items and process.

warning

Do not overgeneralize: mixed loading is not simply “another transport-permission rule”. It evaluates combinations of products. It is also not integrated in every process in the same way; for example, SAP documents purchasing integration around transport permissibility rather than the full mixed-loading check.

Classic EHS-DGP

The classic model is driven by DG master data and check schemas.

This section is intentionally separate from Product Compliance. It is relevant in landscapes that still use the classic Dangerous Goods Management component.

01

Mark material relevance

Define dangerous-goods indicator profiles and assign the relevant profile to the material, traditionally in Basic Data 2. Customizing decides whether only materials marked relevant are checked.

02

Maintain DG master data

Dangerous-goods basic data can be maintained in specification management and filled into the dangerous-goods master. Engineering change management supports time-dependent versions.

03

Determine document context

Sales/shipping checks use logistics context such as sales organization, document type, ship-to, shipping point, route and the relevant document date.

04

Assign check schema

Customizing determines which dangerous-goods check schema runs for the business context.

05

Run check methods

The schema executes check methods against the dangerous-goods master. The classic completeness check must be positioned first in the schema.

06

React and log

The complete reaction of the schema controls messages and whether the user can save. The check log is the first evidence source for diagnosis.

history

Useful distinction: “DG indicator profile + dangerous-goods master + check schema” is the classic mental model. “Compliance relevance + compliance requirements + released assessment + Product Compliance status” is the newer Product Compliance mental model.

Output and integration

A passed check is not the end of compliance.

The transport chain still needs correct regulatory data on documents and interfaces.

Document text

Product Compliance can generate dangerous-goods descriptions from regulation-specific classification rules and use them in Sales and logistics output.

Forms

Standard form templates can include dangerous-goods information. The exact template and trigger depend on the business document and deployment.

EDI / APIs

Dangerous-goods information can be provided to downstream processes electronically. Treat regulatory data as part of the integration contract, not as decorative text.

description

Lead check: prove both decisions: “SAP allowed the transport” and “the required transport data was sent or printed correctly.” A green compliance status does not prove that the carrier received the correct description, labels or EDI content.

Diagnostics

Do not change configuration before you know which decision failed.

Start with the document evidence and narrow the problem to data, regulatory determination, packaging, configuration or integration.

SymptomPlausible causesEvidence to check first

Order was accepted but outbound delivery is blockedExecution context changed: route, transit country, shipping type, quantity/package data or classification validityCompare order and delivery transport context before changing master data.
Purchase order shows dangerous-goods restrictionTransport is not permitted for the determined country/mode, or required assessment is missingPO item/header Product Compliance status and the exact assessment used.

01

Identify the engine

Product Compliance or classic EHS-DGP? Confirm edition/release before using transactions or IMG paths.

02

Read the status/log

Find the exact item and check that failed. Avoid diagnosing from the generic document block alone.

03

Verify compliance data

Check relevance, compliance view, released regulation-specific classification and packaged-product assignment.

04

Verify transport context

Departure, destination, transit, mode/shipping type, route and relevant date.

05

Verify configuration

Document inclusion/exclusion, check behavior, mixed-loading activation, classic check schema where applicable.

06

Prove the fix

Recheck the same scenario and then the downstream document. Confirm output/interface data, not only the absence of a block.

Lead design decisions

Dangerous Goods is an operating model, not only a configuration task.

Scope ownership

Who decides which products, countries, regulations and transport modes are compliance-relevant? Product stewardship and logistics need one controlled ownership model.

Release discipline

Who can release classifications, and how are regulatory updates reassessed? A technically complete record is not enough without controlled approval and validity.

Exception policy

Which warnings can users accept, which failures must hard-block, and which document types may be excluded? Make the business-risk decision explicit.

Packaging model

Does the product/package model represent how goods are really shipped? Wrong package semantics can make a valid substance classification unusable in execution.

Integration contract

Which DG fields must reach TM, carrier interfaces, EDI, warehouse output and forms? Define ownership of mapping and monitoring.

Change impact

A new regulation version can affect many products and open logistics documents. Plan reassessment, monitoring and operational follow-up before the effective date.

Common confusions

Keep the neighboring concepts separate.

Do not confuseDifference
Dangerous Goods vs hazardous substance / SDSDangerous Goods focuses on transport compliance. Hazard communication and safety-data-sheet obligations are related Product Compliance areas but are not the same decision.
DG classification vs SAP ClassificationA dangerous-goods classification is a regulatory transport assessment. SAP CA-CL classes and characteristics are a generic cross-application attribute model.
Route determination vs regulation determinationSD/TM determines logistics route/context; Product Compliance uses transport context to identify the regulatory checks that apply.
Transport permission vs ATPATP answers whether/when supply can be confirmed. DG transport permission answers whether the product may legally move in the defined transport context.
Packaged product vs packaging materialThe packaged dangerous-good classification represents the product in a compliant transport enclosure. A packaging material master alone does not replace that regulatory classification.

Assessment

Explain the decision chain, then the architecture boundary.

A Lead answer should show that you understand both compliance master data and runtime logistics.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: “In S/4HANA Product Compliance, Dangerous Goods is not just a flag on the material. We mark the product compliance-relevant, assign the required compliance purposes and regulations, classify the unpackaged product, release the regulation-specific basic classification, and then classify the packaged product. During Sales, Purchasing, Delivery and TM, SAP determines the transport context and runs dangerous-goods checks. Transport permissibility verifies whether the product may move under the applicable regulation; Sales, Delivery and TM can also use mixed-loading checks. If assessment data is missing or transport is forbidden, the process can be blocked. For troubleshooting I identify whether the landscape uses Product Compliance or classic EHS-DGP, read the item status/check log, then trace product relevance → released classification → packaging → country/mode/route → check configuration → output.”

Follow-up: why can order pass and delivery fail?

Because the execution document can have a more concrete or changed route, transit country, mode, packaging or regulatory-validity context. Re-checking downstream is intentional.

Follow-up: what is the classic difference?

Classic EHS-DGP uses a dangerous-goods master, material DG indicator profiles and configured check schemas. Product Compliance uses compliance relevance, compliance requirements, released assessments and value-chain compliance statuses.

Follow-up: why unpackaged then packaged?

The basic classification belongs to the substance/product without packaging. Transport eligibility also depends on a compliant enclosure, so the packaged product adds transport-specific packaging data.
