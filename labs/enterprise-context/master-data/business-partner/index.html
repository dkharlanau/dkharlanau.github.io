Business Partner / Customer

The Business Partner is the identity layer behind the customer in SAP S/4HANA. Sales does not read one flat customer record. It reads role-specific and organizational data: general BP data, customer company-code data, and customer sales-area data. That separation explains why the same customer can behave differently by sales organization, distribution channel, division, or company code.

Mental model

Start with identity, then role, then organizational level.

database

Scope: Business Partner, customer role, company code, and sales area.

Important data

Customer pricing procedure, price list/group, shipping condition, Incoterms, payment terms, tax classification, delivery priority, account assignment group, sales office/group, partner functions, and sales-area blocks.

Configuration boundary

Do not confuse BP roles and customer data with partner-determination configuration. The procedure defines which partner functions are required or allowed. The master record contains the maintained business data that the procedure consumes.

Lead decision

Put the rule at the narrowest correct organizational level.

A customer value can be shared broadly or maintained for one business context. The design question is not where a field is easiest to change, but where the value is actually true.

Broad default

Use shared data when the fact should remain the same across the processes that consume it. This reduces duplicate maintenance.

Specific context

Use company-code or sales-area data when the business meaning really changes by organizational scope. This prevents one local rule from becoming a global side effect.

Trade-off

More specific maintenance gives better control, but it also creates more records, ownership questions, and replication work.

Lead check: before extending or changing the BP, name the consumers that should change and the consumers that must stay unchanged.

Management model

Create the identity once; extend it only where the business needs it.

In S/4HANA the Business Partner is the leading object for customer and supplier master data. A company that is both customer and supplier normally needs one BP with several roles, not two unrelated identities. Governance therefore starts with identity ownership and continues with controlled role and organizational extensions.

01

Choose the BP category

Select Person, Organization, or Group. This drives the basic data model, especially name fields. Treat this as a structural decision, not a label.

02

Choose grouping and numbering

The BP grouping determines the BP number range and whether numbering is internal or external. Use a clear numbering policy before migration or integration starts.

03

Assign only the roles that are needed

Add customer, supplier, contact, employee, or other roles according to the real business relationship. A role activates application-specific data; it does not automatically create every organizational view.

04

Extend organizational data

Create the required company-code, sales-area, or purchasing-organization data. A customer role without the required sales area still cannot support that Sales process.

05

Govern changes

Restrict who may create, extend, block, or change BPs. For larger landscapes, use workflow or MDG-style governance so identity, tax, bank, sales, purchasing, and finance data have clear owners.

06

Replicate through supported interfaces

Keep BP, customer, and supplier mappings stable across systems. For S/4HANA integrations, treat BP as the identity anchor and avoid building a new customer-only or supplier-only integration model.

account_tree

Lead rule: create one identity, then add business capabilities through roles and organizational extensions. Do not create a second BP just because the same legal entity is used by another process.

Business Partner roles

BP roles answer “what can this party do in SAP?”

The standard system contains many roles and installed components can add more. For logistics and Sales, these are the roles worth knowing first.

RoleMeaningMain data levelWhy it matters
000000Business Partner (General)BP-level general dataIdentity, name, addresses, communication and general attributes. It does not make the BP a Sales customer or purchasing supplier by itself.
FLCU00Customer (Financial Accounting)Customer + company codeRequired when the customer participates in FI-AR. Company-code data includes accounting-specific settings such as reconciliation and payment-related data.
FLCU01Customer (Sales and Distribution)Customer + sales areaRequired for Sales customer data. The sales area is Sales Organization + Distribution Channel + Division.
FLVN00Supplier (Financial Accounting)Supplier + company codeSupports FI-AP supplier data at company-code level.
FLVN01Supplier (Purchasing)Supplier + purchasing organizationSupports purchasing-specific supplier data and purchasing-organization extension.
UKM000SAP Credit ManagementCredit profile + credit segmentHolds credit-relevant master data used by SAP Credit Management. For credit-checked Sales processes, the relevant payer needs the credit role and credit-segment data.
BUP001Contact PersonPerson BP + relationshipRepresents a person connected to an organization. The relationship to the organization is separate from the customer's SD partner functions.
BUP002ProspectBP roleUseful when the party exists before it becomes a full operational customer.
BUP003EmployeeBP roleUsed when an employee must participate as a business partner in supported processes.
warning

Do not mix two different role concepts. FLCU01 is a Business Partner role. Sold-to, ship-to, bill-to and payer are Sales partner functions. A BP can have the customer role and still appear in different partner functions in the sales document.

A BP role is assigned to the Business Partner. The related role category controls application-specific behavior and is also used by CVI. Partner functions belong to Sales partner determination and master relationships; they solve a different problem.

Configuration playbook

Configure BP from identity to CVI, not from isolated screens.

A stable setup is a chain. If one layer is inconsistent, creation or synchronization can fail even when the visible BP data looks correct.

  1. Define BP number ranges and groupings. Under Cross-Application Components → SAP Business Partner → Business Partner → Basic Settings → Number Ranges and Groupings, define intervals and assign them to groupings. Decide which ranges are internal and which are external.
  2. Define BP roles and role categories. Under Business Partner → Basic Settings → Business Partner Roles, maintain the roles the business is allowed to use. Standard customer and supplier roles normally remain the anchor for CVI.
  3. Control fields. Use BP field-modification / field-grouping settings to make fields required, optional, display-only, or hidden for the relevant context. Keep these settings compatible with customer and supplier field status.
  4. Configure customer and supplier account groups. Account groups still matter because they control customer/supplier number ranges, field status, and other application-specific behavior.
  5. Map BP grouping to customer/supplier account group. In Master Data Synchronization → Customer/Vendor Integration, define the BP-to-customer and BP-to-supplier number assignment. Decide whether BP and customer/supplier use the same number.
  6. Map BP role categories for CVI. Configure which BP role categories create or maintain customer and supplier data. A custom role alone does not automatically become a customer or supplier role.
  7. Test organizational extension. Create the company code, sales area and purchasing organization combinations used in real processes. Test both a complete case and a deliberately incomplete case.
  8. Test synchronization and error handling. Verify BP → Customer/Supplier and any required reverse direction, including number mapping, mandatory fields and integration queues or error handling used by the landscape.

BP grouping

Controls the Business Partner number range. This is an identity decision.

Customer / supplier account group

Controls the customer or supplier application object, including its number-range and field-status behavior.

CVI mapping

Connects both models. This is where grouping, account group, role category, field mapping and number policy must agree.

Practical tooling: on-premise and private-cloud systems commonly use transaction BP for maintenance. SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition uses released Fiori apps such as Manage Business Partner Master Data and configuration through the cloud configuration model rather than unrestricted classic SPRO access.

Restrictions and design limits

Some BP choices are structural. Treat them as migration and governance decisions.

ConstraintWhat it means in practice
BP category is fixedPerson, Organization, or Group is selected at creation. SAP does not allow the BP category to be changed later. If the legal identity was modeled incorrectly, this is not a simple field correction.
Grouping assignment is fixedThe grouping determines the BP number range and cannot be reassigned after creation. Design grouping and migration number policy before loading master data.
Role is not extensionAdding FLCU01 does not mean that every sales area exists. Adding FLCU00 does not mean that every company code exists. Organizational data must be created for the exact business scope.
CVI mappings must agreeBP grouping, customer/supplier account group, role category, number assignment and field status must be compatible. A mismatch can terminate synchronization.
Same-number design has prerequisitesIf BP and customer/supplier should use the same identifier, the relevant number ranges and external/internal assignment rules must be compatible. Do not decide this after migration design is complete.
Role removal can be blockedA role assignment may be required by existing relationships or application use. Check where-used and process dependencies before removing a role.
Do not delete transactional historyFor partners already used in business documents, blocking, deletion flags, data-retention rules and archiving are safer governance tools than trying to erase the identity.
Deployment model mattersPublic Cloud exposes released apps, configuration activities and APIs. Private Cloud/on-premise allow a broader classic configuration and extension surface. Do not design a solution around a transaction or enhancement point that the target deployment does not release.
rule

Lead check before creation or migration: category, grouping, number policy, account-group mapping, required roles, organizational extensions, ownership, and source system must be decided together. Fixing them independently later is expensive.

Runtime use

The same Business Partner can supply different data to different parts of the process.

At order entry the document uses specific partner roles and organizational views, not one flat customer record. Sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, and payer can contribute different context. General BP data identifies the party; customer roles make the BP usable in customer processes; company-code and sales-area data provide the organizational values consumed by Sales and Finance.

01

Name the partner role

Start from the actual sold-to, ship-to, bill-to, payer, or other partner stored in the document. “The customer” is too broad for diagnosis.

02

Confirm the required BP role

Check that the Business Partner is extended for the business context the process needs before reading field values.

03

Read the owning level

Separate general BP data from company-code and sales-area data. Use the exact sales organization, distribution channel, division, and company code from the process.

04

Trace the consumer

Connect the field to the mechanism that uses it: pricing, shipping, payment, partner determination, tax, account determination, or another process decision.

05

Prove the document value

Compare master data with what the document stored and check copy logic, interface input, replication, or manual override only after the owning master level is clear.

Troubleshooting

Most “customer master” problems are really role or level problems.

troubleshoot

Start from the wrong document value and name its partner role. Then identify whether the field belongs to general BP data, company-code data, or the exact sales-area view. If the master value is correct but the document is not, move to partner determination, reference/copy behavior, interface input, replication, or an allowed override. Do not change a global BP attribute to repair a sales-area-specific issue.

Assessment recall

Use role + level + consumer.

60-second answer: In S/4HANA the Business Partner is the leading identity for customer and supplier master data. I separate four layers: BP category and grouping, BP role, organizational extension, and the process that consumes the data. For Sales I normally expect FLCU01 plus the required sales-area data; for FI customer data I use FLCU00. CVI maps BP groupings and roles to customer or supplier account groups and number ranges. Category and grouping are structural choices, so I design them before migration. I also separate BP roles from SD partner functions such as sold-to or payer. When something fails, I check role, organizational level, CVI mapping, field status, and then the document value.
