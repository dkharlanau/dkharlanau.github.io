SAP Procurement / Business Partner / FI-AP

Supplier master data connects identity,
purchasing, and accounting.

In SAP S/4HANA, the Business Partner is the leading object. Procurement then needs the correct supplier roles and organizational views. A supplier can exist globally and still be unusable for a purchase order, invoice, or payment because one required view is missing.

Start with the object map arrow_downward
rule

Lead rule: do not debug “the vendor master” as one record. Separate Business Partner identity, FI supplier data at company-code level, purchasing data at purchasing-organization level, and supplier-material relationship data such as the Purchasing Info Record.

Procurement master-data map

Three master-data objects provide most PO defaults.

When a purchasing document is created, SAP can propose data from reusable master records. This reduces manual entry, but the proposal source depends on the field and the procurement context.

Supplier / Business Partner

Identity, address, company-code accounting data, purchasing-organization data, partner functions, and commercial defaults.

Typical PO inputs: supplier address, order currency, Incoterms, payment-related defaults, partner roles.

Material / Product

Reusable product data at client, plant, purchasing, accounting, and other organizational levels.

Typical PO inputs: description, base/order units, material group, purchasing text, valuation and planning context.

Purchasing Info Record

Supplier-material relationship data, normally scoped by purchasing organization and, where relevant, plant.

Typical PO inputs: price/conditions, planned delivery time, supplier material number, order unit and supplier-specific tolerances.

content_copy

Document principle: master data proposes values into the purchasing document. After a value is copied, the document owns its current value. A later master-data change does not automatically mean every existing purchase order is rewritten.

Business Partner role model

One identity, several process roles and scopes.

The useful question is not only “Does the BP exist?” but “Does it have the supplier role and organizational data required by this process?”

LayerTypical BP role / scopeExamples of dataUsed for
General Business Partner Role 000000 · client-wide identity Name, addresses, communication, bank data and common BP attributes Shared identity across customer, supplier and other roles
FI Supplier FLVN00 · company code Reconciliation account, payment methods/terms, correspondence and accounting controls Invoice posting, AP subledger and payment processing
Supplier / Purchasing FLVN01 · purchasing organization Order currency, Incoterms, purchasing controls and partner functions Purchase requisition source use, purchase orders and purchasing defaults
Supplier-material relationship Purchasing Info Record Supplier-specific price, planned delivery time, order unit, supplier material number More specific defaults for one supplier-material relationship

Typical failure

The BP exists and looks correct, but the purchasing organization view is missing. The user can find the partner but cannot use it correctly in purchasing.

Another failure

The supplier is extended for purchasing but not for the required company code. A PO may be possible while invoice/payment processing fails later.

CVI — Customer/Vendor Integration

Business Partner is the leading object; supplier account data stays synchronized.

SAP S/4HANA uses Customer/Vendor Integration to synchronize Business Partner data with the technical customer and supplier account structures used by applications such as Financial Accounting.

01

Choose BP grouping

The grouping controls BP number assignment and is mapped to the relevant customer/supplier account-group design.

02

Add supplier role

Add the FI and/or purchasing supplier role required by the business process.

03

Maintain organizational views

Extend the supplier to the company code and purchasing organization that will use it.

04

CVI synchronizes

Configured mappings keep the BP and technical supplier account representation consistent.

warning

Numbering nuance: the BP number and supplier account number are related through CVI number-range settings. They can be designed to be the same, but do not assume this without checking the grouping, account group, and number-range mapping.

Configuration objects to remember

BP grouping and number range · supplier account group and number range · CVI mapping and synchronization settings · field-control compatibility.

Lead diagnosis

If synchronization fails, check the first inconsistent field or mapping. Do not solve a CVI problem by creating a duplicate supplier identity.

Company-code accounting data

The reconciliation account connects the supplier subledger to the General Ledger.

Each supplier account records liabilities at supplier level in Accounts Payable. The reconciliation account represents those supplier liabilities in the General Ledger.

Supplier subledger

The supplier account contains the open items and balance for one supplier.

Reconciliation account

A G/L account maintained in the supplier company-code data. It groups the corresponding AP liabilities in the General Ledger.

Automatic posting

When an invoice posts to the supplier account, SAP also updates the assigned reconciliation account. The user normally posts to the supplier, not directly to that reconciliation account.

account_balance

Assessment sentence: “The supplier is the AP subledger account. The reconciliation account is maintained in supplier company-code data and automatically represents the supplier liabilities in the G/L.”

Partner functions in Purchasing

The supplier on the PO does not have to perform every procurement role.

Purchasing partner functions connect the main supplier with other Business Partners that perform specific tasks. For example, one company can receive the purchase order, another location can deliver the goods, and a group headquarters can send the invoice. Each alternative partner must have its own Business Partner master record.

Partner functionBusiness meaningExampleTypical consequence
Ordering Address The partner that receives and processes the purchase order. You buy from ABC Group, but South ABC handles the orders. The PO communication is sent to the alternative ordering address.
Goods Supplier The partner that physically delivers the goods and can receive returns. ABC Group is the contractual supplier, but factory XYZ ships the material. The goods-supplier address can be used for logistics and return-delivery processing.
Invoicing Party The partner that issues the supplier invoice. You order from Supplier A, but headquarters C sends the invoice. Invoice processing can determine the different invoicing party from the purchasing document.
Alternative Payee / other configured roles Another partner performs a payment or other configured procurement function. The legal supplier and payment recipient are different according to the approved master-data design. The exact behavior depends on partner-function, account-group and downstream FI configuration.
group_work

Simple case: if the same supplier performs all procurement functions, you do not need to maintain alternative partner functions. Use partner determination only when the business relationship really contains different partners.

Where partner assignments can be maintained

Partner assignments can become more specific than the purchasing organization. The system can therefore use different partners for different plants or parts of the supplier's product range.

LevelWhen it is usefulLead note
Purchasing organizationThe partner is the same for all plants and supplier sub-ranges in the purchasing organization.Use this as the broad default.
Purchasing organization / plantA plant uses a different ordering address, goods supplier or other partner.Use only when the physical or organizational relationship really differs by plant.
Purchasing organization / supplier sub-rangeA part of the supplier's product range uses another partner.The sub-range lets purchasing data follow the supplier's internal business structure.
Purchasing organization / plant / sub-rangeThe partner differs for one plant and one supplier sub-range.This is the most specific level and adds the highest maintenance complexity.

Partner determination configuration chain

To understand why a partner is or is not determined, follow the configuration chain instead of checking only the supplier master.

01

Define the partner role

Define or use the required purchasing partner function. The Unique indicator controls whether only one partner can be maintained for this function at an organizational level. Without it, several partners can be available and one of them can be marked as the default.

02

Allow the role for the account group

The account group of the alternative partner must allow that partner function. This controls which types of suppliers may perform which roles.

03

Supplier partner schema

A supplier partner schema defines which partner functions are valid in the supplier master. For each role, it can require the function or prevent changes after entry. Assigning this schema to an account group is optional; if a schema is assigned, the entered partner must be allowed by it.

04

Purchasing-document partner schema

If partners from the supplier master must be proposed into a purchasing document, the relevant document type needs a partner schema that contains these roles.

05

Maintain the actual partner

Maintain the alternative Business Partner at the correct purchasing organization, plant and/or supplier sub-range level.

06

Prove the document result

Create the purchasing document and check the partners that were actually determined. The document result is the runtime evidence.

IndicatorMeaningAssessment point
MandatoryThe partner function must be present according to the schema.If the required partner is missing, check both the schema and the master assignment.
No ChangeThe determined partner cannot be changed or deleted in the purchasing document.This is stronger than a default. The document user cannot replace the adopted partner.
Higher LvlIf no partner exists at plant or supplier-sub-range level, the system can search at purchasing-organization level.This is the fallback rule from a specific level to the broader level.
EndPartner determination is delayed until the document is checked or saved instead of immediately after the supplier is entered.This helps when the system needs item plant/sub-range information before choosing a partner.

Single plant / sub-range

With determination at the end, SAP can check whether all PO items use the same plant or supplier sub-range and then use the partner maintained at that more specific level.

Several plants in one PO

If PO items use different plants, partner determination falls back to purchasing-organization level. A header partner cannot represent several conflicting plant-specific partners at the same time.

warning

Pricing trap: be careful with the End indicator for the Invoicing Party when discounts or surcharges depend on the invoicing party. Determining the partner only when the PO is saved or checked does not automatically trigger a new price determination.

psychology

Assessment model: partner function definition → permissible role for account group → supplier partner schema → purchasing-document partner schema → partner maintained at the correct level → document result. If one link is missing, partner determination can fail even when the Business Partner itself exists.

Purchasing defaults and controls

Supplier purchasing data does more than hold an address.

The purchasing-organization segment provides defaults and control indicators that affect how the supplier can be used in procurement. Treat them as reusable process rules, not as values that belong to one purchase order only.

Field / controlWhat it influencesAssessment point
PO currencyDefault commercial currency for purchasing documents and related purchasing master data.It is a purchasing-organization supplier default, not a universal BP attribute.
Payment terms / IncotermsDefaults for purchasing documents and, where applicable, related purchasing records.Always check the organizational level and the final value stored in the document.
GR-based Invoice VerificationProposes supplier-specific invoice-verification behavior based on goods receipt.The supplier master provides a default/control; the PO item and process context still matter.
Evaluated Receipt SettlementMarks the supplier for ERS-capable procurement when the required process prerequisites are met.ERS is an automated settlement design, not simply “invoice receipt without an invoice”.
Acknowledgment RequirementControls whether supplier acknowledgments/confirmations are expected by default.Use it when confirmation is part of the operational control model.
Automatic Purchase OrderSupports automatic PO creation for supplier-related procurement when the other prerequisites are satisfied.The indicator alone does not create a PO; source, material and process requirements still have to be valid.

Plant-specific data

Selected purchasing data can be maintained more specifically for a plant. Use this only when the supplier really behaves differently for that plant.

Supplier sub-range

A supplier sub-range divides the supplier's product range into smaller parts. It can be assigned in a Purchasing Info Record or purchasing document item and can carry more specific supplier data.

Partner functions

Partner functions can also differ by purchasing context. For example, the invoicing party determined for a PO can be different from the supplier from which the goods were ordered.

tune

Lead rule: choose the narrowest level where the supplier rule is true. Start with the purchasing organization; specialize by plant or supplier sub-range only when the business difference is real. More-specific master data gives precision but increases maintenance and troubleshooting complexity.

One-time supplier / CpD

Use one collective supplier account when creating a permanent supplier is not justified.

A one-time supplier is not “no master data”. SAP still uses a collective Business Partner/supplier master record, but transaction-specific identity details are entered during document processing.

Collective master

A dedicated one-time account, commonly associated with CpD/CPD design, stores common values that can be shared across occasional suppliers.

Document-specific data

Name, address, bank details and other supplier-specific information can be entered when the purchase order or invoice is created.

Why use it

It avoids uncontrolled master-data growth for suppliers used only once or very rarely.

Good use

Low-frequency, low-reuse supplier where the business accepts controlled document-level maintenance.

Governance risk

Overuse reduces supplier-level history, analytics and controls. Repeated suppliers should normally move to governed permanent master data.

Runtime use

Trace the default back to the most specific owning object.

Procurement documents combine supplier, material, relationship data and configuration. A wrong PO value is not automatically a supplier-master problem.

01

Supplier selected

Supplier identity, purchasing organization view, address and partner context become available.

02

Material selected

Material description, units, material group, purchasing text and plant-dependent controls can be proposed.

03

Relationship found

A valid Purchasing Info Record can provide supplier-material-specific conditions and logistics data.

04

PO owns the result

The purchasing document stores the final values that were determined, proposed or entered for this transaction.

troubleshoot

Debug sequence: field in PO → source and organizational scope → supplier role/view → material data → Purchasing Info Record → source determination / conditions → manual override. Change shared master data only after proving the wrong source.

Create / extend supplier

A practical sequence for implementation and support.

The exact UI differs by deployment and app, but the logical data model stays the same.

01

Identity

Create or reuse the correct Business Partner. Check duplicates before creating a new legal identity.

02

Grouping

Select the BP grouping and verify number-range/CVI mapping to the intended supplier account group.

03

FI role

Add FLVN00 when AP processing is required and maintain company-code data including the reconciliation account.

04

Purchasing role

Add FLVN01 and extend to the required purchasing organization with purchasing defaults and partner functions.

05

Relationship data

Create Purchasing Info Record, source list or quota data only when the business process requires those more specific sourcing objects.

06

Test end to end

Create a PO, verify defaults and partners, then prove goods receipt, invoice receipt and AP posting behavior.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: “In S/4HANA, Business Partner is the leading object. For procurement I separate general BP identity, FI supplier data at company-code level and purchasing supplier data at purchasing-organization level. FLVN00 carries accounting context; FLVN01 carries purchasing defaults, controls and partner functions. Alternative partners such as Ordering Address, Goods Supplier and Invoicing Party can be maintained at purchasing-organization, plant and supplier-sub-range levels. Partner determination is controlled by the partner role, account-group permissions, the supplier partner schema and the purchasing-document partner schema. Mandatory and No Change control the role itself; Higher Lvl controls fallback to purchasing-organization level; End controls when partner determination runs. CVI still synchronizes BP and supplier account structures, while supplier-material data belongs in the Purchasing Info Record.”

If a PO cannot use a supplier

Check BP identity → supplier role → purchasing organization extension → purchasing block/status → source-of-supply restrictions.

If invoice posting fails

Check invoicing party → company-code extension → reconciliation account → payment/tax/accounting data → document-specific invoice settings.
