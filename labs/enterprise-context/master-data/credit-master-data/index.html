SAP Sales / Master Data / MD.SD.CREDIT

Credit Master Data

SAP Credit Management stores risk and limit information separately from ordinary Sales customer data. The relevant unit is the credit account and credit segment, which lets one customer have different credit control across business contexts.

Follow the runtime path arrow_downward

Object profile

01MD.SD.CREDITCatalogue ID
02business masterData class
Business Partner/credit account, credit segment and validity.

Mental model

Master data defines risk capacity; exposure and check logic decide the current outcome.

database

Scope: Business Partner/credit account, credit segment and validity.

Important data

Credit segment, credit limit, validity, risk class, check rule and related scoring/risk attributes used by the implementation.

Configuration boundary

Credit master data provides limit and risk attributes. Credit-check rules, automatic control and reaction behavior are configuration in SAP Credit Management.

Runtime use

A credit limit is only one input to a runtime credit decision.

Sales or delivery processing can trigger a credit check for the relevant credit account and segment. The decision combines maintained credit attributes with current exposure and the configured check logic. A block is therefore a runtime result, not a direct copy of the credit limit field.

01

Identify account and segment

Start from the credit account and credit segment actually evaluated for the document. Do not assume the Sales customer number is enough.

02

Read maintained risk data

Check limit, validity, risk class, and the other credit attributes used by the implementation for that segment.

03

Read current exposure

Separate static master data from dynamic exposure. A correct limit can still produce a block when current exposure or open commitments change.

04

Trace the check rule

Identify which check ran, which thresholds or rules it evaluated, and which reaction is configured for failure.

05

Prove release and recheck

If a block is released, verify the ownership of that decision and whether later document changes or processing trigger another check.

Troubleshooting

Do not raise the limit until you know what the check evaluated.

troubleshoot

Identify the credit account and segment first. Then compare limit, validity, and risk data with current exposure and the exact check result. If the wrong segment was evaluated, changing a limit only hides the ownership problem. If the right segment was evaluated, prove which check failed and what should happen after release or recheck.

Assessment recall

Account → segment → master data → exposure → check.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Credit Master Data belongs to SAP Credit Management and is organized around the credit account and credit segment. I first prove which account and segment the document uses, then check limit, validity, and risk attributes. After that I add current exposure and the configured check rule, because a credit block is a runtime decision, not simply “limit exceeded.” I finish by proving who owns release and whether the document is rechecked later.
