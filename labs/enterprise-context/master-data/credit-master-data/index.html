Credit Master Data

Credit master data is not one customer-level credit limit. SAP Credit Management separates global policy in the Business Partner credit profile from segment-specific capacity. Runtime exposure then changes as orders, deliveries, billing and receivables move through the process.

Do not solve a runtime exposure problem by changing master data before you know which layer is wrong.

Mental model

Profile sets policy. Segment carries capacity. Exposure is runtime state.

This separation is the fastest way to understand SAP Credit Management. The same Business Partner can have one global risk policy and different credit limits for different credit segments.

Credit Profile

Business Partner-wide credit data. Typical fields include the rule for scoring and credit-limit calculation, score, risk class, credit-check rule, customer credit group, external ratings and other credit information.

Credit Segment Data

Data for one credit-management scope. The segment contains values such as credit limit, validity, special-attention or block information and other segment-specific controls supported by the implementation.

Runtime Exposure

Open commitments are not master data. Sales orders, deliveries, billing transfer and Financial Accounting can change exposure. Credit checks combine this changing exposure with the maintained policy.

rule

Assessment trap: risk class and check rule are maintained on the Credit Profile, not independently per credit segment. The credit limit is segment-specific. If you describe all three as “segment fields”, you mix two different data scopes.

Profile anatomy

The important fields describe risk, calculation policy and decision control.

Do not memorize the screen as a list. Read every field as an input to a business decision.

Data What it means Useful diagnostic question
Scoring and credit-limit rule Defines the procedure used to calculate score and credit limits. The exact formulas and inputs are configuration-dependent. Was the expected rule assigned, and was the value calculated or manually overridden?
Score Numeric or classified creditworthiness result used by the credit model. SAP can keep the calculated value even when a manual value is entered. Is the visible score current, valid and based on the expected information?
Risk class Business Partner-level risk classification. It can be maintained manually or determined from the score, depending on the design. Did the risk class change because the score changed, because of a manual decision, or because of a rule change?
Check rule Defines which credit-check steps SAP executes and which parameters or thresholds are used by those steps. Which check step actually failed? “Credit limit exceeded” is not the only possible negative result.
Customer credit group Classification that can support grouping, analysis or rule logic in the credit-management design. Is the account classified in the intended group, and does that classification affect the implemented policy?
Credit limit Approved limit for a credit segment. It is capacity, not the current available amount. Is the correct segment being checked, is the limit valid, and what exposure is already consuming it?
Validity Limits the period in which a score or credit limit can be treated as current, depending on the maintained data and calculation rule. Did the system recalculate or invalidate a value because its validity ended?
Special attention / block reason Additional control information that can indicate an account requiring special handling or a blocked credit account. Is the negative result caused by capacity, or by an explicit account-level restriction?
External ratings Credit information from external providers can support the internal risk assessment and score calculation. Which provider, rating date and validity were used, and did a new rating trigger recalculation?
Collateral, credit insurance, negative events and exceptions Additional credit information can qualify the risk picture and, where configured, influence checks or analyst decisions. Is the information still valid, relevant for the check and maintained at the correct profile or segment scope?

Score, risk class and limit

Automation is useful only when you can explain what drives the result.

SAP Credit Management can calculate the score, derive a risk class and calculate credit limits using configured rules. The value is not “smart” by itself: it is the output of formulas, input data, validity and process governance.

01

Collect credit signals

Internal information can include payment behavior and other credit-relevant master or transaction data. External ratings can add another source of risk information.

02

Calculate or maintain score

The scoring rule turns selected inputs into a score. Depending on design, the score can also be entered or adjusted manually.

03

Derive risk class

The risk class translates the score into a policy category that is easier to use in checks, analysis and governance.

04

Calculate segment limit

Credit-limit formulas can produce limits for the relevant credit segments. Different segments can therefore have different approved capacity for the same Business Partner.

05

Apply governance

Manual overrides, approvals, validity and change history matter because credit values are business-risk decisions, not only system calculations.

psychology_alt

Lead question: if the business says “the system calculated the wrong limit”, ask which input or formula is wrong before changing the final number. Otherwise the next recalculation can recreate the same problem.

Credit segments

A segment is a policy boundary, not another copy of the customer master.

Credit segments let the organization separate credit capacity across meaningful scopes such as business lines, regions or other risk-management structures. Sales and Finance integration maps business transactions into the relevant segment.

Why use segments?

A customer may be acceptable for one business scope but constrained in another. Segment-specific limits avoid one global number becoming the only control for every business relationship.

Integration boundary

The Sales or FI side must determine the credit-control context and map it to the credit segment used by SAP Credit Management. If that mapping is wrong, a correct credit profile can still produce the wrong business result.

Main segment

Some designs use a main credit segment to provide an aggregated view or additional control across lower-level segments. Treat this as a deliberate architecture choice and verify the actual contribution and check settings in the target system.

account_tree

Design test: every segment should answer a business question. If nobody can explain why two segments need different capacity or risk treatment, the segmentation may be adding operational complexity without better control.

Configuration blueprint

How I would set up Credit Management in a project.

I configure Credit Management from the business policy outward. First decide what risk we want to control, then build segments, rules and Sales integration around that decision. Starting from transactions normally creates configuration without a clear operating model.

01

Define the credit operating model

Decide who owns credit policy, who can change a limit, who can release a blocked document, which customers need credit checks, and what should happen when a check fails. Agree this before configuration.

02

Design credit segments

Create only the segments that represent a real risk boundary, for example a business line or region that needs its own limit. Define the segment currency and decide whether a main segment is needed for an additional aggregated control.

03

Connect Sales organization to the segment

Sales determines a credit-control context and SAP Credit Management maps that context to a credit segment. Prove the full chain from Sales Area to Credit Control Area to Credit Segment. A wrong mapping means the correct customer can still be checked against the wrong limit.

04

Define risk classes and check rules

Create a small number of risk classes that the business can explain. Then define check rules for the required policy. The check rule should contain only checks that lead to a real business action.

05

Configure the check steps

Typical checks include static or dynamic credit-limit checks, maximum document value, overdue items, oldest open item or dunning-related checks. Define the threshold and reaction deliberately. A warning, block and analyst review are different operating choices.

06

Build scoring and limit logic

If limits or risk classes are calculated automatically, define the calculation rule, formulas, input data and validity. Keep the formula understandable enough that a credit analyst can explain why the result changed.

07

Activate the Sales integration

Decide which sales document types, delivery events and item categories are credit-relevant. Make sure the relevant item values update exposure. In classic on-premise or Private Cloud configuration, Sales provides the trigger and FSCM owns the actual credit-check logic.

08

Prepare Business Partner credit data

Assign role UKM000, maintain the Credit Profile, create the required segment data, set limits and validity, and define defaults for new accounts if the project wants automatic profile creation.

09

Configure analyst workflow and authorization

Blocked documents need an owner. Define who receives documented credit decisions, who may release or reject them, which amounts need additional approval, and who is allowed to change credit master data.

10

Test policy, not only configuration

Test a new customer, a customer close to the limit, an overdue customer, different segments, a released order that is changed later, a payment that reduces exposure, and a delivery recheck. The test set should prove the business policy end to end.

architecture

Implementation rule: do not create many risk classes, check rules and segments just because SAP allows it. Every variant becomes master-data work, testing effort, authorization logic and a production support path. Keep the model as small as the business policy allows.

End-to-end process

What happens from Sales Order to credit decision.

The process is easier to remember as a sequence of decisions. Sales identifies the commercial context, Credit Management evaluates risk, and the credit analyst handles exceptions.

01

Sales document starts the check

A user creates or changes a credit-relevant sales document. Depending on the design, the check can also run at delivery or another configured checkpoint.

02

Determine the credit account

Sales identifies the account whose creditworthiness is checked. In the standard Sales flow the payer is central. This is why sold-to and credit account are not always the same business partner.

03

Determine the credit segment

The Sales organizational context leads to a Credit Control Area, which is mapped to the relevant Credit Segment. The segment tells Credit Management which limit and exposure scope to use.

04

Read the Credit Profile

The system reads global policy data such as risk class and check rule, plus the relevant segment limit and validity. Additional information such as blocks, ratings or exceptions can also affect the decision model.

05

Build the current exposure picture

Credit Management considers the commitments that belong to the account and segment. Open orders, deliveries and receivables can consume capacity at different stages of the process.

06

Execute the check rule

The check rule runs its configured steps. One step may test limit utilization, another overdue debt, another document value or payment behavior. The first useful diagnostic question is therefore “which step failed?”

07

Pass or block

If the policy is satisfied, processing continues. If a blocking check fails, the document receives a credit block and Credit Management can create a documented credit decision with the evidence behind the negative result.

08

Credit analyst reviews the exception

The analyst reviews the account, exposure, failed checks and supporting information. The analyst can wait for the risk situation to change, reject the business, or make an authorized exception decision.

09

Recheck, release or reject

Recheck means run the policy again using current data. Release means accept the risk and allow processing despite the negative result. Reject means the business should not continue under the current conditions.

10

Exposure moves with the document flow

As the process moves from order to delivery, billing and Financial Accounting, the composition of exposure changes. The commitment should move between stages rather than remain duplicated.

11

Later events can check again

A released order is not permanently “credit safe”. Relevant changes, delivery processing or another configured event can trigger a new check using the latest master data and exposure.

sync_alt

Memory model: document event → payer → segment → profile → exposure → check steps → result → analyst decision → recheck. If support follows the same sequence, the investigation normally reaches the wrong input much faster.

Process features

Details that often explain “strange” credit behavior.

Most confusing credit incidents are understandable once you know where SAP intentionally separates policy, runtime state and exception handling.

Dynamic check is time-sensitive

A dynamic limit check does not simply look at one total. It limits the considered commitments to the configured credit horizon. Two customers with the same total exposure can therefore produce different results when the timing of commitments differs.

Free limit does not guarantee a pass

The check rule may contain overdue-item, document-value, dunning or other checks. Available limit proves only that the limit step may pass.

Release is not a correction

A manual release records a business exception. If the exposure, mapping or master data is wrong, release does not repair that defect.

Main segment can add another control

Where the design uses a main credit segment, the project can require checks at main-segment level, individual segment level, or both. A local segment can therefore have free capacity while an aggregated control still blocks the document.

Exposure relevance starts before Credit Management

Sales item and document settings decide which commercial values are credit-relevant. If an item should not consume credit, that decision must be intentional and tested.

Default data can hide incomplete onboarding

With suitable defaults, Credit Management can create required credit data when the first credit check is requested. This is convenient, but it can hide that the normal customer-onboarding process did not create or review the profile explicitly.

Parent-child risk needs a clear owner

Group structures can centralize credit risk through Business Partner relationships. Decide whether the child, parent or both carry meaningful checks; otherwise the same economic risk can be controlled twice or not at all.

Billing changes exposure but is not the main check event

Billing and the transfer to Financial Accounting move the commitment toward receivables. When exposure is wrong after billing, inspect the lifecycle before assuming the credit limit itself is wrong.

Document changes can invalidate an earlier decision

Quantity, value, requested date, payer or other credit-relevant changes can make the original check result outdated. The process must define when the document is checked again.

fact_check

Best test scenario: create an order that fails, correct the real cause, recheck it, release a different order manually, then change that released order and continue to delivery. This single scenario proves much more than a happy-path “order below limit” test.

External information and security

The profile can hold more than an internal score.

Credit analysts often need evidence that does not come from Sales orders or FI open items. SAP supports external credit information and additional credit data so the decision can reflect the wider risk picture.

External credit agencies

SAP supports integration with external credit agencies. In current S/4HANA documentation, SAP S/4HANA Cloud for credit integration can bring external credit information into the Credit Management process.

Credit insurance and collateral

Insurance and collateral can be maintained with amount, validity and relevance information. They should be treated as governed risk instruments, not as free extra limit.

Negative events and exceptions

Negative credit events and check exceptions can capture information that does not fit a simple limit-versus-exposure model. Their validity and ownership should be explicit.

Data-governance point: external ratings can be stale, legally sensitive or valid only for a defined period. Keep source, date, validity and consent requirements visible in the operating model.

Runtime use

A credit block is produced from master data plus current process state.

SAP documentation names risk class, check rule and credit-limit settings as the minimum Business Partner data for credit-check execution. The check then evaluates the relevant account and segment against current exposure and the configured check steps.

01

Resolve the credit account

Start from the account actually checked by the Sales process. In standard Sales integration the payer is central; do not assume the sold-to party is always the credit account.

02

Resolve the credit segment

Prove the organizational mapping that selects the segment. A wrong segment makes every later comparison misleading.

03

Read the Credit Profile

Check risk class, check rule, score or external information and whether the profile is blocked or requires special attention.

04

Read segment capacity

Check the segment limit, validity and other segment-specific data. Then keep it separate from the runtime exposure.

05

Read exposure and failed step

Find which commitments consume capacity and which configured check step produced the negative result.

06

Choose recheck or release

Recheck after the underlying data or exposure changes. Release only when an authorized credit decision accepts the risk.

Maintenance and integration

Know which surface owns master data and which surface only shows runtime exposure.

On-premise and Private Cloud projects often use classic transactions together with Fiori apps. Public Cloud projects use released apps, configuration activities and APIs. Keep the business object stable and verify the tool for the deployment model.

Tool / object Purpose Practical use
BP role UKM000 Activates SAP Credit Management data for the Business Partner. First check when the expected credit profile or segment data is missing.
UKM_BP / BP Classic maintenance/display entry point for credit-management Business Partner data in supported on-premise or Private Cloud landscapes. Useful technical anchor for profile, risk class, check rule and segment data.
Manage Credit Accounts Fiori-based maintenance and analysis of credit accounts, profile data, segment data and additional credit information. Useful for analysts who need one account view rather than several technical transactions.
API_CRDTMBUSINESSPARTNER_0001 API for maintaining SAP Credit Management Business Partner and credit-account data. Use for governed integration of risk class, rules, segment limits and other supported credit fields instead of direct table updates.
UKM_COMMITMENTS Displays and analyzes credit exposure commitments in supported on-premise landscapes. Use when the master data looks correct but exposure is unexpectedly high or low.
Change history Credit master fields support change documents with time, user and old/new values. Use it to prove whether a block started after a limit, risk class, rule or account change.
auto_fix_high

Useful SAP behavior: when defaults for scoring/limit calculation and the default check rule are configured, SAP can create the Credit Management role, profile and segment data on-the-fly when a sales document requests a credit check. Convenient automation can therefore hide missing onboarding steps; always know whether your project relies on it.

Integration and identity

The credit result is only as good as the account identity and incoming data.

Credit Management sits between Business Partner, Sales and Finance. A field can be correct in each system and still produce the wrong answer if the account mapping or update flow is wrong.

Customer vs Business Partner

When customer and Business Partner numbers are not identical, SAP documentation points to BAdI UKM_CONV2PARTNER for deriving the correct Business Partner. This is an identity problem, not a credit-limit problem.

Sales and FI updates

Sales and Finance contribute commitments and payment information to Credit Management. Diagnose missing or duplicated exposure at the update boundary before touching risk policy.

External data

Ratings and agency data can enter from external services. Define ownership, refresh frequency, failure handling and which fields are authoritative before automating recalculation.

Troubleshooting

Do not raise the limit until you know the first wrong input.

A disciplined investigation follows the same order as the runtime decision. Each step removes a different class of root cause.

01

Which account was checked?

Prove payer and credit-account identity. If the wrong account is used, every profile comparison is irrelevant.

02

Which segment was checked?

Prove the credit-control-to-segment mapping and the segment actually used by the document.

03

Is the profile complete?

Check UKM000 role, risk class, check rule, block status, score and any relevant external information.

04

Is the segment data correct?

Check credit limit, validity and segment-specific controls. Compare calculated and manually maintained values where relevant.

05

Is exposure correct?

Reconcile the amount to Sales, delivery, billing and receivables. Exposure is a separate runtime layer.

06

Which check step failed?

Read the credit-check result and log. A customer can fail for reasons other than simple limit utilization.

07

What changed?

Use change history to correlate the first failure with changes to rule, risk class, limit, block reason or other maintained data.

08

What proves the fix?

Recheck the document after correcting the real cause. Do not use a manual release as proof that the defect is gone.

troubleshoot

Common anti-pattern: Sales says “credit is wrong”, support opens the profile, increases the limit and closes the ticket. This can hide a wrong payer, wrong segment, stale exposure, expired limit, changed check rule or explicit account block. Fix the first wrong input, not the final symptom.

S/4HANA mental reset

Do not bring classic credit-master habits into FSCM without checking the ownership model.

In S/4HANA Credit Management, the Business Partner with role UKM000, Credit Profile and Credit Segment Data form the main master-data model. Classic customer-credit transactions and tables are useful migration or historical references, but they should not drive the architecture of a new FSCM design.

Old question

“Which customer master credit field should I change?”

Better S/4HANA question

“Which Business Partner credit profile and segment are evaluated, and which system owns the risk decision?”

Lead consequence

Migration must include policy, limits, mappings, exposure behavior, analyst ownership and operational processes — not only field conversion.

Assessment recall

Profile → segment → exposure → check → decision.

record_voice_over

90-second answer: In S/4HANA, credit master data belongs to SAP Credit Management and is maintained for the Business Partner in role UKM000. I separate global Credit Profile data from Credit Segment Data. The profile contains the scoring and credit-limit rule, score, risk class, check rule and other account-wide credit information. The segment contains the credit limit and segment-specific controls. At runtime the system resolves the credit account and segment, combines this master data with current credit exposure and executes the configured check steps. That is why a credit block is not simply “limit exceeded”. For support I first prove account and segment, then profile and limit, then exposure and the failed check step. I use change history to see what changed and I recheck after fixing the real cause. A manual release is a risk decision, not a technical fix.

Strong follow-up

Can risk class differ by segment? Not in the standard model described by SAP Help. Risk class and check rule are Credit Profile data; the limit is segment-specific.

Strong diagnostic point

If the limit looks correct but the order is blocked, inspect the check rule and failed step before changing the limit.

Strong architecture point

Credit Management is a cross-process control: Sales triggers the check, BP/FSCM owns credit policy, Finance contributes exposure, and the credit analyst owns exception decisions.
