Assessment recall

Master data is useful only when its level, precedence, and consumers are clear.

Do not answer “customer master” or “material master” as one flat object. Explain the organizational slice that owns the behavior.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: I separate shared identity from organizational data. Business Partner holds the party identity; customer roles add company-code and sales-area data. Product data also has global, sales, and plant-dependent views. Customer-material and condition records add relationship or commercial context. When a document value is wrong, I trace it to the source object, exact organizational level, precedence rule, and replica used by the process. For governance, I also separate ownership of meaning, maintenance, approval, and distribution.

Diagnostic framework

Seven questions before changing master data.

Start with the business behavior, then trace it to the data layer that controls it.

01 OBJECT

What business object are we changing?

Identify the object and its key before opening a maintenance screen.

02 CONTEXT

Which business context uses this value?

The same object can behave differently in sales, purchasing, finance, planning, warehouse, or other processes.

03 SCOPE

Where is the value valid?

Check the organizational level: global, company code, plant, sales area, purchasing organization, warehouse, segment, or another scope.

04 OVERRIDE

Can more specific data replace the default?

Relationship data, conditions, determination rules, or local records may take priority over a generic value.

05 EFFECT

Which process decision does the value influence?

Connect the field to runtime behavior such as price, source, availability, tax, account, delivery, payment, or output.

06 FLOW

Which systems consume this value?

A correct source record does not prove that every replica or consuming application has the same version.

07 CONTROL

Who owns the lifecycle of this data?

Know who can create, approve, change, distribute, block, and retire the value.

OBJECT → CONTEXT → SCOPE → OVERRIDE → EFFECT → FLOW → CONTROL. Use the path to move from a business symptom to the master-data layer that may explain it.

Sales example

A wrong delivery date in a sales order is the symptom. Do not immediately change the customer or product master.

Apply the framework

Identify the object, sales or planning context, organizational scope, possible overrides, the ATP or delivery decision affected, downstream replicas, and the owner of the correction.

Business Partner

One identity, several business contexts.

I separate general BP data, customer sales-area data, company-code accounting data, partner roles, and credit data. A customer can be valid in one context and incomplete in another.

Business Partner→General identity / address
BP + FLCU01→Sales Area→Sales / Shipping / Billing defaults
BP + FLCU00→Company Code→FI customer context
BP + Credit Management→Credit Segment→Risk policy / limit

Lead lens: When order creation works but billing or credit does not, I do not call the customer master “correct”. I ask: correct for which role and organizational level?

Product

A product is also a set of organizational views.

Product behavior changes by sales organization/distribution channel and by plant. That is why “the material is correct” is not enough evidence.

Product→Basic / cross-distribution data
Product+Sales Org + DC→Sales data / plant proposal / pricing attributes
Product+Plant→ATP / MRP / loading / logistics controls

Lead lens: Compare failing and working records at the same organizational level. Comparing DE01 plant data with a global product view is a very efficient way to learn nothing.
