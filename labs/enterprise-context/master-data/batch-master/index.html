Batch Master and Classification

Batch master data identifies a specific lot of a product and can carry characteristics such as production date, shelf life, origin or quality attributes. Sales and delivery use those values when the product is batch-managed and selection matters.

Follow the runtime path arrow_downward

Object profile

01MD.SD.BATCHCatalogue ID
02logistics adjacent masterData class
Product, plant, batch, status and classification characteristics.

Mental model

The batch describes a lot; the determination strategy decides which lot to use.

database

Scope: Product, plant, batch, status and classification characteristics.

Important data

Batch number, material/product, plant or batch level depending on design, status, shelf-life dates and classification characteristics.

Configuration boundary

Batch characteristics and status are master data. The batch-determination strategy, search procedure and selection class are configuration.

Runtime use

Selection starts from the product and plant, then narrows to eligible lots.

For a batch-managed product, the process can search available lots according to the active batch-determination design. Batch master and classification describe each candidate; the strategy decides which characteristics, status, date, and other selection criteria matter. Delivery or warehouse execution then continues with the batch that was actually selected.

01

Confirm batch relevance

Start with the actual product, plant, and execution context. Prove that the product is batch-managed before analysing a batch strategy.

02

Read candidate lots

Check the batch status, classification characteristics, shelf-life or date attributes, and plant context for the lots that could satisfy the demand.

03

Trace the selection strategy

Identify the strategy or search logic that filters and sorts those candidates. Master data describes the lots; configuration explains why one candidate is preferred.

04

Prove availability and selection

An eligible batch must also be usable in the execution context. Compare candidate quantity and status with the batch finally assigned to the delivery or warehouse step.

05

Keep traceability intact

After selection, follow the actual batch through delivery and inventory evidence instead of treating classification as proof that the lot was issued.

Troubleshooting

An eligible batch and a selected batch are different states.

troubleshoot

If the expected batch is not selected, first prove that it is valid and usable for the product and plant. Then compare its status and characteristics with the active selection criteria and sorting logic. If the strategy selects it but execution still fails, move to quantity, storage, warehouse, or delivery evidence rather than changing classification blindly.

Assessment recall

Separate lot attributes from lot selection.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Batch Master describes an individual lot: product, plant context, status, dates, and classification characteristics. It does not by itself decide which batch Sales or delivery will use. I first prove the candidate lot is valid and usable, then trace the batch-determination strategy that filters and sorts candidates, and finally verify the batch actually assigned in execution. That separates master-data eligibility from selection and warehouse or delivery availability.
