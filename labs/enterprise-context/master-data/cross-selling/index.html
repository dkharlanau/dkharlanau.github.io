Cross-Selling

Cross-Selling helps the order taker offer a useful companion product at the moment a main product is entered. The main item stays in the order; SAP simply proposes related items that the user may add. Think “phone → case” or “printer → cable and toner”, not “replace one product with another”.

Mental model

Cross-selling suggests another item; it does not replace or entitle one.

database

Scope: Main product, suggested products and configured sales context/profile.

Important data

Main product, suggested product list, sales context or condition key, cross-selling profile where used and proposal sequence.

Configuration boundary

Cross-selling proposes additional products. Material determination replaces a requested product; item proposal presents a reusable predefined basket. These are distinct mechanisms.

Business picture

The recommendation should make sense because of the product already in the order.

Imagine a customer orders an industrial printer. The order taker can immediately offer the correct cable, starter toner and a maintenance kit. The customer may accept one, all or none of them. The printer remains the main item.

Good Cross-Selling case

The relationship is product-driven and easy to explain: laptop → docking station, machine → spare-parts kit, pump → compatible seal set.

Poor Cross-Selling case

The business really wants a customer-specific repeat basket, a mandatory assortment rule, automatic substitution, or a free item. Another Sales mechanism owns those requirements better.

psychology_alt

Memory rule: Cross-Selling answers “What else fits this product?” Item Proposal answers “What does this customer usually order?”

Classic setup

Do not memorize transactions first. Follow the determination chain.

Classic SAP S/4HANA Cross-Selling uses the condition technique together with a Cross-Selling profile. The profile controls how recommendations are determined and presented, while the condition setup resolves which related products belong to the triggering material.

01

Define the determination key

Choose which business fields should identify the relationship. A simple design may start from the entered material; a more specific design can include Sales context where the business needs different recommendations.

02

Build the condition technique

Maintain the condition table, access sequence and Cross-Selling determination scheme. This is the search logic SAP uses to find related products.

03

Define the Cross-Selling profile

The profile controls important runtime choices such as determination logic, pricing display, dialog behavior and whether availability should be checked for proposed items.

04

Determine the profile

Assign the profile through the relevant Sales area, document and customer procedure context. If the profile is not determined, correct relationship data alone will not produce the expected dialog.

05

Maintain relationships and test

In classic GUI landscapes, VB41 is commonly used for Cross-Selling condition records. Enter the main material in the Sales order and prove that the expected proposals appear in the intended context.

settings

Lead habit: treat transaction codes such as VB41 and the classic OV4x Customizing transactions as release-specific entry points. The reusable knowledge is the determination model: key → access → relationship → profile → dialog.

Runtime use

The main product triggers a recommendation, then ownership moves to normal Sales items.

During order entry the main product and Sales context can trigger related-product suggestions. The user can add a suggested product, but the original item remains. Once a suggestion becomes a document item, normal item-category, pricing, ATP, delivery, and billing logic take over.

01

Start with the main product

Confirm the product and Sales context that should trigger a recommendation.

02

Read the assignment

Check the maintained related products, profile, sequence, and other context used by the cross-selling design.

03

Prove the suggestion

Separate “no recommendation was produced” from “the user did not select it.” Those are different states.

04

Hand off to Sales processing

After a suggested product is added, troubleshoot its price, confirmation, delivery, or billing as a normal Sales item.

Cross-Selling vs Item Proposal

They both suggest products, but they start from different questions.

This distinction is easy to test in an assessment: remove the material from the order. If the proposal logic no longer has a trigger, you are probably discussing Cross-Selling. If the customer itself is enough to build a useful basket, you are closer to Item or Product Proposal.

Question Cross-Selling Item / Product Proposal
What triggers it? A material already entered in the Sales document. The customer and Sales context; a static proposal can also be called explicitly.
What business question does it answer? “What additional product fits the item being ordered?” “What products are relevant for this customer or repeat order?”
Typical source Material-to-related-material relationships found through Cross-Selling determination. A maintained basket, or dynamic sources such as order history, listing, customer-material data and other proposal sources.
User experience A recommendation dialog appears after the triggering material is processed; the user accepts or ignores suggestions. The user receives a customer-oriented proposal list and copies selected items or quantities into the order.
Best fit Accessories, consumables and companion products that belong naturally to another product. Repeat orders, customer-specific baskets and history-based order-entry assistance.
What happens after selection? The selected material becomes a normal Sales item. Item category, pricing, ATP/aATP, plant, shipping, delivery and billing are then controlled by their normal owners.
compare_arrows

Fast assessment answer: Cross-Selling is material-driven; Item or Product Proposal is mainly customer-driven. Cross-Selling grows the basket around an item. Item Proposal reduces repetitive order entry for a customer.

Choose the right mechanism

Similar screens can hide very different business rules.

Cross-Selling

Main material → companion product.
Use when the relationship belongs to the product being ordered.

Item / Product Proposal

Customer → likely basket.
Use when the goal is faster customer-specific order entry or history-based proposals.

Material Determination

Entered material → replacement material.
Use when the business wants substitution, not an additional line.

Free Goods

Commercial rule → free quantity or item.
Use when the extra product is an entitlement, not an optional recommendation.

Finding good combinations

Cross-Selling data can be designed from evidence, not only from opinion.

SAP documents report SDCRSL01 as a way to analyze Sales orders for products that are frequently bought together. That can reveal useful combinations before they are maintained as Cross-Selling relationships.

query_stats

Design caution: “often bought together” is a signal, not automatically a business rule. Validate compatibility, margin, availability, legal restrictions and commercial ownership before turning an observed pair into a recommendation.

Troubleshooting

Separate recommendation failure from item-processing failure.

01

Prove the trigger

Is the expected main material really in the order, and is the test using the intended Sales area and document type?

02

Prove profile determination

Check the customer/document/Sales-area context that selects the Cross-Selling profile. A missing profile can look like missing master data.

03

Prove the relationship

Confirm the relevant condition record or relationship data, validity and access key. Test the same key that the order is actually using.

04

Separate display from determination

If the system found candidates but the dialog does not behave as expected, inspect profile settings such as dialog control and availability-check behavior.

05

Stop at the ownership boundary

If the suggested item is added successfully but later has the wrong price, confirmation, plant or delivery behavior, diagnose the normal Sales item. Cross-Selling has already finished its job.

troubleshoot

Useful split: “SAP did not propose the product” and “SAP proposed it, but the new line behaves incorrectly” are two different incidents with different owners.

Deployment boundary

Do not carry classic GUI configuration into every S/4HANA deployment model.

The classic Cross-Selling function described on this page is documented by SAP for S/4HANA On-Premise. SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition has its own product-proposal scope, including Intelligent Product Proposal. For a Public Edition design, confirm the available scope and apps first instead of assuming that VB41 or classic OV4x configuration is available.

Assessment recall

Recommendation is not substitution or entitlement.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: Cross-Selling is a material-driven recommendation function. When a user enters a main product, SAP can propose related products such as accessories or consumables. Classic determination uses the condition technique plus a Cross-Selling profile. The user decides whether to add a proposal; the main item is not replaced. That is why Cross-Selling is different from Material Determination, Free Goods and customer-driven Item Proposal. For support, I check the trigger, profile determination and relationship data first, then hand any pricing, ATP or fulfillment issue to the normal Sales-item logic.

quiz

Assessment trap: if the interviewer asks for recommendations based on customer purchase history, do not answer Cross-Selling. SAP explicitly separates material-triggered Cross-Selling from customer-driven Dynamic Product Proposal.
