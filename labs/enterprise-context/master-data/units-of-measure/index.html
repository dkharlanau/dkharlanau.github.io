Units of Measure and Quantity Constraints

Units of measure translate commercial quantities into the units used by inventory, pricing and logistics. Incorrect conversion data can look like a pricing, ATP or delivery issue even when the root cause is the product's UoM setup.

Mental model

Know what the object owns before tracing a document.

database

Scope: Product, base UoM, alternative UoM, sales UoM and distribution chain.

Important data

Base unit, alternative units, conversion numerator/denominator, sales unit, delivery unit and quantity rounding or minimum-order constraints where maintained.

Configuration boundary

The conversion factors belong to product master data. Pricing units, rounding rules and process-specific quantity controls may involve additional configuration or document logic.

Runtime use

How the data enters Order-to-Cash.

Sales order quantities may be entered in a sales unit, converted to base quantity for ATP and inventory, and priced per another pricing unit. Delivery and EDI/API messages also depend on consistent unit semantics.

Troubleshooting

Start with the most specific evidence.

troubleshoot

Compare order unit, pricing unit, base unit and delivered unit. Recalculate the conversion manually. If only one material is affected, inspect the alternative-UoM conversion before changing pricing or ATP configuration.

Assessment recall

A concise answer that still shows depth.

record_voice_over

For quantity problems I map every unit: entered sales unit, pricing unit, base unit and delivery unit. Then I verify the product conversion factors and only after that the consuming process.
