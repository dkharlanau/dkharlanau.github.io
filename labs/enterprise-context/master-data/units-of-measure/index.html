Units of Measure and Quantity Constraints

Units of measure translate commercial quantities into the units used by inventory, pricing and logistics. Incorrect conversion data can look like a pricing, ATP or delivery issue even when the root cause is the product's UoM setup.

Follow the runtime path arrow_downward

Object profile

01MD.SD.UOMCatalogue ID
02logistics adjacent masterData class
Product, base UoM, alternative UoM, sales UoM and distribution chain.

Mental model

One business quantity can be represented by several units at different process steps.

database

Scope: Product, base UoM, alternative UoM, sales UoM and distribution chain.

Important data

Base unit, alternative units, conversion numerator/denominator, sales unit, delivery unit and quantity rounding or minimum-order constraints where maintained.

Configuration boundary

The conversion factors belong to product master data. Pricing units, rounding rules and process-specific quantity controls may involve additional configuration or document logic.

Runtime use

The same line can have an entered unit, base unit, pricing unit, and delivery unit.

Sales can accept a commercial sales unit, convert it to the product's base quantity for inventory and availability, calculate price using a pricing unit, and later execute delivery in a logistics unit. Interfaces add another boundary because quantity without a clear unit code is not a complete business value.

01

Map every unit

Write down the entered sales unit, base unit, pricing unit, and delivery or interface unit involved in the symptom.

02

Read conversion factors

Check the product's alternative-unit numerator and denominator and any Sales-specific unit proposal or quantity constraints.

03

Recalculate the base quantity

Convert the document quantity manually. This quickly separates bad master conversion from later ATP, pricing, or logistics logic.

04

Trace the consuming process

For price issues, inspect pricing unit and basis. For confirmation issues, inspect base quantity. For delivery issues, inspect execution unit and rounding.

05

Validate interfaces

Compare both quantity and unit semantics across API, EDI, or other integration payloads; equal numbers with different units are not equal business values.

Troubleshooting

Do the unit conversion before changing Pricing or ATP.

troubleshoot

Compare order unit, pricing unit, base unit, and delivered unit, then recalculate the conversion manually. If the base quantity is already wrong, stay in product/UoM master data. If the conversion is correct, move to the mechanism that consumes it: pricing basis, ATP requirement, delivery rounding, or integration mapping.

Assessment recall

Map units before mechanisms.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: For quantity problems I first map the units: entered Sales unit, product base unit, pricing unit, and delivery or interface unit. Then I verify the conversion factors and calculate the expected base quantity manually. If that conversion is wrong, the root cause is in product/UoM data. If it is correct, I move to the consuming process such as pricing, ATP, delivery rounding, or integration. This prevents a quantity-conversion problem from looking like four different module defects.
