Sales BOM: structure is only half of the design

A Sales BOM lets one sellable header product represent a structure of components in a sales document. The BOM answers which components belong to the product. Sales configuration decides which level carries price, availability, delivery, billing and requirements.

This distinction is the key to both implementation and troubleshooting. A correct BOM can still behave incorrectly in Sales, and a correct item-category design cannot repair a BOM that selected the wrong structure.

Mental model

Four control layers turn a product structure into Sales behavior.

Do not treat “Sales BOM” as one setting. At runtime, several independent control layers work together.

1. Product master

The header material carries an item category group such as ERLA or LUMF. This is an important input to Sales item-category determination.

2. BOM master data

The material BOM defines components, quantities, validity, plant context, alternative BOM and BOM usage. In classic standard Sales BOM processing, usage 5 represents Sales and Distribution.

3. Explosion control

BOM usage, BOM application, validity, alternative selection, structure scope and, for configurable products, configuration-profile filters decide which structure can be exploded.

4. Sales controls

The resulting header and subitem categories control pricing, delivery relevance, schedule lines, requirements, billing behavior and other Sales functions.

account_tree

Lead rule: ask two separate questions. What structure did SAP select? Then ask which document level owns the commercial and logistics process? Mixing these questions creates most Sales BOM troubleshooting mistakes.

Configuration chain

Configure the chain from material to document, not isolated transactions.

The exact UI depends on deployment. The transaction codes below are useful classic S/4HANA on-premise or private-edition references; do not assume the same maintenance path in Public Cloud.

01

Choose the business model

Decide whether the customer buys one assembled product, a kit of separate components, or a hybrid where pricing and logistics live at different levels. This decision comes before the SAP codes.

02

Maintain the header material

Set the Sales item category group that represents the intended processing model. Classic examples are ERLA for header-level processing and LUMF for component-level processing.

03

Create the material BOM

In classic GUI, CS01 creates the material BOM. Maintain material, plant where relevant, BOM usage, validity, alternative BOM, components and quantities. Standard Sales BOM examples commonly use BOM usage 5.

04

Check item-category determination

In classic SD, item categories are defined in VOV7 and determined through VOV4. For subitems, the higher-level item category is an important determination input.

05

Enable the required structure scope

The main item category must allow the required BOM or configuration structure. Do not memorize one structure-scope code as universal: the relevant value depends on the scenario and release.

06

Add variant-configuration controls when needed

For configurable materials, the configuration profile adds another control plane: process type, single- or multi-level explosion, BOM application and filters. A standard Sales Order SET profile commonly uses Sales Order processing and application SD01.

07

Prove the result in a sales order

Enter the header product and inspect the hierarchy, selected item categories, schedule lines, pricing, confirmations and delivery relevance. The document result is the proof that the configuration chain works together.

Processing models

ERLA and LUMF answer a business ownership question.

The useful way to remember the models is not the code itself. Remember where the sale is valued and where the goods are executed.

Model Business meaning Pricing owner Logistics / inventory owner Common standard item categories
ERLA Header-level processing. Typical when the customer buys one assembled or commercially indivisible product. Header Header TAQ header, TAE subitems in standard examples
LUMF Component-level processing. Typical when the header is a kit label and the physical components are the real sales items. Components Components TAP header, TAN subitems in standard examples
CPFH Hybrid sales-kit model: total price at header, logistics at component level. Header Components Current S/4HANA Cloud sales-kit scope; verify availability and categories in the target release
DISH Hybrid sales-kit model: logistics at header, price carried by components. Components Header Current S/4HANA Cloud sales-kit scope; verify availability and categories in the target release
cloud

Release boundary: ERLA and LUMF are the classic core models. Current SAP S/4HANA Cloud sales-kit documentation also describes CPFH and DISH. Treat the hybrid models as release- and scope-dependent, not as a guarantee for every landscape.

ERLA vs. LUMF

The same BOM can support very different commercial behavior.

ERLA: sell one product

The header is the commercial and logistics object. It carries the price and standard delivery processing. The exploded components explain the structure but are normally not independently priced or delivered.

Example: a pre-assembled workstation sold as one SKU. The customer buys one workstation even though the internal structure contains a monitor, keyboard and other parts.

LUMF: sell the kit components

The header is mainly structural. Pricing and delivery processing take place on the component subitems. Standard processing can create a delivery group so the components are coordinated and the latest component date becomes relevant to the group.

Example: a promotional kit where the physical items can also be sold independently and must be picked and delivered as components.

fact_check

Do not overlearn schedule-line codes: examples may show categories such as CP or CT, but a schedule-line category is determined separately from the BOM item category. MRP type and SD schedule-line configuration matter. Prove the schedule line in the document instead of treating it as an intrinsic property of ERLA or LUMF.

Sales-order runtime

Read the explosion as a determination chain.

A Sales BOM problem becomes easier when you follow the same order in which the system needs the information.

01

Enter the header product

The sales order first receives the material, sales area, plant context and sales-document type that drive later determination.

02

Determine the main item category

The header material's item category group participates in item-category determination. This is where the ERLA/LUMF processing model starts to become document behavior.

03

Select a valid BOM

The system needs a BOM that is valid for the relevant material, plant/application context, date, usage, status and alternative-selection rules.

04

Explode the allowed structure

The BOM application, structure scope and any configuration-profile filters decide what is copied into the sales document and how deep the explosion goes.

05

Determine subitem categories

Each copied component receives Sales behavior through item-category determination. For subitems, the relationship to the higher-level item is part of the logic.

06

Run downstream functions at the active level

Pricing, schedule lines, requirements, ATP, delivery and billing now act on the header or components according to the resulting document controls.

Troubleshooting

First classify the failure: structure or behavior.

This is the fastest diagnostic split. Do not change BOM master data when the structure is correct but Sales behavior is wrong.

A. BOM does not explode, or components are wrong

Start with the structure-selection path: header material, plant context, valid-from date, BOM usage, BOM status, alternative BOM, lot-size or selection rules, BOM application, structure scope and configuration-profile filters.

B. Components are correct, but price or delivery is wrong

Move away from BOM maintenance. Inspect main and subitem categories, pricing relevance, schedule lines, requirements, delivery relevance, billing relevance and copy-control consequences.

01

Prove the header control

Check the material item category group and the actual main item category created in the sales order.

02

Prove a valid BOM candidate

Confirm material, plant/application context, usage, validity, status and alternative. If SAP has no eligible BOM, there is nothing to explode.

03

Prove the explosion application

Check which BOM application is active and which BOM usages it searches in which priority. A technically valid BOM can still be the wrong candidate for Sales.

04

Prove structure scope and filters

For configurable products, verify the configuration profile, explosion depth and filters. A filter can intentionally remove components from the document.

05

Prove Sales relevance

In Sales Order SET processing, only BOM items relevant to Sales are copied as sales-order subitems. Do not assume every BOM item must appear.

06

Prove the resulting item controls

When the hierarchy is correct, inspect the actual item and schedule-line categories before changing master data. They explain most pricing, ATP and delivery differences.

troubleshoot

Common trap: “the BOM is there in CS03” does not prove that Sales can use it. The real proof is that the sales-order application finds the correct valid BOM and that the selected item category permits the intended explosion.

BOM application

Usage says where a BOM may be used; application controls how the consumer searches.

This distinction becomes especially important in variant configuration. The active application can define a priority of BOM usages. The first valid BOM found may not be the BOM you expected from a Sales-only view.

BOM usage

A property of the BOM that defines its intended application area. In classic standard processing, usage 5 is Sales and Distribution.

BOM application

A runtime control used by the consuming process. It can define which usages are searched and in which priority. For configurable Sales processing, SD01 is a common standard application.

Configuration-profile filter

A configurable-material profile can restrict which BOM items are relevant for the configuration and Sales process. The master BOM may contain more items than the sales order should show.

priority_high

Diagnostic implication: if an application searches another usage first, SAP can select that BOM before the Sales BOM. The existence of usage 5 alone does not guarantee it will win the search.

Configurable materials

Sales Order SET adds configuration logic to normal Sales BOM logic.

For LO-VC or Advanced Variant Configuration scenarios, the configuration profile controls more than characteristics. It also controls when and how the BOM is exploded.

01

Define Sales Order processing

Use a configuration profile designed for Sales Order processing. Standard SET processing can use application SD01.

02

Choose explosion depth

Single-level processing exposes only the first BOM level. Multi-level processing can continue into subordinate configurable assemblies when their profiles allow it.

03

Mark the Sales-relevant BOM items

SET processing copies only Sales-relevant BOM items into the sales order. The BOM can therefore contain technical components that remain outside the Sales document.

04

Align item-category behavior

If component-level requirements transfer and pricing are required, the configurable header must use a processing model that creates the needed sales-relevant subitems. Standard SET examples use item category group 0004.

05

Test configuration and logistics together

A configuration can be logically complete but still fail later because a selected component has no confirmation, wrong plant, wrong item category or missing delivery relevance.

layers

Keep two “multi-level” concepts separate: normal SD structure explosion is controlled through Sales item-category structure settings, while configurable-material explosion also depends on configuration profiles, applications and filters. In a complex product, both layers can matter.

Single vs. multi-level

Choose explosion depth from the business document you need.

Single-level explosion

The sales order receives the first level below the entered material. A subordinate assembly can still have its own BOM, but that deeper structure is not automatically shown by a single-level Sales explosion.

Multi-level explosion

The structure can continue into lower assemblies. This gives Sales more visibility and control but also creates more document items, more determination points and more opportunities for inconsistent master data.

Lead trade-off

Do not explode more levels only because SAP can. Show a lower level in Sales only when Sales, pricing, requirements, ATP, delivery, configuration or customer communication needs that level as a real document object.

ATP, requirements and delivery

The BOM does not perform ATP; it creates the items on which ATP can act.

Availability behavior follows the active document level. Start from the item and schedule line that transfers requirements and is relevant for logistics, then continue into the ATP or aATP design.

ERLA

The header is the normal inventory and delivery object. Diagnose its schedule line, requirements transfer, checking group/rule and confirmation. The subitems are usually structural information rather than independent ATP objects.

LUMF

The components are the logistics objects. Availability can therefore differ by component. Delivery-group logic can coordinate dates so the kit is not promised as if each component were an unrelated sale.

Configurable SET

Requirements are meaningful only for the sales-relevant lower-level items created by the configuration. An incomplete or inconsistent configuration can prevent the expected requirements from reaching planning.

inventory

Do not infer the goods-movement type from ERLA or LUMF alone. Standard sell-from-stock outbound delivery often uses movement type 601, but movement type is a downstream logistics result and can differ by process. Prove the delivery item, schedule line and movement configuration.

Goods issue and billing

Follow the item that carries execution and value.

For standard ERLA and LUMF processing, pricing, delivery and billing normally stay on the same logical level. This makes the model easy to remember, but the actual document still depends on item-category, schedule-line, billing-relevance and copy-control configuration.

Item category Role Pricing Delivery / goods issue Billing
TAQ ERLA main item Yes Yes — header is the logistics item Yes — standard billable value is carried by the header
TAE ERLA subitem No No — normally structural / explanatory only No separate billing item
TAP LUMF main item No No — header is mainly structural No separate billing item
TAN LUMF subitem Yes Yes — each component is a logistics item Yes — components normally become the billable items
rule

Configuration boundary: the table describes standard delivered behavior. Do not diagnose a productive system from the category name alone. Verify the item category in VOV7, the schedule line, billing relevance, copy control and the actual delivery/billing document.

ERLA goods issue

The main item is delivery-relevant, so the goods movement is posted for the header material. The TAE components do not normally create separate outbound-delivery or goods-issue items.

In a standard sell-from-stock outbound process, movement type 601 is a common result. Treat this as a downstream logistics result, not as a property built into TAQ.

LUMF goods issue

The TAN components are the delivery items. Each physical component can therefore post its own goods issue. The TAP header does not normally post a separate goods movement.

Again, 601 is typical for standard customer delivery from own stock, but the real movement type must be proved from the delivery and movement configuration.

ERLA billing

The invoice normally contains the commercial header item. The customer sees and is charged for the finished product or kit as one commercial object. TAE components do not normally create separate billing lines.

LUMF billing

The billable value sits on the TAN components. The invoice can therefore show separate component quantities and prices, while TAP remains a structural parent.

Delivery group behavior

LUMF components can be separate items without becoming separate customer promises.

When components are the executable items, SAP can correlate them through a delivery group. The goal is to prevent one component from being promised as if the rest of the kit did not exist.

01

ATP confirms components

Each delivery-relevant component can have its own confirmation date and quantity.

02

Delivery group correlates the dates

The system can align the BOM items to the first date on which all required items in the group can be delivered together.

03

The latest constraint drives the group

If one component is available later than the others, that later component can determine the executable date for the whole delivery group.

04

Configuration decides the correlation rule

The main-item category controls whether correlated schedule lines are generated. Current S/4HANA documentation supports delivery-group modes for date correlation and date-plus-quantity correlation.

local_shipping

Lead point: LUMF is component-level logistics, not necessarily independent component delivery. The delivery group is what turns several component confirmations back into one coordinated customer promise.

Profitability and margin analysis

Billing level suggests the reporting grain, but account assignment still owns the accounting result.

It is useful to expect header-level commercial margin for ERLA and component-level commercial margin for LUMF, but do not state this as an unconditional CO-PA rule. Profitability-segment derivation, account assignment, COGS processing and the active Margin Analysis design can change the final reporting grain.

ERLA

The header carries price, delivery and billing in the standard model, so revenue and COGS evidence normally concentrates on the header product. This supports finished-product or kit-level margin analysis when account assignment follows the standard structure.

LUMF

The components carry price, delivery and billing, so profitability can be analysed at component level when the accounting objects and profitability characteristics follow those items.

DISH

Current S/4HANA Cloud Sales Kit documentation explicitly treats the header as the logistics object while subitems carry price. For this model, SAP documents profitability reporting at main-item level and propagates the profitability segment from the main item to subitems.

analytics

Support edge case: SAP KBA 3441527 documents a specific situation where message KE350 is not issued for a billing item with category TAQ, while the same product can raise the message with another item category. Treat this as a narrow CO-PA diagnostic clue, not as a general rule for all TAQ billing.

DISH billing variants

Hybrid Sales Kits can separate logistics, pricing and invoice behavior.

Current S/4HANA Cloud Sales Kit scope includes the DISH model, where logistics stay at the main item and pricing is carried by the subitems. SAP also provides main-item variants for normal, rebated and free-of-charge processing.

Item category Purpose Pricing / invoice behavior Logistics / profitability behavior
FPHD Default DISH main item Subitems carry the sales price Main item is logistics-relevant; SAP documents profitability reporting at main-item level
FBHD Free-of-charge with invoice A 100% rebate makes the item free of charge while the billing process still exists Main-item logistics model remains
FFOC Free-of-charge without billing No pricing and no invoice Process ends after delivery
cloud

Scope boundary: these DISH variants come from current S/4HANA Cloud Sales Kit scope. Verify availability, naming and supported combinations in the exact target release before copying them into an on-premise or private-edition design.

Returns and credit memos

Preserve the BOM hierarchy when the commercial flow reverses.

A returns process is not only a negative copy of the original order. Follow-on documents must preserve the higher-level item relationship if item-category determination and BOM structure still depend on it.

01

Trace the source hierarchy

Record the BOM header, subitems and higher-level item references in the returns order.

02

Inspect the generated credit memo request

Check whether the parent-child relationship is still present. In classic document structures, field UEPOS identifies the higher-level item.

03

Re-check item-category determination

If the hierarchy is lost, SAP can determine an unexpected item category or fail to reproduce the intended BOM header/subitems.

04

Separate product logic from a known product defect

SAP KBA 3122379 documents this symptom for advanced returns: BOM hierarchy information can be lost when the credit memo request is generated.

assignment_return

Diagnostic rule: when a returns or refund document suddenly loses the expected BOM item categories, compare hierarchy fields and document flow before changing the BOM or item-category customizing. The root cause can be the copy/generation step, not master data.

Sales BOM vs. Order BOM

Reusable product structure and customer-specific structure are different master-data decisions.

An Order BOM should not be described as just another Sales BOM alternative. It is tied to a specific sales order item and exists so one customer-specific structure can change without changing the reusable material BOM.

Question Sales BOM / material BOM used in Sales Order BOM
Reuse Reusable master structure for many sales documents. Specific to one sales order item.
Identity Material BOM context such as material, plant, usage, alternative and validity. Uniquely linked by sales order, sales order item and material.
Why use it? Sell a standard kit, set or structured product. Change the structure for one customer/order without changing the global material BOM.
Variant configuration Can explode directly in the sales order in SET-style processing when configured. In Order BOM processing, the header can be configured in Sales while detailed BOM processing is handled as an order-specific structure.
Classic maintenance CS01/CS02/CS03 are common material-BOM transactions. Classic Order BOM maintenance is available from the Order BOM functions; exact transactions and apps depend on deployment and release.
engineering

Architecture rule: if a customer-specific change must survive into MRP, procurement or production without changing the standard product definition, evaluate Order BOM rather than cloning a new global material BOM for every order.

Integration boundary

Do not let an interface create the structure twice.

When an external channel, CPQ solution or integration creates a sales document, decide whether SAP should derive the BOM hierarchy or whether the incoming document already owns the component structure.

01

Define the structural owner

If SAP owns the product structure, send the header product and the configuration inputs required for SAP to determine the correct components.

02

Avoid duplicate explosion

If a preceding document already contains an authoritative hierarchy, test reference creation carefully so follow-on processing does not recreate the same subitems a second time.

03

Preserve parent-child identity

Integration mappings must preserve higher-level item relationships, quantities and configuration references. A flat list of materials is not equivalent to a BOM hierarchy.

04

Prefer released interfaces

For Order BOM integration, SAP provides a released Order Bills of Material OData service in current S/4HANA on-premise documentation. Prefer released APIs over designing new integrations around unreleased or legacy function modules.

05

Reconcile the result

After creation, compare the expected hierarchy with the actual sales document or Order BOM. The interface response alone does not prove that downstream pricing and logistics behavior is correct.

Test decisions, not only a successful VA01 save.

Lead design decisions

Ask these questions before choosing the SAP setup.

What does the customer buy?

One commercial product, separate components, or a bundle where price and physical execution live at different levels?

What must ATP promise?

Availability of one header object or simultaneous availability of several components? This choice affects requirements and delivery coordination.

What must the invoice show?

One bundle price, component prices, or both for information? Pricing design should match the commercial contract and billing output.

Who owns structure changes?

Product engineering for all future orders, Sales for a reusable kit, or engineering/configuration for one specific customer order?

How deep must Sales see?

Every technical component does not need to become a sales-order item. Expose only levels that Sales or downstream execution must control.

Which deployment are we designing for?

Classic GUI transactions, Cloud configuration activities, released APIs and supported kit variants differ by S/4HANA deployment and release.

Assessment recall

Explain the ownership chain, not a list of transaction codes.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: A Sales BOM separates product structure from Sales processing. The BOM defines the components and quantities. In the material master, the item category group such as ERLA or LUMF helps determine how the structure behaves in the sales order. ERLA normally means pricing and logistics at header level; LUMF moves them to the components. The explosion itself depends on a valid BOM plus usage, application, structure scope and, for configurable products, the configuration profile. If a BOM does not explode, I first prove BOM selection and explosion control. If it explodes correctly but pricing, ATP or delivery is wrong, I move to item categories and schedule lines instead of changing the BOM.

Follow-up: ERLA vs. LUMF?

Answer with ownership: ERLA = header commercial/logistics object; LUMF = component commercial/logistics objects.

Follow-up: BOM exists but no explosion?

Prove effective BOM, usage/application priority, item category structure scope and configuration-profile filters.

Follow-up: Sales BOM vs. Order BOM?

Sales BOM is reusable product structure. Order BOM is a sales-order-specific structure for one customer requirement.

Follow-up: where is ATP?

ATP follows the active logistics items and their schedule lines. The BOM creates structure; it does not replace ATP logic.
