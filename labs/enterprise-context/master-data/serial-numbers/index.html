SAP Logistics / Traceability

Serial numbers: identity below the material level.

Material identifies the product. Batch identifies a lot. Serial number identifies one exact physical unit. This distinction matters when the business needs unit-level traceability, service history, warranty, or controlled goods movements.

Start with the mental model arrow_downward
rule

Boundary: The material master controls serial-number relevance through the serial-number profile. The serial master identifies the unit. A delivery, goods movement, or service document proves where that unit participated in a transaction.

Mental model

Do not confuse product, batch, serial number, and equipment.

These objects answer different questions. A Lead should know which identity owns which part of the lifecycle.

Material / Product

What type of thing is this, and which serial-number procedures apply?

Serial Number

Which individual unit is this?

Batch

Which production or procurement lot did it come from, when batch management is also used?

Equipment

Which technical object has maintenance or service lifecycle data, when equipment is created or linked?

Configuration

The Serial Number Profile is the control center.

In classic S/4HANA configuration, a four-character Serial Number Profile is defined in OIS2 and assigned to the material at plant level. In the classic material master, the plant-level profile is represented by MARC-SERNP. Public Cloud uses configuration activities and Fiori product apps instead of assuming the same GUI path.

01

Decide the control point

Goods receipt, production, sales order, delivery, warehouse movement, return, service, or several of these. Start from the business need, not from the transaction.

02

Define the profile

Set the general rules for serial-number existence, stock consistency, automatic number creation, equipment requirement, and equipment category where equipment integration is needed.

03

Add serialization procedures

Each procedure represents a business operation. The usage then tells SAP whether serial entry is inactive, optional, obligatory, or automatic where supported.

04

Assign the profile to the material

The assignment is plant-specific. The same product can therefore be serialized in one plant and not in another, subject to the logistics design and transfer constraints.

05

Align EWM if used

ERP/LE and EWM serialization must be compatible. Decide whether the warehouse needs document-level, warehouse-level, or exact bin-level serial tracking.

06

Test the full lifecycle

Test inbound, internal movement, outbound, return, production, and service handoffs. A profile that works at goods receipt can still fail later at picking or PGI.

rule

Design rule: do not copy one “standard profile” to every product. A low-value component, a medical device, an industrial pump, and a serviceable machine can need very different serialization depth. Every mandatory scan adds control and operating cost.

Procurement and Inventory Management

At goods receipt, quantity becomes individual pieces.

If serialization is obligatory, receiving quantity 10 is not complete until ten individual units are identified. This is the cleanest way to separate quantity management from unit identity.

External serial

Use the supplier or manufacturer identifier when the physical product already carries the authoritative serial number.

Internal serial

SAP can generate internal serial numbers when the company owns the numbering scheme. Supported extensibility can generate controlled custom patterns.

Stock consistency

The profile can check whether the recorded serial-number stock situation is consistent with the material movement, helping prevent impossible unit movements.

Sales and Shipping

Order-time and delivery-time serial selection solve different problems.

Serial in Sales Order

Use this when the customer is buying a specific physical unit and the identity is already known, for example a used asset, demo device, or reserved machine.

Serial in Delivery

Use this when Sales promises product and quantity while warehouse execution selects the actual physical pieces.

PGI control

Before Post Goods Issue, the delivery quantity and assigned serials must satisfy the configured completeness and serialization rules.

local_shipping

Integration boundary: when an upstream S/4 delivery already carries serial numbers, EWM cannot simply substitute different physical units without respecting the serial-number handoff. The document identity and the physical pick must stay aligned.

Production

Choose when a manufactured unit receives its identity.

Depending on the manufacturing scenario, serial numbers can be required or generated around order release, production execution, assembly, or finished-goods receipt. The right point is the earliest moment when the business can reliably preserve the identity through scrap, rework, and rescheduling.

precision_manufacturing

Trade-off: earlier serial assignment gives stronger traceability but creates more handling when the planned unit is scrapped, replaced, or reworked before it becomes the final product.

EWM

EWM controls how deeply the unit is tracked inside the warehouse.

“Serialized” is not a single warehouse setting. The serial-number requirement defines how much location detail EWM must preserve.

RequirementWhat EWM knowsOperational consequenceTypical fit
A — Document ItemSerials on relevant inbound/outbound documentsNo continuous exact internal location trackingReturns validation or customer traceability
B — Warehouse NumberWhich serialized units are inside the warehouseSerial capture at GR/GI, not every internal moveWarehouse-level unit visibility
C — Inventory ManagementExact stock/bin context of each unitEvery relevant warehouse movement becomes serial-awareHigh-value or regulated units
D — No RequirementNo EWM serial requirement for this warehouseThe product can still be serialized elsewhereWarehouse-specific exception
warehouse

Trade-off: A → B → C gives more traceability but also more scanning, validation, and exception handling. Choose the lowest level that still meets the business control.

From SAP S/4HANA 2020, harmonized serial number profiles can be used for embedded and S/4HANA-based decentralized EWM. Changing serialization for products that already have warehouse stock or open warehouse documents needs controlled migration; it is not a casual master-data change.

Serial Number and Equipment

Create Equipment only when the unit needs a technical lifecycle.

Serial only

Enough when the main requirement is logistics traceability: receive, move, ship, return, and prove document history.

Serial + Equipment

Use when the individual unit becomes a maintainable technical object with maintenance orders, installation history, technical location, warranty, or service lifecycle.

Synchronization

The profile controls equipment requirements and serialization level. Some designs synchronize serial and equipment identifiers; others keep separate identifiers linked to the same unit.

build

Lead decision: do not create Equipment for every serialized product by default. Equipment adds lifecycle capability, but also master-data governance and maintenance responsibility.

For reporting or integration, do not treat one table as “the serial-number table”. Serial history is distributed across the object list and document-specific structures. SAP documents OBJK together with serial-document tables such as SER01 for deliveries, SER03 for goods movements, SER05 for production orders, and SER07 for physical inventory. For new integrations, prefer released CDS views and APIs over fragile direct-table logic where possible.

Lead diagnosis

Start from the failed checkpoint, not from OIS2.

Serial-number incidents often look like profile problems but are actually identity, status, document-handoff, or warehouse-state problems.

“Serial number is required”

Check material/profile → active procedure → usage → concrete movement or delivery context. Do not change the profile until you know which rule made this step mandatory.

“Serial already exists / cannot be received”

Display the serial and its history. Prove whether it is already in stock, assigned to another open document, delivered, installed, or otherwise in a conflicting status.

Delivery cannot PGI

Compare delivery quantity with assigned serials, then inspect delivery serialization/completeness rules and the serials returned from warehouse execution.

EWM serial mismatch

Check ERP/LE and EWM profile compatibility, the active A/B/C/D requirement, whether the serial was copied from the upstream delivery, and where EWM requires capture.

Serial exists but stock is wrong

Separate master existence from stock state. Trace the latest material or warehouse movement and serial history before attempting a master-data correction.

Profile change is blocked

For EWM-managed stock, inspect warehouse stock, open warehouse tasks, and open delivery documents. Serialization changes can require stock migration or profile repair.

troubleshoot

Diagnostic sequence: material/profile → procedure/usage → document quantity → assigned serials → serial status/history → stock/warehouse location → upstream/downstream integration.

record_voice_over

60-second assessment answer: “A serial number identifies one physical unit of a material. I control serialization through a Serial Number Profile assigned at plant level. The profile defines the business procedures — for example goods movements, delivery, or production — and for each procedure whether serial numbers are none, optional, obligatory, or automatic. Then I decide where the unit identity must be captured: at GR, production, delivery, warehouse movement, return, or service. If EWM is used, I also choose the warehouse tracking depth: document item, warehouse level, or exact inventory/bin level. Equipment is a separate decision and is needed when the unit requires a maintenance or service lifecycle.”

Batch vs Serial?

Batch identifies one lot shared by many pieces. Serial number identifies one individual piece. A material can use both.

Serial vs Equipment?

Serial number provides logistics identity. Equipment provides a technical object for maintenance and service lifecycle.

Why not EWM level C everywhere?

Because every relevant internal movement becomes serial-aware. Extra traceability must justify scanning and exception-handling cost.
