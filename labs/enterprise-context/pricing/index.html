Lead answer frame

Do not explain pricing from the final net value.

A short Lead answer should show where the pricing path first diverged. That gives the interviewer a diagnosis, not a list of configuration objects.

1. Context and procedure

  • State the expected commercial result and document context.
  • Confirm the pricing procedure before checking one condition.
  • If the procedure is wrong, trace its determination inputs upstream.

2. Search and calculation

  • Check condition eligibility, access sequence, and runtime key.
  • Prove whether a valid record was found and whether it stayed active.
  • Separate rate, base, scale, formula, and final condition value.

3. Boundary and proof

  • Compare order and billing as separate pricing contexts when required.
  • Check custom-field or routine inputs before changing shared configuration.
  • Explain blast radius, proof of cause, and proof of corrected value.

Good diagnostic sentence: “The record exists, but first I need to prove that the expected procedure, condition, access, and runtime key were actually used.”

How I read pricing analysis

I look for the first divergence.

Pricing analysis is more useful than the final amount because it tells me what the engine tried, what it found, and what it did not use.

  1. 01Procedure

    Was the expected pricing procedure determined?

  2. 02Step

    Is the expected condition type in the procedure and eligible?

  3. 03Access

    Which condition tables were checked, and in which order?

  4. 04Key

    Which runtime field values formed the access key?

  5. 05Result

    Was the condition missing, found, inactive, excluded, or manually changed?

  6. 06Math

    What rate, base, scale, formula, and subtotal produced the value?

A classic custom-field chain

The field is only the beginning.

A frequent design mistake is to celebrate when a custom field exists on the order. Pricing does not care about the celebration. The value must reach pricing communication and then be consumed by the condition technique.

Business Attribute→ Pricing Communication→ Field Catalog→ Condition Table→ Access Sequence→ Condition Type→ Condition Record

Order vs billing

Correct order pricing does not prove correct billing pricing.

When custom pricing fields or routines are involved, I compare both documents as separate runtime contexts. The useful question is not "why did billing change the price?" but "where is the first difference between the two pricing paths?"

Sales Order→ Pricing Analysis↔ Compare Context & Custom Fields↔ Billing Pricing Analysis← Billing Document

Classic implementations often need equivalent pricing-communication population on the billing side. The diagnostic goal is parity of required inputs, not blind duplication of code.
