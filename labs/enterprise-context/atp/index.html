Lead answer frame

Separate promise logic from the stock number.

A good ATP answer explains why a requirement could or could not be confirmed on a date. It does not stop at “stock exists” or “ATP is zero”.

1. Build the requirement

  • Confirm ATP relevance and the actual requirement quantity.
  • Check plant and material availability date before reading supply.
  • Separate a scheduling-date problem from an availability problem.

2. Build the promise

  • Check scope and the supply-demand picture used by the availability check.
  • Explain the PAC result before adding advanced policy controls.
  • Then inspect PAL, Supply Protection, alternatives, or BOP when they are active.

3. Prove the owner

  • Show whether the first divergence is date, supply, scope, or business policy.
  • Trace alternative plant/product decisions into Shipping, EWM, TM, tax, or margin impact.
  • Close with proof of confirmation and downstream business feasibility.

Boundary with Shipping: ATP answers what can be promised. Shipping and scheduling explain how the logistics dates and execution context are built. A wrong upstream date can make a correct availability check look wrong.

PAL and SUP

One caps demand. The other protects supply.

I explain this in business terms first. Product Allocation limits how much a matching group may consume from constrained supply. Supply Protection keeps quantities protected from demand belonging to other groups. The characteristic and time-bucket design matters because badly aligned models can block each other.

Available Supply→ SUP: keep minimum protected→ PAL: limit group consumption→ Commercially Available Promise

The exact execution design depends on active methods and configuration. The mental model is about intent: protection and allocation are policy constraints, not inventory creation.

Alternative-Based Confirmation

Availability can change the sourcing decision.

ABC can evaluate substitute products, plants, or storage locations. That can improve the confirmation and still create new freight, route, tax, warehouse, or margin consequences. I therefore treat alternative selection as an Order-to-Cash design decision, not as an isolated ATP feature.
