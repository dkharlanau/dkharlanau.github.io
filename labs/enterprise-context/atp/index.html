Sales / availability promise

ATP answers a promise question.
It does not show a stock snapshot.

A sales order asks whether a quantity can be confirmed for a date. SAP answers that question from the requirement context, scheduling, the configured scope of check, relevant supply and demand, and any active advanced ATP policy. Physical stock is only one input.

record_voice_over

60-second answer: I start with one requirement: product, plant, quantity, requested date, and material-availability date. PAC checks that requirement against the supply and demand included by the scope of check and returns a confirmed quantity and date. aATP can then add business policy: allocation, supply protection, alternatives, or later backorder processing. If a confirmation looks wrong, I explain the basic PAC result first and add only the advanced rule that was actually active.

Stock exists, but no confirmation

Check the requirement date, scope of check, competing demand, and policy. Physical stock is not the same as available-to-promise quantity.

PAL or Supply Protection?

PAL limits what a matching segment may consume. Supply Protection keeps quantity available for selected demand groups. They solve different scarcity problems.

Why did confirmation change later?

Separate the original confirmation from BOP or another later availability change. A later redistribution can be expected behavior.

Sourcing or ATP?

Sourcing decides how supply is obtained or which source may fulfill. ATP decides whether and when the current requirement can be confirmed.

Lead policy decisions

Availability is not only a calculation. Scarcity needs a business policy.

PAC explains what can be promised from the current supply-and-demand context. Advanced ATP decisions become relevant when the business must decide who should receive scarce supply, whether existing promises may move, and whether alternatives are acceptable.

Service priority vs equal treatment

Allocation and protection rules can differentiate demand instead of letting every requirement compete in the same way.

Trade-off: protecting strategic demand can improve service for one segment while intentionally reducing flexibility for another.

Stable promise vs later optimization

Backorder processing can revisit confirmations after supply or priorities change.

Trade-off: reallocating supply can improve the overall business outcome, but it can also move an earlier customer promise. Governance and customer communication therefore matter.

Exact source vs fulfillment flexibility

Alternative-based fulfillment can search beyond the first product or location context when the business allows it.

Trade-off: more alternatives can increase service, but may add logistics cost, substitution rules, and explanation complexity.

Fast answer vs precise context

A confirmation is only as meaningful as the requirement, dates, plant, scope, and active policy behind it.

Lead rule: do not optimize ATP policy until the basic PAC result is understood and the business objective is explicit.

fact_check

Fact vs assumption: “100 pieces are physically in stock” is a stock fact. “This order should receive 100” is a promise assumption until the requirement date, scope, competing demand, and active scarcity policy are evaluated.

The core model

A promise is built from context before it is limited by policy.

When a confirmation looks wrong, it helps to separate the basic availability calculation from the commercial rules around scarce supply. Mixing those layers is why an ATP incident often turns into a search through unrelated configuration.

Requirement→ Plant + dates→ Scope of check→ Supply − demand→ aATP policy→ Confirmed qty / date

The sales document provides the requirement and its business context. Scheduling matters because the requested delivery date is not automatically the same date on which material must be available. PAC can perform backward and forward scheduling when it searches for a confirmation.

The availability scope is equally important. In current SAP S/4HANA documentation, the checking rule and checking group work together to determine which supply and demand elements are considered. The same physical inventory can therefore produce different confirmation results when the check context differs.

Product Availability Check

PAC is the foundation. aATP does not remove the need to understand it.

Product Availability Check calculates whether the requirement can be confirmed, and if so, in what quantity and on what date. Before looking at Product Allocation, Supply Protection or Backorder Processing, the basic PAC result should make sense.

Requirement

Confirm the product, plant, requested quantity, requested delivery date and the material availability date used by the check. A date problem can look like a supply problem.

Scope

Check which stock, receipts, issues and requirements are included. Current SAP S/4HANA configuration also allows scope behavior such as check horizon and delayed-supply handling to influence the result.

Result

Read the confirmed quantity together with its date. “Zero today, full next week” and “zero everywhere” are different outcomes and usually point to different causes.

A useful troubleshooting question is: what was the first input or rule that made this confirmation inevitable? That is more precise than asking why ATP is “wrong”.

Advanced ATP

The advanced functions solve different scarcity problems.

aATP is not one extra check. It is a set of capabilities around availability, prioritization, substitution and later redistribution. The names are similar enough to blur together, but their business questions are different.

PAC

Product Availability Check

Can this requirement be confirmed for a quantity and date from the supply-and-demand picture defined by the scope of check?

PAL

Product Allocation

How much may a matching business segment consume against planned allocation quantities over time?

SUP

Supply Protection

Which quantities should remain protected for selected demand groups instead of being consumed by other demand?

BOP

Backorder Processing

When the situation changes, which existing requirements should be rechecked and how should available supply be reassigned by business priority?

ABC

Alternative-Based Confirmation

If the original requirement cannot be confirmed as intended, can a configured alternative produce a better confirmation?

R4D

Release for Delivery

Which due sales orders should receive limited material before the process moves into delivery execution?

Allocation and protection

PAL and Supply Protection both constrain scarce supply, but in opposite ways.

Product Allocation starts from planned allocation quantities and characteristics. A matching requirement consumes against the allocation available to that combination. Supply Protection starts from protected quantities and demand groups; PAC reduces what other demand can consume when the protection applies.

Product Allocation

Think of PAL as a commercial allocation rule. It answers how much of constrained capacity or supply a matching segment may consume during a period. The design depends on allocation objects, characteristic combinations, planning data and assignment.

Supply Protection

Think of SUP as a reservation of opportunity rather than physical stock. It protects a defined quantity for matching demand groups. SAP explicitly warns that demand paths which bypass Supply Protection can undermine the model.

Used together

They can be complementary, but the characteristic design and time logic must be understood. A tight allocation and an overlapping protection rule can both restrict the same commercial promise even though physical supply exists.

Reconfirmation

The first confirmation is not necessarily the final allocation of supply.

Supply and demand move after an order is created. Backorder Processing (BOP) exists to recheck selected requirements and apply business priority again. This is why a later confirmation change is not automatically a defect.

A BOP variant defines the population and processing behavior, with segments used to select and prioritize requirements. Depending on the strategy, a run can improve, retain, reduce or remove confirmations. The important operational question is therefore not only “what changed?” but “which BOP rule selected this requirement and what priority did it receive?”

The Availability Change Log (ACL) supports a different concern. It records changes that affect the availability situation for supported objects and can be consumed by follow-on processes such as BOP. It is a way to focus reprocessing on changed availability situations; it is not a replacement for understanding the BOP variant itself.

Alternatives and delivery

A better confirmation can change the fulfillment design.

Alternative-Based Confirmation can evaluate configured substitutes when the original requirement is not the best available promise. The commercial result may improve, but the alternative still has to make sense for the downstream process.

An alternative product or location can change more than the ATP result. Delivery execution, transportation, warehouse processing, customer acceptance and commercial conditions may also change. For that reason, ABC belongs in an end-to-end order-fulfillment design, not in an isolated availability-check discussion.

Release for Delivery sits later in the process. SAP describes the app as a way to prioritize due sales orders for materials with limited availability before subsequent logistics processes such as picking or packing. It is a controlled operational decision over scarce supply, not another way to calculate stock.

One example

“We have 100 pieces in stock. Why did the order confirm only 20?”

The stock figure is not enough to answer. Several very different explanations can lead to the same visible result.

The 100 is not all available to this requirement

Other requirements may already consume part of the quantity, or the scope of check may include demand and exclude a receipt the user expected to count.

The date is different

The order may need the product on a material availability date that is earlier than the user assumes. A later receipt can exist and still be too late for the requested promise.

Policy limits the promise

PAL may restrict the matching segment, or Supply Protection may reserve part of the quantity for another protected group. The physical stock is real; the commercial availability is intentionally narrower.

The diagnosis becomes useful when it names the exact branch: date, scope, competing demand, allocation, protection, alternative logic, or later reconfirmation.

Diagnostic path

When a promise looks wrong, reconstruct it once from left to right.

This is the shortest diagnostic sequence that still respects the SAP model. It is intentionally smaller than a transaction checklist.

01context

Freeze one requirement

What exact product, plant, quantity and dates did SAP check?

Use one reproducible document item. Do not compare different orders until the first result can be explained.

02scope

Explain the basic PAC result

Which stocks, receipts, issues and requirements were included, and what did the check horizon permit?

If the base supply-and-demand picture is wrong, advanced policy is not the first problem.

03policy

Add only the active aATP controls

Did PAL, Supply Protection or ABC intentionally change what PAC could confirm?

Read the matching characteristics and time logic rather than assuming the policy from the customer name or sales organization.

04later change

Separate original confirmation from reconfirmation

Was the order later touched by BOP, an availability-changing event or a delivery-stage decision?

A correct original confirmation and a correct later redistribution can produce two different quantities at two different times.

conversion_path

Boundary with fulfillment: ATP proves a promise, not delivery completion. A confirmed quantity can still meet problems in shipping, warehouse execution, transportation, credit, compliance or another downstream control. Keep the availability decision and the execution decision separate.
