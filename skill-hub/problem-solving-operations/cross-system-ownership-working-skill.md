# Cross-System Ownership

Most enterprise failures are wonderfully well owned inside each system and mysteriously ownerless between them.

## Working method
1. Start from one business outcome, not an application list.
2. Map systems, teams, data objects, interfaces, decisions, and operational boundaries involved.
3. Separate ownership types: business outcome, data semantics, producer, consumer, platform, integration, security, operational monitoring, incident response, change approval, and end-to-end validation.
4. Identify handoff boundaries where everyone owns their component but nobody owns completion.
5. Define the decision rights at each boundary.
6. Define escalation and fallback ownership.
7. Check whether ownership still works during incidents, releases, cutovers, and supplier outages.
8. Record unresolved gaps as risks, not as vague "shared responsibility".

## Output
A **Cross-System Ownership Map** shows the outcome, components, ownership types, decision rights, escalation path, and uncovered boundaries.

## Lead signal
The Lead question is not "who supports system A?" It is "who is accountable when the business outcome crosses A, B, a queue, a file, and two teams?"
