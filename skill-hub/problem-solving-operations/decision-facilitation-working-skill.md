<p class="eyebrow">Working skill / Lead decision work</p>
<h1>Make the decision visible before debating the solution.</h1>
<p class="lead">Use this skill when several stakeholders are discussing options but the actual decision, criteria, evidence, and ownership are unclear. The goal is not to win the meeting. The goal is to leave with a decision or a precise reason why a decision cannot yet be made.</p>

<h2>Use when</h2>
<ul>
<li>A design meeting keeps returning to the same topic without closure.</li>
<li>Teams argue about products or implementation details before agreeing on the problem.</li>
<li>Several options exist and trade-offs are real.</li>
<li>A decision is blocked by missing evidence, unclear authority, or hidden constraints.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Required inputs</h2>
<ul>
<li>Decision topic and business outcome.</li>
<li>Known options or at least the current proposed direction.</li>
<li>Stakeholders and accountable decision owner.</li>
<li>Constraints, risks, dependencies, and available evidence.</li>
<li>Deadline or event that makes the decision necessary.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Workflow</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Name the decision.</strong> Write one sentence that can be answered with a choice or explicit deferment.</li>
<li><strong>Name the decision owner.</strong> Participation is not the same as authority.</li>
<li><strong>Define the outcome.</strong> State what business or technical result the decision must protect.</li>
<li><strong>Separate constraints from preferences.</strong> Legal, security, contract, budget, timing, architecture, and operational constraints need different treatment from preferences.</li>
<li><strong>Build real options.</strong> Include materially different system shapes, not several vendors implementing the same idea.</li>
<li><strong>Define criteria before scoring.</strong> Use business value, risk, delivery effort, reversibility, operational complexity, data impact, integration impact, security, and strategic fit as relevant.</li>
<li><strong>Attach evidence.</strong> For every important claim record evidence, assumption, or unknown.</li>
<li><strong>Expose trade-offs.</strong> State what improves and what becomes worse for each option.</li>
<li><strong>Identify decision blockers.</strong> If evidence is missing, name the smallest fact or experiment that would change the decision.</li>
<li><strong>Make or defer explicitly.</strong> Record chosen option, rationale, conditions, rejected options, and next review trigger.</li>
<li><strong>Assign actions.</strong> Convert conditions and open questions into owned follow-ups.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Decision rules</h2>
<ul>
<li>If the decision cannot be written in one sentence, the discussion scope is still too broad.</li>
<li>If no accountable decision owner exists, escalate governance before pretending consensus equals a decision.</li>
<li>Do not score options before criteria are agreed.</li>
<li>Different vendors are not different options when the system shape and trade-offs are the same.</li>
<li>If one unknown can change the preferred option, test that unknown before spending hours polishing a decision matrix.</li>
<li>A deferred decision must include the missing evidence, owner, and trigger for reopening it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Output</h2>
<p>Produce a <strong>Decision Facilitation Record</strong> with decision statement, owner, outcome, constraints, options, criteria, evidence, trade-offs, blockers, decision, rationale, conditions, rejected options, and follow-up actions.</p>

<h2>Quality gates</h2>
<ul>
<li>The decision is stated in one clear sentence.</li>
<li>An accountable owner is named.</li>
<li>Constraints and preferences are separated.</li>
<li>Options are materially different.</li>
<li>Evidence, assumptions, and unknowns are visible.</li>
<li>Trade-offs are explicit.</li>
<li>Decision or deferment has rationale and next actions.</li>
</ul>
