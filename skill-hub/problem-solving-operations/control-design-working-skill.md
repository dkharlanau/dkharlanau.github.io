# Control Design

A control is not the same thing as another approval button. Sometimes the best control is a deterministic rule, a reconciliation, a limit, or a visible exception.

## Working method
1. State the risk event and business consequence.
2. Identify the cause or condition the control can influence.
3. Decide whether prevention, detection, correction, or compensation is the right control shape.
4. Place the control at the earliest reliable boundary without duplicating it everywhere.
5. Define evidence, owner, frequency, threshold, and response.
6. Test bypass paths and privileged operations.
7. Define false-positive cost and operational workload.
8. Define how control effectiveness will be measured.
9. Add periodic review so obsolete controls do not become permanent process friction.

## Output
A **Control Design Record** contains risk, control objective, control type, trigger, enforcement point, owner, evidence, response, bypass conditions, testing, and effectiveness metric.

## Lead signal
Strong control design reduces risk without quietly destroying throughput, usability, or ownership.
