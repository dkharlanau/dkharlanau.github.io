# Operating Model Design

A solution is not finished when it reaches production. That is merely the moment it starts asking for attention indefinitely.

## Working method
1. Define the business service or outcome the operating model supports.
2. Map ownership across business, product, platform, data, integration, security, and operations.
3. Define service boundaries, support hours, criticality, and escalation.
4. Define monitoring, alert response, incident, problem, and knowledge processes.
5. Define change ownership, release gates, emergency changes, and dependency coordination.
6. Define data-quality and reconciliation responsibilities.
7. Define capacity, resilience, recovery, and continuity responsibilities.
8. Define supplier and platform responsibilities where relevant.
9. Define metrics for service health, business outcome, operational load, and improvement.
10. Define governance cadence and how recurring operational evidence becomes backlog or architecture work.

## Output
An **Operating Model Record** contains service scope, ownership, support model, monitoring, incident/change/data responsibilities, governance cadence, metrics, and improvement loop.

## Lead signal
The strongest design connects architecture choices to the people and controls that will operate them after the project team has gone elsewhere to create fresh complexity.
