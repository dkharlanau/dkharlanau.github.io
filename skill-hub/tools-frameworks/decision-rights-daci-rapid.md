<p class="eyebrow">Decision-rights tool</p><h1>DACI and RAPID</h1>
<p class="lead">Use a decision-rights model when the problem is not “who does the work?” but “who drives, contributes to, approves, decides, and executes the decision?”</p>

<section><h2>RACI is not a decision model</h2>
<p>RACI is strong for work ownership. It becomes weak when several senior stakeholders must shape one decision. In that case, use a model that explicitly separates the decision maker from contributors and execution.</p>
</section>

<section><h2>DACI</h2>
<table class="study-table"><thead><tr><th>Role</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Driver</td><td>Runs the decision process, gathers evidence, and keeps the decision moving.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Approver</td><td>Makes the final call.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Contributors</td><td>Provide expertise and recommendations.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Informed</td><td>Need the result after the decision.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>DACI works well for a project or product decision with a manageable group of stakeholders.</p>
</section>

<section><h2>RAPID</h2>
<table class="study-table"><thead><tr><th>Role</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Recommend</td><td>Frames options and proposes a course of action.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Agree</td><td>Must formally agree or has a defined veto/sign-off role.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Perform</td><td>Executes the decision.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Input</td><td>Provides facts or expertise before the decision.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Decide</td><td>Makes the final decision.</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>RAPID is useful when the decision spans organizational boundaries and the difference between recommendation, agreement, decision, and execution matters.</p>
</section>

<section><h2>How to choose</h2><ul>
<li><strong>Use DACI</strong> for a project-level decision that needs one driver and one approver.</li>
<li><strong>Use RAPID</strong> when formal agreement and execution roles must be separated from the final decision.</li>
<li><strong>Use RACI</strong> when the problem is recurring task or deliverable ownership rather than the decision itself.</li>
</ul></section>

<section><h2>SAP example</h2>
<p>Decision: “Should customer credit checking move to SAP Credit Management in the target architecture?” The solution architect may drive or recommend the analysis, Finance and Sales provide input, Security may have an agreement requirement, the program design authority makes the decision, and the delivery teams perform the resulting work.</p>
</section>
