<p class="eyebrow">Collaborative process discovery</p><h1>Domain Storytelling</h1>
<p class="lead">Use Domain Storytelling when business experts and IT need one understandable picture of who performs an activity, what information or work object they use, and what happens next.</p>

<section><h2>What makes it different</h2><p>The domain expert tells a concrete story. The modeler draws it while the expert watches and corrects it. This keeps the language close to real work and makes misunderstanding visible immediately.</p></section>

<section><h2>Working method</h2>
<ol>
<li>Choose one concrete scenario, not the whole enterprise.</li>
<li>Ask a domain expert to tell the story step by step.</li>
<li>Draw actors first.</li>
<li>Add work objects such as order, quote, delivery, approval, file, or message.</li>
<li>Add numbered activities in the order they happen.</li>
<li>Use the words the domain expert uses; normalize language only after agreement.</li>
<li>Ask what changes when the case is exceptional.</li>
<li>Capture unresolved questions outside the main story.</li>
</ol></section>

<section><h2>Use it when</h2><ul>
<li>A workshop has many slides but little shared understanding.</li>
<li>Business users do not read formal process notation comfortably.</li>
<li>You need to discover tacit workarounds and manual hand-offs.</li>
<li>You want a bridge from process discovery to requirements or domain design.</li>
</ul></section>

<section><h2>SAP example</h2><p>A sales coordinator describes how a customer sends a purchase order, how they check partner and material data, what happens when pricing is missing, who approves a deviation, and when the order becomes ready for delivery. The story can expose steps that are invisible in SAP because they occur in email or spreadsheets.</p></section>

<section><h2>Decision rules</h2><ul>
<li>If the audience needs formal execution semantics, convert the validated story into BPMN.</li>
<li>If the domain is driven by many events and policies rather than actor activities, EventStorming may expose more structure.</li>
<li>If the story becomes too large to tell naturally, split it into scenarios.</li>
</ul></section>
